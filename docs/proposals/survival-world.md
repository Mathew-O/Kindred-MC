# Co-op Survival: the whole game, and a world worth walking through

Status: proposal, 2026-11-10. IDs E-58 to E-63 and M-01g are reserved in the [tracker](../feature-spec.md). The
match modes have their own spec ([match-modes.md](match-modes.md)). Everything here keeps the contract: earned,
nothing hidden, every block and item through the ledger; boss encounters only when qualified and allowed
(PROG-03).

## Where Co-op Survival stands

The player can start from nothing and reach a lit portal; build cabins, houses, manors, keeps and bunkers; farm,
ranch, fish, trade and run a trading hall; enchant, brew and smith netherite; sleep, eat, flee and clutch; keep a
wolf. Done or in progress in the tracker: M-01a to M-01f, E-01 to E-08, E-13 to E-16, E-21 to E-25, E-27, E-29 to
E-31, E-36 to E-38, E-42 to E-46. What the tracker does not mention at all, by a word count of its text: banners,
shields with banners, armour trims, conduits, beacons, elytra, shulkers, end cities, ocean monuments beyond a word,
woodland mansions, raids as a village event, trial chambers, archaeology, amethyst, bees and honey, allays, axolotls,
frogs, camels, armour stands, item frames, map art, jukeboxes and discs, campfires, composters, bells, grindstones,
looms, cartography and fletching tables, stonecutters, iron farms, mob farms, roads, bridges as *things built*
rather than route steps, Nether highways. That is roughly half of the game a survival player plays past iron.

## The idea

Two programmes. **The systems** (E-58 to E-62): the player can do each thing the game has, the way a player does it,
each a small slice with a fixture. **The world** (E-63): what the systems add up to, a settlement that grows into
something people want to visit, planned as players plan a world rather than as a pile of builds. The gate (M-01g) is
a month of co-op survival on a server with people in it.

The ordering principle: **the player builds what it would use.** A beacon because it mines; a conduit because it has
an ocean to work in; roads because it walks the same way every day; a bell because the village has one; banners
because it has a faction, a base and a shield. Nothing is built as a showpiece until it is wanted, which is also what
keeps the world coherent.

## E-63 The world: a town that grows

What the systems add up to. **Not a bigger base: a settlement.**

- **A town plan** (`TownPlan` in core, pure): a square at the spawn village or the first base; districts that come
  with the systems (houses, farms, the hall, the storage, the stables, the apiary, the docks if there is water, the
  beacon on a hill); roads between them on the atlas's paths; room for each player's own house in its own style
  (E-29a) on a plot the plan assigns; a wall when the mode wants one. The plan is generated from the terrain and
  grown as the town grows, re-planned from the world like a build (E-08a), never a fixed template.
- **A shared stock and a shared plan**: the team coordinator (E-11, E-40a) hands out the town's jobs (who paves,
  who farms, who builds the hall), each player's house its own.
- **Days and seasons of work**: mornings at the farms, afternoons building, evenings at the base with music and a
  campfire, nights in; a rhythm people see (the lessons and the attention budget already give the hours shape).
- **With people**: a person's build is theirs; the town grows around it, roads reach it, a road to a person's house
  is a gift; the players ask before building next to someone.
- **Showing it**: the watch page's map of the town with what is built and planned; a tour ("Echo, show me around")
  as an outing (E-27) that walks a person through the town and talks about it.

Acceptance: the plan in JUnit (districts, plots, roads from a staged terrain and atlas); a GameTest growing a town
through three stages on a flat world; the month run (M-01g) as the real test.

## M-01g The co-op month

Thirty days on a server with people, Co-op Survival mode, the systems above as far as they have landed, under the
evaluation engine's recording and the watch page: what was built, where, by whom; what was used; deaths, stuck
time, tick time; what people said. Passing: the town grown through its stages with no rule broken, the server at 20
ticks a second, and the players still playing on day 30; a tour given to a person. Filed under
`docs/evidence/eval/coop-month/`.

## Order

E-62a, E-62e, E-62g and E-62h first (roads, farms, storage, light: they change how the base looks every day and how
the engine's runs go), then E-59a/b/g (banners, shields, decoration: cheap and visible), E-61a/b (horses, pets),
E-60a/b (the monument and the sea), E-58a/b (Wither and beacon, under their switch), E-62c/d/f (rails, highways,
farms), E-59c..f, E-60c..e, E-61c..e, E-58c..e, then E-63 as the systems land, and M-01g.

Most slices are one half to one session each; the Wither and the End cities two each; the town plan two. Roughly
twenty sessions for the lot, none needing a model.
