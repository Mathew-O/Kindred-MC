# Kindred MC AI-player feature tracker
Version 2.0 · 15 October 2026

This is the only completion checklist. [The player contract](../minecraft_player_spec.md) defines the product; [architecture](player-architecture.md) defines boundaries. The companion roadmap has been replaced, not preserved as an alternate plan.

## Product and implementation order
Build autonomous AI players with personal needs/goals and real teamwork. Three must earn an empty-inventory survival playthrough through the Dragon. Follow/help are tasks; creator access is administration. Human presence and a mode toggle are not the new autonomy model.

First qualify the native player body (F-25b), then integrate local survival/recipe progression (F-25c with F-04/05/10..17 and E-04/07/12). Bring shared assignments E-11 into the core path, then exploration/Nether/equipment/End (E-14..17/39/40). Optional inference F-19 and social refinements must not displace these physical dependencies. No Jev or mandatory hosted orchestration.

Compatibility names, save fields and old command aliases are not instructions to rebuild the old concept. Historical checked foundations establish only their dated bounded evidence; none proves current player-body mechanics or a full release.

## Game modes (direction recorded 2026-10-20)
The owner set four core modes. Each is a parent feature below (M-01..M-04) on a shared mode and match framework (M-00) and schematic library (M-05). The player contract (MODE-01..05) and AGENTS.md describe them; the earned survival playthrough (E-40) stays the production objective and the foundation the survival modes are built on.
- **Co-op Survival (M-01):** progress at the human's natural pace; never seek out a boss fight, but be ready when the human is (revised 2026-10-23: boss fights are an optional progression, below); bases, farms, enchanting, and builds planned from schematics with every block earned.
- **Manhunt (M-02):** runner or hunter, between humans and AI players, including two or more AIs; the hunter gets iron-level gear and then hunts, going further only when it is opportune; the runner speedruns while evading.
- **Speedrun (M-03):** random seed; any% glitchless with the techniques the speedrunning community accepts.
- **Builder (M-04):** a creative build 1v1 on a random theme in a generated arena, with the presentation of a minigame server.

Direction recorded 2026-10-23: the owner introduces no further specs. Work sharpens what exists and moves toward the four core modes, the north star, plus one optional mode.
- **Co-op Survival (M-01)** is a regular playthrough with natural progression: a house or base, farms, a ranch with animal pens, horses tamed and ridden, XP farms, enchanting and the like. Boss fights are an optional progression (MODE-02).
- **Notes recorded later on 2026-10-23 (owner):**
  - **Needs are a queue.** Items a build or craft needs do not halt everything for one fetch after another. The player finishes what it can with what it holds and fills in what it has, then fetches the shortfall together in one trip planned for its time. An item urgent to finishing the task, or very easy to get (stored in a chest it knows), it fetches at once. Example: an enchanting room with too few bookshelves is built first, the table encased as far as the shelves go, then it goes for more.
  - **Manhunt (M-02).** Everyone starts from scratch. The runners' goal is to beat the game quickly but reliably, not at speedrun pace, while evading the hunters. The hunters' goal is to kill the runners while gearing up as the game goes on.
  - **Builds.** Huts serve for tests. The north star is detailed houses, bunkers and builds: several floors and, above all, functional.
  - **Staged progression (M-01).** A base starts small, to get the world started and give the player a place to store resources, and keeps improving: three stages, from a small base to bigger and better. The same staging holds for farms and other functional builds. Mob-spawner farms are rare, so each is a one-off functional build for grinding and resources.
- **Factions (M-06, optional)** is the same natural survival progression, but each AI player plays as an independent, unfriendly to the other players and AI players in it. It calls for more strategy: hidden bunkers, concealed resource farms, defences and scouting. D7 holds: only players who joined it are fair game.
- **Notes recorded later still on 2026-10-23 (owner):**
  - **Factions bases and bunkers in five stages (M-06a, M-06b).**
    - *The base.* A walled-in mini civilization that the player expands as its resource needs grow. Its farms, cattle, house and the rest are all enclosed in a walled, restricted, castle-like area. One of the later stages adds a moat and bridge.
    - *The bunker.* It follows the same five-stage, expanding logic: multi-level and multi-room, an underground mini civilization that grows as the game goes on.
    - *For both:* walls are always lined with something, and entrances are discreet.
  - **Redstone (M-01d).** Farms and other builds use working redstone mechanics.
  - **Repair (E-31a).** A player keeps what it built whole. A broken chest is replaced; what is blown up or destroyed is fixed. There is never a graveyard build left behind.
  - **One weighted list for everything it does (F-25c9).**
    - *Life first.* When hunger is low it gets food, enough to last. At night it sleeps.
    - *Builds near the bottom.* Ongoing builds come near the bottom of the list.
    - *A bed at home.* Home-like builds have beds, so the player's spawn is set there.
  - **Staying alive while evading (M-02c).**
    - *Keeping itself going.* While evading another player or a threat, a player queues what keeps it alive and gets it to safety as its hunger runs down. It eats at a safe distance to recover health, or changes course.
    - *Not leading them home.* In a world where others are unfriendly, it goes somewhere else rather than back to its base, which would give the base away.
  - **Builds grown from a seed block (E-29a).** The owner's idea, still being worked on: a player given a seed block (cherry wood, say) builds whatever it builds round that block. Builds come in different shapes and sizes, never the same static build repeated across worlds.

Recommended order: finish the survival core (F-25c4, then E-07 combat and caves, E-15 portal travel); then build M-00; M-04 can proceed in parallel because creative play does not depend on survival. After that come M-03, M-02 (built on the M-03 runner plus PvP), and the remainder of M-01 (schematic builds, enchanting, farms).

Decisions confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-20 (next focus: the survival core):
- **D6:** A mode rule may hand out an item only when it is announced and identical for human and AI participants, such as Manhunt's tracking compass for every hunter. Survival modes have no other exception to earned progress.
- **D7:** An AI player attacks a human only inside a match that human joined; outside matches, player attacks stay refused.
- **D8:** Kindred ships a small library of bundled schematics whose licenses allow it, and users may supply their own: files they provide, or single links they give, used within each site's terms and the creator's license, with attribution kept. No bulk scraping.

## Work protocol
Claim an open ID before implementation. Preserve IDs; add explicit children. Put a LIVE EDIT marker first while editing contracts; remove it when finished. Check all acceptance criteria before marking done, with date/evidence and affected user docs in the same change. Reopen invalid evidence; keep blocked work open. Native outcomes require native evidence; live scenarios require real clients/runs. Use [the evidence template](evidence/README.md). Update the manifest only with supported capabilities/qualified gates.

### F-10e — Routes that build and dig

### F-10h — A staircase built up as it walks
Depends on F-10e and F-10d. ## Game-mode cards
### M-00 — Game-mode and match framework
Depends on F-25b/F-25c. Owner/operator selects a mode per world or match from the K panel or `/aiplayer mode`; the existing playstyle profile maps onto it rather than running beside it. A mode declares its rules: survival or creative, edit policy, PvP among opted-in participants (D7), announced mode items (D6), respawn rules and win conditions. Matches have a lobby, roles, countdown, timer, end and results. Presentation: titles, boss bar, action bar, scoreboard, sounds and particles, visible without the client mod, with richer screens when it is installed. Acceptance: native fixtures for every lifecycle transition and rule; live runs with ordinary clients; stopping a match revokes all match-granted rules synchronously.

### M-01 — Co-op Survival mode
Depends on M-00, F-25c4, E-07, E-13, E-16, E-39, M-05. A regular playthrough with natural progression (owner, 2026-10-23): a house or base, crop farms, a ranch with animal pens and breeding, horses tamed, saddled and ridden, XP farms and enchanting, all from earned materials. Progress tracks the human: the AI player does not run ahead of the group's milestones. Boss fights are an optional progression, set per world: off, it prepares gear and supplies for a boss but starts one only when a human does, and then fights alongside; on, it works toward the bosses as part of the playthrough. It builds chosen schematics by computing the bill of materials, gathering or crafting every block, and placing them in a buildable order. Needs are a queue (owner, 2026-10-23): it builds what it can from what it holds, fills in what it has, and fetches the shortfall together in one planned trip; an item urgent to finishing, or easy to get from a known chest, it fetches at once. Bases improve in three stages (owner, 2026-10-23): a small base to start the world and store resources, then bigger and better, ending in detailed, multi-floor, functional builds (rooms with a purpose, storage, workstations, an enchanting room). Farms and other functional builds are staged the same way; a mob-spawner farm is a one-off functional build where a spawner is found. Acceptance: live sessions in which a human sets the pace, nothing is granted, and a schematic build completes from earned blocks.

### M-02 — Manhunt mode
Depends on M-00, M-03 and native PvP combat. Roles: runner or hunter, with humans and AI players in any mix, including AI against AI. Hunters track the runner with the announced compass rule (D6), the same for humans and AIs, pointing at the runner's last portal across dimensions. Everyone starts from scratch (owner, 2026-10-23). The hunter doctrine is iron gear and a shield, then pursuit, gearing up further as the game goes on and going further only when it is opportune. The runner's goal is to beat the game quickly but reliably, not at speedrun pace, while evading the hunters: keep distance, block pursuit, and fight only when cornered. Win conditions are dragon dead or runner dead; hunters respawn. Acceptance: matches of AI against human and AI against AI that run to a result with no shortcuts.

### M-03 — Speedrun mode
Depends on F-25c4, E-15, Nether and End skills, E-39. A random seed, with no seed knowledge or locator (the seed locator was removed). Any% glitchless along community-accepted routes: lava-pool or ruined-portal entry, bastion bartering for pearls, fortress blaze rods, eye-of-ender triangulation, and bed or one-cycle dragon kills. An in-game timer and splits. Acceptance: completed runs on several random seeds with recorded times and no rule-breaking actions.

### M-04 — Builder mode
Depends on M-00, E-29 and M-05; optionally F-19 for theme interpretation. A generated arena with a lobby, protected plots, and spectator and voting paths. Each round has a random theme announced with a title, a timed build in creative mode (blocks are free in this mode only), voting by human and AI players, then results with a podium and fireworks. AI builders turn the theme into a structured build plan, using the local design grammar, reference schematics from M-05, and optionally the model as planner through companion-model (data plans only, no generated code), then place it block by block within the time limit. Acceptance: complete 1v1 rounds against a human and between AIs, with ordinary-client presentation.

### M-06 — Factions mode (optional)
Depends on M-00, M-01's progression and E-41's combat. The owner's optional mode (2026-10-23): the natural survival progression of M-01, but each AI player plays as an independent faction, unfriendly to every other player and AI player in the match. It plays for strategy as well as progress: a hidden bunker as a base, concealed resource farms, defences, scouting others' bases, and choosing when to fight. A human is fair game only after joining the match (D7); a player outside it is never attacked. Entering another participant's base or taking from its chests follows the mode's announced rules and the server's protection, the same for humans and AIs; nothing is free. Acceptance: live sessions in which AI players and a joined human each build up in secret and contest one another, with no shortcuts and no harm to anyone who did not join.

### M-05 — Schematic library
Depends on E-08. Import Sponge `.schem`, Litematica `.litematic` and vanilla structure `.nbt` files, bundled with the mod or supplied by users (D8). Store the source link, creator and license with each import. Compute a bill of materials and a buildable placement order (supports first, gravity blocks last), and report blocks that survival cannot obtain. Acceptance: pure parser and ordering tests; a native fixture that builds a small imported structure from owned blocks.
