## Scope and implementation

F-35d step 3 showed the simulator's packs collapse once it walks with the real planner: almost every refusal was a
target above or below the body, which the planner, digging only at its own level, cannot reach. In the game
`PortalRunCampaign` gets up and down through rock with its own skills, and gives a target up after a failed walk. The
lead's decision: no change to `RoutePlanner` and no new moves; port the campaign's own decisions into core, unchanged,
and have the adapter and the simulator both call them.

Phase 1 mapped the methods first (helpers, fields, world reads, tasks): about 690 lines to move, no need to move the
rest of the campaign, every read answerable behind a per-cell fact or a body callback, and `PlayerRuntime` touched
only through one static boolean (`inDeepWater`), which the adapter answers. Go.

- `companion-core` `dev.kindred.core.route` (new):
  - `DigTerrain extends Terrain`: four more per-cell facts, int coordinates, no Minecraft types or ids:
    - `empty`: no collision box at all; the campaign's `passable` is `empty` and not lava;
    - `falls`: vanilla's `FallingBlock`, sand, gravel and the like;
    - `fallsThrough`: `FallingBlock.canFallThrough`;
    - `falling(x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1)`: a falling block entity in the integer box, the same `Box` the campaign built.
  - `Digger`: the decisions, ported line for line from the campaign:
    - `climb`, with `fromBeside`, `intoTheHoleOverhead` and `pillarBesideTheWater`;
    - `digDown`, both forms;
    - `descendOrTunnel`;
    - `tunnel`, `tunnelAlong` and `tunnelStraight`;
    - the branch mine's turn at a leg's end (`branchAdvanced`);
    - the helpers: `liquidNear`, `onlyLavaBelow`, `comingDown`, `diggableFaceAhead`, `landingWithin`, `holeOver`,
      `pocket`, `capped`, `headRoomRefused` and `settling`.
    - The state those skills keep between calls: the heading, the tunnel's floor, turns and bridging, the dug and
      tunnelled cells, the under-water heading, the step aside, the settle wait, the pillar misses and the branch run.
    - Decision only: each call returns one step, made by the body's own step makers (`go`, `clear`, `openOverLava`,
      `settle`, `pillar`, `swap`, `fill`). The facts about the body come through `Digger.Body`: reach, tools, edit
      policy, inventory, island edge and danger zones.
  - `SetAside`: the campaign's ledger, from `avoid`, `unreachable` and `blocked`. The exact target for 2,400 ticks,
    doubled for each failure there up to the sixth. With a column, the cells round it too, a block each way and six up
    and down; for a pool, six each way and two up and down. They stay set aside until the back-off ends and the player
    has moved eight away. Also `until`, `put` and `clear` for the campaign's other set-asides.
- `companion-server-adapter`:
  - `PortalRunCampaign` keeps every method its callers use (`climb`, `digDown`, `tunnel`, `tunnelAlong`,
    `tunnelStraight`, `descendOrTunnel`, `unreachable`, `blocked`), each a call into core through an inner `Dig`.
  - `Dig` answers each fact with the campaign's own test: `breakableNow`, `handClearable`, `snareClearable`,
    `islandEdge`, `dangerBelow`, `wayBlockSlot`, and the reveal-lava test inline. It builds each step as the exact
    task the campaign built: the same skill, args, label and order.
  - Its other users of the moved state (38 uses of the heading, the explore's and `digToward`'s tunnel settings, 18
    set-asides, `wayUpBy`'s under-water place) go through accessors.
  - `WorldDig` is the live world as a `DigTerrain`. Its walk facts are a fresh `NativeNavigator`'s, as the climb asked a
    navigator of its own; `NativeNavigator.terrain()` is new.
  - The campaign is 609 lines out and 138 in (its wrappers, accessors and `Dig`); core has 911 new lines (`Digger`
    809, `SetAside` 75, `DigTerrain` 27).
- `companion-sim`:
  - `SimWorld` implements `DigTerrain`, as vanilla's blocks would answer: gravel and sand fall; air, water and lava
    are fallen through; nothing is ever in flight.
  - `SimRun` asks the same core calls where the campaign does:
    - An ore or lava not in view is dug toward with `descendOrTunnel` at the campaign's depths: iron 56 and 16,
      diamonds -52 and -58, lava -54 and -57. For coal it climbs from well below 48, then tunnels.
    - Below ground, with logs, water or animals wanted at the surface, it climbs (`climb`), as the campaign's
      `wayUp` mostly does. It also climbs toward water seen more than four up, as `fillWater` does.
    - Stone not in view is dug for with `digDown`, six down.
    - A target whose walk found no way is given up with its column (a pool's part for water and lava) by `SetAside`,
      and skipped in what it sees.
    - Each step is carried out in its world: a walk on the core navigator, a break by the pickaxe held, a pillar or a
      placed block from its dirt or cobblestone, a second's wait.
    - Its outcome teaches the state as the campaign's `taskFinished` does: cells dug, tunnelled cells, the branch
      turn, and a failed step's cell set aside, with a failed dig, tunnel or climb turned a quarter.
  - The straight tunnel stand-in stays only for gravel. The step-3 copy of `liquidNear` is now the core's.
  - The packs' logs were identical on two runs.
  - `companion-sim/README.md` (new) lists its known gaps for movement fidelity.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- Not ported, and so not in the simulator:
  - `wayUp` (the trail back, `pillarOut`, opening its own seal) and `digToward` (the cast's dig toward lava). They
    call the ported methods and stay in the adapter.
  - The campaign's `lead`, `cave` and `lookAround`. Its night `Dig in for the night` (`digDown` by hand) and the
    danger zones (`dangerBelow`, always false in the simulator).
- The three step forms the dump did not reach ("dig out what came down" and "open the ceiling beside it" in
  `fromBeside`, and the climb's "out from under the water, a level lower") are ported unchanged, but only a reading of
  the code covers them. The simulator's hemmed-in scene takes the last one many times.
- The adapter keeps its own `liquidNear`, `onlyLavaBelow` and `comingDown` for its other callers. They read the world
  directly, as before. The core's copies are used by the ported methods and by the simulator.
