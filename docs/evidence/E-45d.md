## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): "it should build villager stations near its own area for ones with good trades".

**The hall** (`TradingHall`, core, pure; `tradinghall` among the generators). A row of cells along a corridor, as players build them. Each cell is one block wide and three deep: its bed along it, head to the back wall, and the villager's floor before the bed's foot. Walled from the next, floored and roofed, it is open to the corridor only across its front, where the villager's job block goes at its feet once the villager is in, which shuts it in: a lintel over the job block leaves no room to stand on it, and the one block open between the two is where the player trades across. The corridor is walled three high, lit within by a torch at each cell and shut by a door in the middle of its front wall, against zombies, and open to the sky. One to twelve cells; its cells' places (where the villager stands, its job block, the opening, where the player trades from) are given for what brings villagers to it (E-45e).

**How the design came to this, from the fixture.** A roof over the corridor too, seven deep over a closed hall, left its middle row out of any reach to place it from: the build stopped short of it. Cells two wide with the bed across the back wall left the bed's foot with nowhere to stand facing along it (a bed is placed facing the way the player looks): "No way to reach minecraft:white_bed". A villager set on a bed's top in a cell never took up the lectern before it. With the bed along the cell, the player places it from the cell's floor or the corridor, looking in, and the villager stands on the floor, its lectern at its feet.

**Of its own accord, by its base** (`PlayerRuntime.baseDecision`, with the operator's leave for bases and AI world edits on). A player whose first base stage stands and that keeps a villager worth a cell of its own (a librarian it rerolled and kept, or a villager it has looked at whose offers are worth keeping to it as it stands, by E-45c's whom to keep) and has no hall sites one of three cells: on the nearest flat open ground to its last base (`BaseSite`, as a stage is sited), clear of its bases, in its base's wood, before its next stage. It says why: "A villager worth keeping, and a cell to keep it in: a trading hall of 3 cells." The hall is recorded with its bases (a new optional `hall`: where, how many cells, its wood, whether it stands), taken up again if begun, and finished after its last try as far as it goes; a later stage's site keeps clear of it.

**Built** as the player's other builds are: through the build runner, its blocks each in their exact state, the cells and the corridor kept clear (E-08b), its shortfall fetched through the needs queue (E-08c).

Changed modules/files: `TradingHall` (new; its width, depth and height for siting), `TradingHallTest` (new), `Generators` (`tradinghall`); `PlayerRuntime` (the hall in `baseDecision`, `startHall`, `hallBuilt`, `keepsAVillager`), `PlayerMemoryStore` (`Hall`, `Bases.hall`); `NativeTradingHallGameTests` (new, two tests).
Dependencies satisfied: E-08b's build runner; E-45c's whom to keep.
Schema/configuration changes and migration coverage: the player's bases file gains an optional `hall` object. A file written before has none and reads as no hall; Gson ignores a field it does not know, so an older build reads a newer file as before. A hall that cannot be built again as recorded (no dimension, an unknown wood, cells out of range) is left out on load, as a base is.
