## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: a person asks an AI player in chat, by name, and it does what the matching command does when that
person types it, then says how it went: done in a few words, or the command's refusal as its own. Goals, ways of
playing, working together or alone, builds, ranches and fields where the speaker stands, skins, races and duels, joining
and leaving a match, fewer or more lines, and (with no model) E-20b's come, wait, stop and toss. A race or duel is
proposed only once every AI player in it says yes by its own rule, and each person in it joins only by their own "I'm
in". A race or duel with nobody named is asked back once. The operator's switches and anything with coordinates stay
commands, and the player says which.

Changed modules/files:

- `companion-core`:
  - `social.Intent` (new): the kinds of ask, who may make each (a controller; anyone, for a proposal; the person, for
    themselves), and `of`, the one check every ask passes: a known kind, an argument of the kind's own, names well
    formed and at most eight.
  - `social.IntentGrammar` (new): the plain forms, read without a model; the ask back and its answer; the operator's
    switches and asks with coordinates recognised.
  - `match.MatchChoice` (new): an AI player's own yes or no to a race or duel.
  - `social.IntentGrammarTest` (new, 7).
- `companion-model`: `Strategist`: a model's toss is checked through `social.Intent`, as an ask read in chat is.
- `companion-server-adapter`:
  - `ChatIntents` (new): each ask run through its command's method with the speaker's own command source and output
    captured; the done words and the refusal made its own; the ask back; a race or duel's AI players asked by
    `MatchChoice` first; "I'm in" joining the newest match the person is invited to, and starting it once everyone is in.
  - `CompanionSocials`: the hearing hook answers an ask before the voice; with no model at all, E-20b's four in their
    plain forms are read here (`fallback`); `Voice.available`.
  - `PlayerVoice`: `available`.
  - `PlayerRuntime`: hook lines (the intents in the hearing; small read-only accessors for ChatIntents; the native
    `askInChat` path into E-20b's queue; a fixture accessor) and `proposeDuel` and `proposeSpeedrun` overloads taking the
    AI players that said yes for themselves, whose yes stands in for their controller's for that match alone.
- gametest: `NativeChatAskGameTests` (8), a new entrypoint.
- Docs: the [README](../../README.md) cheat sheet (most of it is now said in chat), the
  [operator guide](../operator-guide.md) (Play by asking), the [docs glossary](../README.md) ("Ask").
- No new dependency; the manifest's versions are unchanged.

Schema/configuration changes: none.

## Design changes and what was not built

- Asks are read by the local grammar with or without a model; the model's vocabulary is unchanged (E-20b's four), and
  its toss is now checked through `social.Intent`. An ask phrased unusually may not be understood; the model is not
  asked to carry the new kinds. To widen the model's asks is a follow-up (its schema and instructions are cached).
- Not built: manhunts and build battles by chat (they stay `/aiplayer match manhunt` and `match build`); `GO HOME` and
  `SLEEP` (a native player has no command for either); the operator's switches by chat, which the design did not
  require; asks from one AI player to another as records (E-51 and E-53); a player proposing a match itself when idle
  (E-53's attention budget first); "you two, work together" addressed to more than one player.
- `QUIET` and `TALK` are the person's own `prefs unasked fewer|more` (E-19a), not a player-wide chatter setting (E-51's
  `KINDRED_CHATTER` is the operator's).
- A race asked by someone who does not control the AI player is proposed on the AI's own yes; the registry's join rule
  is unchanged (a person still joins only themselves).
