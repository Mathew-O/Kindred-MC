## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: a builder placed each next block from that block's own best spot, the nearest block first, so it
walked a step to each block of a row it could have placed from one spot. Now, within a layer:

1. **From where it stands first, inside the build or out.** A block it can place from its feet goes first, the farthest
   of them in reach first. Inside the build this rests on E-08a1's `sealedIn`, which takes a way out up only as a step
   onto footing, never over a wall; a block whose placing would shut it in is refused there as before.
2. **Then the spot that serves the most of the layer.** With nothing placeable from its feet, it walks to the spot from
   which it can place the most of the layer now, ties to today's cost (the walk, 3 more inside the build, a tenth of the
   reach), and places the farthest it serves from there.
3. **Before calling a block out of reach,** where a plan would otherwise be blocked, the rest of the layer beyond the
   nearest 48 (`CANDIDATES`) is tried, each from its own best spot. Working from where it stands, a builder can finish a
   course in the middle of a wide room. The next course's nearest blocks are then the middle of a ceiling, with nothing
   yet to set them against, and the blocks over the walls lie past the first 48. The spruce manor's ceiling in
   `GeneratorsTest` was called out of reach so. Only a plan that main would also have blocked looks this far.

What does not change: the layer order and support order, the scaffold and prop fallbacks, every refusal (sealed in, the
partner cell, claims and policy), and what is placed where. Only which block is next, and the spot it walks to, change.

**A departure for the owner.** The proposal says nearest first. The planner places the farthest in reach first: from one
spot, a nearer block placed first stands in the sight line to the farther ones' faces, and they can then only be placed
from somewhere else. A row of nine placed nearest first takes three spots; farthest first, one. The lead approved it with
conditions (only among blocks placeable now; never before a block's own support). The row stays open until the owner
agrees.

**Its cost, kept to main's.** Weighing every spot against the whole course raised `next`'s p99 on the keep from 36 to
64–70 ms. Two changes keep the choice the same at a lower cost:
- A spot serves at most the course's blocks within reach of its eyes. Spots are weighed in that bound's order, and only
  while one could still serve as many as the best. A spot is given up on once it can no longer, and weighed again in
  full if the best comes out lower. The choice is the one weighing every spot in full would make.
- `sealedIn`'s answer does not change with the block placed when that block is already searched over as wanted, and is
  not a door: placing it changes no cell of the search. Such blocks now share one search per spot. A spot inside the keep
  had searched once per block in its reach, 22 to 41 ms a spot. This also serves E-08a1's own calls.
