# F-28 — A headless simulator for the brain

Date: 2026-10-31
Agent / branch: Claude / claude/loose-ends-1029
Build: the commit that adds this file; recorded in [validation](../validation.md)
Status: complete. Every criterion of the acceptance row passes (below); the third passes by its second branch, the gap
recorded. Design changes and what was not built are listed at the end.

**It tests decisions and plans, not execution.** The adapter's `Native*` actions are not run, nor any Minecraft code.
What a skill does and how long it takes are the simulator's own approximations, close to vanilla where they matter.
A result from the simulator says what the player's decisions and plans do in a made-up world. It says nothing about
whether the body carries them out on a server. That is what the GameTests and the evaluation runs (F-26) are for.

## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: `./gradlew :companion-sim:runPacks` plays three scenario packs of a hundred seeds each, about
half a minute in all. Each run's `eval-result.json` and `latest.log` are written as an evaluation run's are, so
`tools/eval/score.py` scores a pack as it scores a server's runs. Each pack's `anomalies.txt` lists what the planner
did that no player should:

- a step it asked for twenty times running, refused each time;
- a minute of daylight with no step it could act on;
- iron left in a furnace it had walked away from.

Changed modules/files:
- `companion-sim` (new; depends on `companion-core` only, and on nothing else at runtime):
  - `SimWorld`: a seeded 128-by-128 world from y −64 to 127.
    - Terrain: hills of grass over dirt and stone, 40 worm caves, lava at the bottom of the deep ones, six lava lakes,
      gravel, trees.
    - Ores placed by the atlas's priors (`Priors.atHeight`), so the simulator and the atlas agree on where things are.
    - Light: sky light by column (leaves let it through), block light by radius, a day clock.
  - `SimRun`: one player's game.
    - The decisions are the core's own: `PlayerDecisionLoop` and its `TaskDirector` choose among candidates built as
      the adapter builds them.
    - `SurvivalPolicy` weighs threats and food; `PortalRunPlanner` plans from a `PortalRunSnapshot` and `WorldFacts`
      counted as the campaign counts them; `Lessons` turns deaths into habits; the `Atlas` keeps sightings and harm.
    - Skills, with outcomes and durations by vanilla's rules where they matter: breaking by tool and block, walking and
      sprinting, tunnelling, crafting by vanilla's recipes, smelting with vanilla's slots and fuel, eating, the night's
      dig-in, fights, runs and cover.
    - Mobs: zombies, skeletons, creepers and spiders, spawned, despawned and moving as vanilla's do (below).
  - `SimMain`: the packs as data (night: dusk on open ground with stone tools; caving: in a cave with an iron
    pickaxe; portal: empty-handed at dawn), run in parallel, with the timings and the anomalies.
  - `SimLog`: lines in the server's form, which `RunMetrics` reads.
  - `SimRunTest` (3): the same seed plays the same game; every line is in the server's form; the result is an earned
    evaluation record.
  - `build.gradle` with `runPacks`; `settings.gradle` includes the module.
- `companion-core`: `PortalRunPlanner`, the decision bug's fix (below), and `PortalRunPlannerTest` (one test).
- gametest: `NativeFurnaceWaitGameTests` (1), a new entrypoint.
- No new dependency; the manifest names the module.

Schema/configuration changes: none.

### The decision bug

Found by the caving pack's check for iron left in a furnace. The planner's iron stage:

1. smelts the first raw iron and makes a shield of the first ingot (or a pickaxe of the first three);
2. then asks for more iron ore, while the rest of its iron is still smelting;
3. the campaign walks off to the ore;
4. the campaign's snapshot counts a furnace's contents only within sixteen blocks, so on the way that iron drops out
   of the count, and out of the plan;
5. nothing brings the player back for it.

The night has a second way to the same loss. Below ground with logs next, the campaign mines "while the night passes"
(`withoutWood`), with the same disregard for the furnace.

On the final simulator, the same seeds with the planner before and after the fix:

| Pack | Runs that left iron in a furnace, before | Iron left, before | Runs after | Iron after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| night | 14 | 33 | 0 | 0 |
| caving | 55 | 214 | 0 | 0 |
| portal | 6 | 21 | 0 | 0 |

The fix came in two steps. The first fixed only the iron stage, and left one portal run with 8 iron in a furnace: that
was the night's way, found by the same check.

The fix (`PortalRunPlanner.ironSmelting`): the iron its furnace close by is smelting is waited for, not walked away
from. Both the iron stage and the night's work wait for it (`AWAIT_SMELT`), and only then go on for more.

Confirmed on a real server: `NativeFurnaceWaitGameTests.itWaitsForTheIronInItsFurnaceBeforeGoingOnForMore`.

- The scene: a passage sealed in stone and lit. The player holds four raw iron, one coal, a shield's planks and an
  iron pickaxe (so the first iron goes to a shield). Iron ore lies 24 blocks down the passage.
- The check: the furnace's contents at the moment the player first goes more than sixteen blocks from it.
- Before the fix: 0 of 1. "None of its iron left in the furnace when it went on: input 2 minecraft:raw_iron, output
  0 minecraft:air".
- After the iron stage's fix: 3 of 3 alone.
- On the final planner: 3 of 3 alone. The unthrottled test server plays the scene in about ten seconds each time.

In the core, `PortalRunPlannerTest.theIronSmeltingInItsFurnaceIsWaitedForNotWalkedAwayFrom` fails before the fix
("its three smelting waited for: Step[action=MINE_IRON, …, why=Mine iron ore (3/12)]") and passes after. The planner
tests are 40 of 40.

Neighbours on the final planner (the goal's fixtures and the smelting ones):

- `NativePortalCampaignGameTests` and `NativeSmeltGameTests` together: 94 of 97;
- the goal's class alone: 88 of 92;
- without the fix, the goal's class alone: 91 of 92.

Every fixture with a furnace passed in every run. Five fixtures failed in one run or another: the lava lakes' frame
site, the walled-in cell's cast, the tree in water, the far tree and the hungry hunt. None of them holds raw iron or a
furnace, so the changed rule never applies in them, and the lava lakes' failed without the fix too. They ran with two
evaluation servers on the same cores. None is among the failures of the full suite runs recorded in
[validation](../validation.md); the final suite on this branch is compared against them as well.

### Deaths an hour, against the server's

The comparable server figure is a player's first game hour of an earned `goal=nether` run, from the F-26 evaluation
runs (three players a run, so per player). The portal pack is a fresh start toward the same goal, one player for one
game hour.

| | Deaths a player-hour, pooled | Median of the seeds [range] | By cause |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The simulator's portal pack (final build) | 0.18 (18 in 100 player-hours) | 0 [0 to 7] | zombie 9, skeleton 6, spider 1, creeper 1, starvation 1 |
| Server, the same core (e49-base, on 39af678; nine full seeds) | 0.59 | 0.33 [0 to 1.67] | skeleton 5, zombie 5, creeper 2, a fall 2, husk 1, drowned 1 |
| Server, the night fix (093d188, nine full seeds) | 0.67 | 0.67 [0.33 to 1.33] | zombie 8, skeleton 3, spider 1, creeper 1, drowning 1, drowned 1, a fall 1, stray 1, polar bear 1 |
| Server, before the night fix (nine full seeds) | 0.37 | 0.33 [0 to 1.00] | creeper 3, skeleton 2, spider 1, cave spider 1, slime 1, drowning 1, drowned 1 |

The servers' figures are over nine seeds each. Seed 404 is left out: on it two of the three starters never came into
the world, in every ten-seed run (a fault of F-26's engine, since fixed). The first figures filed here, 0.48 for
e49-base and 0.60 and 0.33 for the other two, counted seed 404's two missing players as players with no deaths.

On the same core the gap is a factor of 3.3, the simulator low. Counting only the deaths the simulator can have
(zombies, skeletons, creepers and spiders), the server's is 0.44 and the simulator's 0.17: a factor of 2.6.

What the simulator does not have, and the server does:
- Deaths it cannot have: water and drowning; falls along the navigator's real routes; the biome's own mobs (husks,
  strays, drowned, slimes, polar bears, cave spiders). They are a quarter of the server's first-hour deaths on the same
  core, and a quarter to two fifths on the other builds.
- 1.21's world: noise caves, aquifers, ravines, mineshafts and dungeons, against the simulator's worm caves.
- A team: the server's runs have three players working near one another; the simulator plays one.

How the figure was reached: the first working simulator died far more than the server, 1.38 a game hour in the portal
pack, and at its worst 9.41. Each fault found on the way was the simulator's own, and is listed below. The final rules
are vanilla's wherever the simulator models a thing at all, and the remaining gap is what it does not model.

### The simulator's own faults, found on the way

None of these were the planner's. Each first looked like a planner fault or an odd death count, and was traced to
the simulator:

| What it showed | The simulator's fault | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plans asking for a shield with no ingot held, or a chestplate with six of the eight | A furnace's contents counted while far off, and after a death | Workstations are placed blocks that stay in the world; a furnace's contents count only within sixteen blocks, as the campaign counts them |
| "SMELT raw_iron: no fuel for 10" | Smelting refused without fuel for the whole load | Vanilla's slots; as much loaded as the best fuel smelts, the rest kept, as the adapter loads; charcoal a log with a plank |
| Creepers killed 64 of 138 | The player stood still until a walk ended; its fight outranked its escape and had no hit and run | Positions move as time passes; the campaign's creeper work (hit and run, run from a hissing one, a shield faces it, a wall when there is no way to run); a hissing creeper stops whatever is running |
| Mobs reached it through rock | They walked and shot through solid blocks | Open cells only, and sight for shots, fuses and blows |
| Fights of fifteen seconds with foes it could not reach | No foe left alone | A foe it could not reach is left alone thirty seconds, as the campaign does, unless it is in reach |
| Tunnels three times too cheap | A third of the blocks paid for | Every block on the line paid by its break time, and its drop taken |
| Hundreds of stuck spells | Fights and the night counted as stuck | The campaign's watchdog: only active work counts, and a new best or new ground starts the clock again |
| 719 digs for cobblestone with nothing dug | It stood on a tree's leaves | Stone in view first, then dug for underfoot, else refused; walks end on the ground |
| No dig-in under a tree's crown | Leaves counted as shelter | The campaign's own test: a shaft or a cave, not a forest floor |
| "Nothing to dig in to: air underfoot" | No gravity | It falls, with vanilla's damage; a walk's end lands on the floor, as the navigator takes no long drop |
| Night deaths gathering logs | The campaign's night rule for logs missing; SHELTER_EARLY learned but not used | Logs wait for daylight (below ground, the iron meanwhile); dusk is night once the habit is learned |
| Creepers again, then far too few deaths | Spawns 12 to 24 blocks off, pursuit unseen, a cap of six, despawned past 48 | Vanilla's: never nearer than 24, after a player only once seen within the follow range (35 for zombies, 16 for the rest), wandering until then, despawned as vanilla's, sixteen at most |
| Almost no deaths below ground | Too few caves; cave mobs only at the player's own height; no cave exploring | 40 caves; cave spawns at any height near it; the campaign's cave exploration for ore |

- execution of every kind: paths, swimming, the navigator's failures, block-by-block placement;
- the Nether and beyond;
- a team;
- the expeditions' planner;
- sleeping and pillaring.
