## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: with the voice on (E-20a), a person who may already command a native player (its controller or an operator) can ask it in chat to come over, wait a moment, stop, or toss them some of an item it carries: "Echo, come here", "Echo, stop", "Echo, toss me some bread". The model's reply carries the ask. The player says its reply first ("On my way."), then does what the matching `/aiplayer native` command does, through the same runtime method and with the speaker's own authority. Readiness, the edit and area rules and the arbiter are all checked again at that moment. If the command would refuse, the player says why in the command's own words. Nobody else can ask: for every other listener the prompt says no asks may be made, and a reply that carries one anyway is refused whole, so the fixed reply is said and nothing is done.

| Ask | Becomes | Through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COME | a walk to where the speaker stands, within a block and a half | `nativeGoto`, as `/aiplayer native goto <pos>` |
| WAIT | a wait of four seconds where it stands (the native wait's longest) | `nativeSkill(..., "native_wait", 4)`, as `/aiplayer native wait 4` |
| STOP | a stop, latched until resumed; work revoked as it goes in | `nativeLifecycle(..., "stop", ...)`, as `/aiplayer native stop` |
| TOSS item | up to eight of that item, from one stack of its own, tossed to the speaker | `nativeToss`, as the new `/aiplayer native toss <item> <count>` |

### Where the code and the design differ (code wins)

- There was no toss command, so one was added (`/aiplayer native toss <item> <count> [target]`), and the ask calls its method. A chat toss hands over up to eight, from one stack.
- A native player's commands are the `/aiplayer native ...` ones; `/aiplayer goto` and `/aiplayer wait` drive the legacy body.
  - COME is a native goto to the speaker's block, within 1.5 blocks.
  - WAIT is the native wait at its longest, four seconds.
  - A legacy body is asked through the same native methods, which refuse it in their own words.
- Being spoken to by name starts E-33a's two-second look at the speaker, and a native task is refused while another runs. So an ask waits in the player's tick for that look to end, at most three seconds, before it goes in. A stop never waits.
- A line that carries an ask is said at once, without the typing delay, and the ask goes in at the player's next tick: said first, then done, and a stop in chat no slower than the command's.
- The design has a non-controller's ask "refused, nothing starts, the reply still spoken". Here the whole reply is refused and the fixed reply is the one spoken: a line that tried to act for someone who may not ask is not said.
- An opponent in a match is never offered asks, even one who is an operator; the command still serves them as before.
- FOLLOW is not an ask: there is no native follow yet. The model is told to say that chat does not do that.
- The command's refusal can be technical, for example "Connect and resume a reconciled, idle native player; activity settlement must finish first." when it is busy. It is said as the command says it, as the brief asks.
