## Event kinds as a registry (2026-11-10)

`dev.kindred.core.record.EventKinds`: a kind is a string registered once by one owner (`core` for Kindred's own); the
same owner again is a no-op, another owner is refused. Core's kinds are registered by value in its static list; their
constants stay in the classes that fold or write them and stay compile-time constants (switches over kinds unchanged).
The adapter's market kinds (`market.intent`, `.cancel`, `.received`, `.uncertain`) are registered by `MarketDesk` as
`kindred-server`, the way an integration registers its own. `SqliteCompanionStore.record` writes only registered kinds:
a batch with another is refused whole as a failed future (logged by the runtime, never thrown on the tick). Saved events
of a kind no longer registered are still read and folded as before. The kind literals left in code became constants
(`Ledgers.CHECK`, `ActionLedgerView.JOURNAL`, `Capture.VULNERABLE`/`SECURE`, `MarketDesk.UNCERTAIN`). No schema change.

## F-31c: the double storage, decision pending (2026-11-11)

Filed at the lead's word. A journal line is kept in `journal_events` and as a `journal` event in `world_events` (`insertEvent`); an action-ledger row in `action_ledger` and as an `action` event for each insert or change (`actionEvent`, three call sites); both events with tick -1. On `keepdb-101-d640518` (3 players, seed 101, 12 game days; a build before F-31b) these were 99.9% of 92,786 events: journal 60,515, action 32,224. With their indexes, `world_events` 17.9 MB, `journal_events` 10.5 MB, `action_ledger` 7.8 MB, of a 36.3 MB file (dbstat on a copy taken after the stop).

Options: keep both as now; prune by a retention rule (F-31b folds settled `action` events after three game days; the journal is not folded); stop writing the mirror (every ledger rebuilt from the Record would need the tables' rows another way). Decision pending, the owner's: nothing changes before the month run without the owner's word (the lead, 2026-11-11).

## F-31d: the stop's flush within its bound (2026-11-12)

E-40's consoles ended with one `ERROR Kindred shutdown flush incomplete; reconcile on startup` (a `TimeoutException`
in `PlayerRuntime.finishShutdown`, after the result was written and the world saved): 4 of 10 in e40a20 (202,
20261030, 31102026, 404; RUNNER 2's count) and 8 of 10 in e40a21. The flush waits at most 10 seconds for every
player's writes, the World Record's last write and the store's close, all on the store's one writer thread.

**The cause, measured.** A scratch build (not committed) timed each job on the writer and the stop's chain, on two
144,000-tick runs as E-40 runs them (`starters=Kindred,Sol,Echo; goal=nether; ticks=144000; sprint=on`, the rest as
e40a20, two servers at once on four cores):

| The agreement check, every 24,000 ticks | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 (tick 143,999) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Seed 202 | 0.6 s | 1.9 s | 5.2 s | 9.3 s | 11.1 s | 15.7 s |
| Seed 404 | 1.0 s | 2.7 s | 6.4 s | 11.1 s | 14.5 s | 15.5 s |

The check rebuilds the action ledger from the Record's action events (`ActionLedgerView`). `Ledger.replay` applies
each event in turn, and `ActionLedgerView.apply` returns a copy of the whole ledger with the one row changed, so a
rebuild copied the ledger once for every event: actions times events. The check runs at `tick % 24,000 == 23,999`,
so an evaluation of 144,000 ticks begins its largest one tick before it ends. At the stop 6 to 8 jobs were queued
behind it; the players' last writes were done 13.4 and 13.6 seconds after the stop began, the ERROR at 10, the
close at about 12. It was the store's writer, not `PlayerMemoryStore`'s thread (that one closes before the
chain, in the stop itself). The same check held every write behind it in the middle of a run as well: a write
waited up to 11.1 and 14.5 seconds (the queue holds 256).

**The fix.** `ActionLedgerView.replay` folds the events into one map: the same rows, a pass. `apply` is unchanged
for anything that steps one event at a time. Nothing else changes: the timeout stays 10 seconds, no write is
dropped or reordered, and a stop with writes still outstanding still logs the ERROR and reconciles at the next start.

## F-31e: the memory files' last writes at a stop (2026-11-12)

Found while keeping a player's villagers across a restart (F-30d3): `PlayerMemoryStore.close()` only called
`shutdown()` on its writing thread, a daemon, and the chain `finishShutdown` waits for (every player's writes, the
World Record's last write, the store's close) did not include it. What the stop writes to the memory files (each
player's atlas, its peers, its rapport, its stores) could therefore be lost when the process ended at once, or read by
a runtime started again in the same process before it was made.

The fix: `PlayerMemoryStore.closeAsync()` is done once every write already asked for is made (its one thread takes
them in order; a last task after them completes it), and the stop's chain waits for it after the Record's last write,
under the same 10-second bound. `close()` keeps its meaning (it closes without waiting). The stop logs
`Kindred memory files written at the stop in N ms`. No timeout changes; nothing is dropped or reordered; a stop that
times out still logs the ERROR and reconciles at the next start.

| --- | --- | --- |
| `StoreMemoryFileTest.closedOnlyOnceEveryWriteAskedForIsMade`: 200 sightings written, then `closeAsync()`; the file on disk and read back whole at its completion | (new) | Pass |
| `NativeAtlasGameTests.whatItsAtlasLearnedJustBeforeAStopIsOnDiskWhenTheStopIsDone`: the atlas at its bound of 4,096 chunks, the chunk walked into within the atlas's minute, then the stop; what is on disk the moment the stop is done, then a restart in the same process | 0 of 3: "no atlas" on disk when the stop was done | 3 of 3 (the class 8 of 8 each): held on disk, and read back after the restart |

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Not built: the JSON stores moved into the Record (atlas, peers, rapport, bases, work, customs; F-32 makes the peer ledger
and atlas views over the web). The sale in the restart fixture: added with M-06g (2026-11-08). Work-claim leases (`ClaimRegistry`) stay epoch-scoped by design (a restart drops them); the
durable claims ledger is the territory one, `ClaimLedger`. A restart reads every event of the ledgers' kinds; snapshots
are not built (to measure on the month run). The Record is append-only for every kind but one: since F-31b, a settled
action's history older than three game days (3,600,000 ms of real time; action events carry no game tick) is compacted
into one `action.folded` event carrying its final row, so the action ledger rebuilt from the Record still equals its
table. Ledger kinds are never compacted. A new `action.folded` id is not news: readers by id (`RecordFeed`, the Chronicle,
the Wire's record, the web's rebuild) all filter by kind and none reads `action` or `action.folded`; a new reader by id
must filter by kind too. A `ledger_check` event's "through event N" names the ledgers' own kinds' last event, never an
action, so a fold leaves it true. Still linear: one `action.folded` per action ever taken (the action ledger's table, one
row per action, is kept whole for idempotency and reconciliation). A constant bound (old settled actions' rows and
summaries pruned together) is a named follow-up, Session A's call.
