## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: none yet. The owner's note of 2026-10-22: players on a two- or three-player run share out what they find, one taking a chestplate and another a sword depending on what each already has and what the team has.

**Finds shared out by what each lacks** (`ShareOut`, core, new, pure). The adapter is to say who is on the team, what each wears and holds of the kit that matters, and the work each is on: gathering, the Nether, the stronghold, building. Given a find (diamonds, iron, gold or netherite), it says who gets which piece, in the order to make it.
- **Essential first.** The team's first diamond pickaxe, which obsidian for the portal needs, goes to whoever is mining, before any armour.
- **Then the most for what it costs.** The piece that gains the most protection (vanilla 1.21.1's armour points and toughness) or damage for each ingot or gem, weighted by the work: armour counts for more to the one going to the Nether, the pickaxe to the one mining.
- **Between equals,** the player worse protected, then the lower id, so every player reckons it alike.
- **Nobody is given a piece no better than its own,** and what is too little for any piece is kept.
- **Gold is armour only** (a gold tool is worse than stone). One piece of it goes first to whoever is going to the Nether wearing none, so that piglins leave it alone, in place of an iron helmet, which is no better protection.
- **Netherite** only makes a diamond piece better, an ingot each.

Nothing here makes or moves an item: pieces are made from what the team found, and passed by hand.

**The run divided** (`RunDivision`, core, new, pure). Given the team (each player's armour points, pickaxe, gold and eyes) and where the run stands (the portal lit, blaze rods, pearls and eyes counted, the stronghold found, the team ready for the End), it says each player's part and why, in words it can say.
- **Before the portal is lit,** everyone gathers; the one with the best pickaxe digs for the portal's obsidian.
- **With the portal lit and rods or pearls short,** the best protected goes to the Nether, for rods first. A third goes too, for pearls from piglins, if it has gold to trade; the rest gather food, wool for beds and arrows.
- **With eyes enough,** the one holding the most finds the stronghold; the rest gather beds, arrows and blocks for the End.
- **With the stronghold found and the team ready,** all meet there.
- **Between equals,** the lower id; a lone player does each part itself, in turn.

**What it tells the team** (`RunDivision.Part.says`, `TeamTalk`, `SocialBrain.team`, core). Each part carries what the player says as it takes it up ("I'll go to the Nether for blaze rods.", "I've got gold; I'll trade with the piglins for pearls."). A find is said with who gets what of it, from the share-out alone and the count it holds: "I've got 11 diamonds: diamond boots and a diamond helmet for Wren and a diamond sword for me." Both go through the social lane's own topic (`TEAM`), under its consent, audience and allowance, and the same word is not said again for five minutes, so a part reckoned again each step is said once.

## In play: a find shared out and handed over (2026-10-25, `7e6e700`)

When a playing native player comes to hold more diamonds, iron ingots or gold ingots than at its last reckoning, it shares out what it holds
unpromised between itself and its teammates: the other native players in its world within 48 blocks, as all play together in co-op,
and none while it or they are in a match, whose own rules hand out items. (No team setting exists yet; the legacy owner field names each AI
player itself, so it could not serve.) If anyone else is
better for some of it, the share-out is saved through E-11's ordered writer as a project of hand-overs (`HandOvers`): one task for each
piece, its own included, each claimed by the finder with its material reserved from the finder's held stock (`held:<player>:<item>` in the
ledger), so the same find is never promised twice. The finder says what it found and who gets what (`TeamTalk.find`) to its controllers and
listeners. Then, above the next step of its own work and below anything urgent, it makes every piece at a table (sticks first for a weapon
or tool), carries each of a teammate's over and tosses it across from close by: each step an ordinary recorded skill (`native_craft`,
`native_goto`, `native_toss`). The toss verified completes the task; a piece of its own is done once made. The teammate puts on what it
is given as it does any armour it holds. A first sight of what a player holds is no find. A hand-over let go by a stop is claimed again by
its finder; nobody else takes one up. Six failed steps let it go, its material free again.

## In play: one portal for the team (2026-10-25, `79bb3e7`)

Before the portal, players on the Nether-portal goal together (native players in one world within 48 blocks, none in a match) divide the
run (`RunDivision`): the one that has begun a portal builds it, or else the one with the best pickaxe digs for its obsidian; the rest get
iron, food and wood for the run. Each says its part once, when it takes it up ("I've got the best pickaxe; I'll dig the obsidian for the
portal.", or "I'm building the portal."; "I'll get iron, food and wood."), and again only when its part changes. Of the parts, only these
two yet steer play: the others are reckoned and not said, as nothing does them yet. A player getting provisions follows its own route as
before until its own kit is in (an iron pickaxe; iron armour too where there are caves; a shield, or too little wood for one); not the
portal's flint and steel or buckets, which are the builder's. Then it stocks food (six) and wood (32) for the run and waits for the team's
portal (`AWAIT_TEAM_PORTAL`) instead of building one of its own. Once a teammate's portal is lit it takes it as its own and says so
("The team's Nether portal is lit."), claiming none of it; the builder says "I built and lit a Nether portal!".
