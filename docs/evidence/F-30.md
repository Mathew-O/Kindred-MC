## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: each player keeps a map of the world from its own play, saved with its memory. When it wants
iron or diamonds, it goes back to where it saw some, out past the reach it gathers ore in, rather than searching
anew. At night its walks pay extra for a place where it was hurt or killed by night, so a way round of about the same
length is taken. The strategist's report says how many chunks it knows and where it last saw iron and diamonds.
`KINDRED_ATLAS=off` turns it off.

Changed modules/files:
- `companion-core`:
  - `atlas.Atlas` (new, pure): the chunks it stood in or saw into, with the time and the biome; sightings per chunk by
    resource; the danger of each 16-block cell, by night or by day, fading over three game days; its shelters. Bounded:
    4,096 chunks (one it never stood in goes first, the longest unseen), 1,024 danger cells of 16 harms each, 64
    shelters. Queries: `whereToLookFor`, `nearestFrontier`, `dangerAt`, `dangerNear`, `safeShelterNear`, `lastSeen`.
    `snapshot` and `restore` for its file.
  - `atlas.Priors` (new) and `atlas/priors.txt` (new, a resource): where each resource is likely in 1.21.1, as data.
  - `eval.EvalScenario`: the `atlas` key, `on` by default.
  - `AtlasTest` (8); `EvalScenarioTest` and `EvalRecordTest` updated for the key.
- `companion-server-adapter`:
  - `PlayerMemoryStore`: `<uuid>.atlas.json` beside the player's memory, read and written off the game thread.
  - `PlayerRuntime`: the atlas kept per player across goals; read from its file once; fed once a second (the chunk it
    stands in and its biome, its bed), at each hit from a hostile mob, and at its death; saved once a game minute and at
    the end; handed to the goal; the night cost of its walks (`nightShun`); the switch (`atlas`); an accessor for fixtures.
  - `WorldKnowledge`: tells a listener of each block it remembers for the first time.
  - `PortalRunCampaign`: what its sweep sees goes into the atlas, and what it saw before the atlas was read; the leads
    back to iron and diamonds it saw (`lead`), after the ore in reach and before caves and digging; its seals as
    shelters; the report line.
  - `NativeNavigator`, `NativeGoToSkill`, `NativePlayerSkills`: a walk's extra cost of a place (`shunning`), passed with
    the player's movement habits.
  - `KindredMod`: `KINDRED_ATLAS=off`. `EvalBootstrap` applies the scenario's `atlas`.
- gametest: `NativeAtlasGameTests` (3), a new entrypoint.
- No new dependency; the manifest is unchanged.

Schema/configuration changes: a new file per player, `kindred/memory/<uuid>.atlas.json` in the world's folder: its
chunks (dimension, x, z, the last time, whether it stood in it, the biome, the sightings), its danger cells with their
harms, and its shelters. A world from before has none and starts with an empty atlas. `KINDRED_ATLAS` and the scenario
key `atlas`.

## The priors, checked against 1.21.1

Each number in `priors.txt` was read from the Minecraft Wiki's pages for 1.21.1's generation before it went into the
file (the file names the pages):

| Resource | In the file | 1.21.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diamonds | y −64 to 16; −64 to −50 weighted three times | Diamond ore from −64 to 16, most from −50 down (the design said −64 to −48) |
| Iron | −24 to 56 peaking at 16; 80 to 384 peaking at 232; −64 to 72 evenly | The three iron bands as the wiki gives them |
| Coal | 0 to 192 peaking at 96; 136 to 320 evenly | The two coal bands (the design said 0 to 190) |
| Lava | below −54; aquifers from −10 down; lakes above 0 | Air below −54 generates as lava; aquifers at −10 and below may be lava; lava lakes only above 0 (the design said near −55 and on the surface) |
| Villages | plains, meadow, savanna, taiga, snowy plains, desert | Java's village biomes; the design left out the meadow |
| Fortress | any Nether biome | As said |
| Stronghold | found by eyes | As said |

The upper bands of iron and coal weigh less than the lower: most of their range is open air outside the peaks.

## What the design said existed, checked against the code

| Claim | Found |
| --- | --- |
| `WorldKnowledge` remembers exposed blocks near the player, ores and fluids in its line of sight, cave floor, "and a few pins (portals, the fortress, eye flights, the stronghold)" | Partly. `WorldKnowledge` remembers what it sees as said, capped at 2,000 of each kind, but it belongs to the goal and is lost at a restart. The pins are kept by `PlayerMemoryStore`, saved per world, not by `WorldKnowledge` |
| "`ExpeditionPlanner` explores by distance and returns on triggers" | Changed. The runtime uses `ExpeditionPlanner` only for its return triggers (the walk home at night, in a storm, or hurt). The goal's exploring is the campaign's own `explore`: a heading per player, walked 28 blocks at a time, turned when it fails, within reach of home |
| "Nothing says … where it keeps dying" | Not quite. The campaign keeps up to eight danger zones in memory: twelve blocks round a death to a mob, for a game day; a strategist's place to avoid, for a quarter day; a cave's danger, for 6,000 ticks. They make it pass over ore and stairs there. Routes do not read them, and they are not saved. The atlas's danger is per 16-block cell, by time of day, saved, and read by night walks |
| Danger "from E-41a's harm record and the death record" | E-41a's record is one file per fight it fought. The atlas takes each hit from a hostile mob as it lands, fighting or not, and each death |
| Shelters: "its own beds, bases and sealed holes" | Its bed (its spawn point) and its seals go in. Its bases do not; nothing reads `safeShelterNear` yet |
| The route cost at night through `NativeRoutePlanner` | Walks are planned by `NativeNavigator` (in `NativeGoToSkill`); the night cost is added there, to each block's cost multiplier: half the cell's danger, at most 2, so a block in a cell marked by one death costs 2.5 times an ordinary one |
| Users: the mining choice "a strip mine at the prior's y against caving, weighed by the danger map" | Built differently: ore it saw and has out of reach is gone back to (`lead`) before caves and digging. The prior's height is used for a lead to the frontier, which no user takes yet |

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- The evaluation ran on the E-49 build, not the final one.
- The design's other users are not built: exploring toward the frontier (the campaign's `explore`), the fortress and
  stronghold searches, the team's share-out of sightings (a teammate's are marked as theirs in core, but nothing
  shares them), and its bases as shelters.
- A sighting is kept per chunk and resource: the latest place it saw that resource in that chunk. Stood within six
  blocks of it with none in view, it is let go.
- Updated docs: [operator guide](../operator-guide.md) ("The atlas (F-30)", the scenario's `atlas`),
  `tools/eval/README.md`.
