**The question** (the lead, 2026-11-12, from the owner's design `proposals/veteran-mechanics.md`): do the sound packets the server sends a player's connection reach a native body, and in what form?

**Answer: yes, all of them, at one place.**
- A native body is registered through `PlayerManager.onPlayerConnect` with its own `NativeConnection`. That gives it vanilla's `ServerPlayNetworkHandler`, so every packet the server sends the player goes through `NativeConnection.send`, which dropped it until now.
- The `SinkNetworkHandler` set when the body is built is replaced at that registration, so it never sees play packets.
- The diagnostic tap sits in `NativeConnection.send`. When a fixture listens, each sound-carrying packet is recorded, bundles included:

| Packet | What it carries |
| --- | --- |
| `PlaySoundS2CPacket` | The sound's id and category, its position, volume and pitch |
| `PlaySoundFromEntityS2CPacket` | The same, with the entity's id in place of a position |
| `WorldEventS2CPacket` | An event id that the client turns into a sound, such as 2001 for a block broken |
| `ExplosionS2CPacket` | The explosion's position, radius and sound |

**Who sends them, from vanilla's own call sites:**
- Entity sounds, footsteps, hurt, ambient calls, shots and block placing go through `World.playSound`. `PlayerManager.sendToAround` sends them to every player within 16 blocks, or 16 × volume when the volume is over 1. Walls are not taken into account. The source is excluded only when a player made the sound.
- Block breaks and doors arrive as world events (`syncWorldEvent`).
- Explosions arrive as their own packet, to players within 64 blocks.

**What never arrives:** sounds the client makes by itself.
- Lava's pop and ambient sound and water's flowing come from `randomDisplayTick` on the client, and nothing is sent.
- A bow being drawn has no sound in vanilla. A skeleton's shot (`entity.skeleton.shoot`) is sent.
- A sneaking player's steps are not played at all, so they're never sent. That matches the design's "sneaking is silent".

## Design note: the hearing interface (for E-71f's later steps)

**What core sees.** Core gets immutable observations, never packets or positions, and never Minecraft objects:

```
HeardSound(kind, bearing, distance, tick)
```

- `kind` is a small fixed vocabulary mapped from the sound id: CREEPER_PRIMED, BOW_SHOT, HOSTILE_CALL, STEP, BLOCK_BROKEN, EXPLOSION, DOOR, CHEST, EATING. Anything else is dropped.
- `bearing` is one of 8, relative to where the body faces.
- `distance` is NEAR (4 blocks or less), MID (8 or less) or FAR (16 or less).
- Nothing else is passed. The packet's exact position is used only to compute the bearing and the band, then discarded.

That is "never a position behind a wall": a groan through stone is a hostile call, FAR, to the north-west, and no more.

**Where it hooks in without blocking the tick:**
- The tap above, made permanent and on only when E-71f is enabled, maps each packet to a `HeardSound` on the server thread, inside the send.
- The work per packet is a map lookup and two small arithmetic steps. There is no IO, no allocation beyond the record, and no model call.
- The result goes into a bounded per-body queue (the newest 64).
- The body's observation builder drains that queue at its next decision, as it reads everything else the body perceives.
- Stop clears the queue and the tap, so nothing late is read after a stop (the cancellation barrier).

**Lava and water: emulate the client's own rule** (the lead, 2026-11-12). The server sends nothing for them, but a person's client hears them by its own rule, so the fair source is that rule and nothing more:

- **The client's loop:** `ClientWorld.doRandomBlockDisplayTicks` picks 667 random blocks around the player each tick, each within 16 blocks and again within 32 blocks. For each one it calls `randomBlockDisplayTick`, which calls the block's fluid's `randomDisplayTick`.
- **Lava** (`LavaFluid.randomDisplayTick`): only when the block above is air and not an opaque full cube. Then `block.lava.pop` at 1 in 100 and `block.lava.ambient` at 1 in 200.
- **Water** (`WaterFluid.randomDisplayTick`): only where the water is flowing and not falling. Then `block.water.ambient` at 1 in 64. Still water makes no sound, only particles.

The emulation:
- It runs that same rule, at those radii, counts and chances, on the server thread around the body.
- Each sound it makes becomes a hearing event of the same `HeardSound` shape: kind, one of 8 bearings, and a near/mid/far band.
- Lava sealed behind stone with no air above makes no sound for a person, and makes none here.
- It must never scan the blocks ahead of the dig face or read anything a client's own rule would not reach. That would be knowledge of unseen blocks.

"A bow drawn" has no sound in vanilla and is heard only as the shot.

## Step 2: the hearing observations (2026-11-12, `claude/e71f-hearing-obs-1112`)

The lead's step 2 builds the observations with no behaviour: nothing in the player's decisions reads them.

**Core** (`dev.kindred.core.hearing`):
- `HeardSound(kind, bearing, band, tick)`, immutable.
  - `kind` is one of CREEPER_HISS, BOW_SHOT, HOSTILE, STEP, BLOCK_BROKEN, EXPLOSION, DOOR, CHEST, EATING, LAVA or WATER.
  - `bearing` is one of 8, from where the body faced.
  - `band` is NEAR (4 blocks or less), MID (8 or less) or FAR.
- `Hearing.kindOf`, `bearing`, `band` and `heard` are pure functions of strings and numbers.
- `Earshot` is a bounded queue: the newest 64, O(1) to add, drained, and cleared.

**The adapter:**
- `NativePlayerBody`'s connection tap is permanent. Each sound packet is mapped to a `HeardSound` inside `send`, on the server thread, in O(1) (an instanceof, a map, two small sums). The exact position is used to bucket and is kept nowhere.
- `NativeAmbience` is off by default: it is on only with `KINDRED_HEARING_AMBIENCE=on`, read once at start, or when a fixture turns it on (the lead, 2026-11-12). It stays off until the reaction slice has a consumer for it, and that slice's gate weighs its cost. When on, it runs the client's own rule for lava and water each tick, from the body's own unchecked random (never the world's):
  - 667 picks within 16 blocks and 667 within 32, each axis `nextInt(r) - nextInt(r)`.
  - Lava with air above that is not an opaque full cube: 1 in 100, then 1 in 200.
  - Water flowing and not falling: 1 in 64.
  - A pick beyond 16 blocks (past where those sounds carry) is dropped before anything is read, and only chunks already loaded are read. So it reads less than the client does, never more, and loads nothing.
- `PlayerRuntime.listen` runs once a tick per connected body, after its tactical observation. It drains the earshot into the player's "heard lately" (the newest 64). A latched stop clears both, and a disconnect clears the body's.

**Cost when on:**
- 93 to 117 µs a tick per body in the fixture (`NativeAmbience.meanMicros`; 95 to 117 in one set of 3 runs, 93 to 105 in another), at vanilla's pick counts.
- With 8 players that is about 0.8 to 0.9 ms a tick, about 3% of keepdb8's 32 ms mean tick, which is why it is off by default.
- The thread-checked `Random.create()` cost 163 to 206 µs; it is now `Random.createLocal()`.
- The packet tap stays on: it is O(1) a packet.

**How the fixture checks bearings:**
- The fixture records where the body stood and faced each tick, and checks each sound's bearing against the source's position from there.
- Its idle gaze turns it, and its survival reflex steps it back from the creeper it sees. That reflex is sight, not hearing, and is as before.
- **The first runs' failure:** they assumed it kept facing south and failed for that reason. The fixture's expectation was corrected; nothing in the code changed for it.
