## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior:
- `/aiplayer watch on`: a person lets the AI players learn from them how deep they mine coal, iron and diamonds.
- `/aiplayer watch off`: it stops, and what was learned from them is deleted at once, from the server's file too.
- `/aiplayer watch`: says how it stands for them.
- Only the person can say it, for themselves. The console, a command block, an AI player and anyone running the
  command as someone else (`/execute as`, an operator included) are refused: "Only a person can say whether the AI
  players may learn from them, and only for themselves."
- Once those who opted in have mined 16 of an ore between them, an AI player's goal digs for it toward its own depth
  moved three tenths of the way toward theirs, never more than eight blocks. The log says
  `<name> mines iron toward y 15, leaning toward the depth this server's people mine it at`.

Changed modules/files:
- `companion-core`: `customs.ServerCustoms` (new) and `ServerCustomsTest` (5).
  - Consent: `optIn`, `optOut` (deletes the person's counts with their consent), `consents`.
  - Counts: `mined(person, ore, y)`, counted only for a person who consents and only within the world's heights; kept
    as counts by height in bands of four blocks, per ore and per person.
  - The custom: `miningHeight(ore)`, the median over everyone consenting, once 16 are mined in all.
  - The blend: `blend(ore, prior)`, the prior moved by at most 0.3 of the way toward the custom and at most 8 blocks.
  - `snapshot()` and `of(snapshot)` for the file; a snapshot read back drops the counts of anyone not consenting.
- `companion-server-adapter`:
  - `ServerCustomsKeeper` (new): hears ore breaks, learns from a consenting person's only, and keeps the customs in
    `<world>/kindred/customs.json`. The file is read once as the server starts, before its first tick. It is written
    off the server thread from a copy taken on it: the first thing learned at the next tick, then at most once a
    minute, and at once on an opt-in or an opt-out. Each write replaces the file whole.
  - `KindredMod`: the keeper opened at the server's start and handed to the runtime, fed by the break event, ticked,
    and closed at the stop.
  - `PlayerCommands`: `/aiplayer watch [on|off]`, and the line in `/aiplayer`'s help.
  - `mixin.ServerCommandSourceAccessor` (new, and its line in `kindred.mixins.json`): who really ran a command, which
    `/execute as` does not change.
  - `PlayerRuntime`: the customs handed to the goal's campaign at each decision; `oreDepthForFixtures`.
  - `PortalRunCampaign`: `depth(ore)`, used by the coal, iron and diamond steps where they used the constants (the cave
    they explore toward, the depth they dig down to, the climb toward coal); a log line when it moves.
- gametest: `NativeCustomsGameTests` (1), a new entrypoint.
- No new dependency (Gson is Minecraft's own).

### The GameTest

`NativeCustomsGameTests.aConsentingMinersDepthMovesTheOrePlanWithinTheBlendAndOptingOutTakesItBack`, on the field
fixture:

1. An AI player on the Nether goal, with the server's customs handed to its runtime. Its depth for iron is its own, 16.
2. Two mock people log in. The first runs `/aiplayer watch on` herself. The server runs the same command as the second
   person (`withEntity`, as `/execute as` does): refused, and he is not opted in.
3. She gets 64 diamonds, and says in chat, through the runtime's own chat entry, that she mines diamonds at y −59.
4. Each breaks twenty iron ore through the server's own `tryBreakBlock`, so the break event fires as in play: hers at
   y 12, his at y −40.
5. Learned: her twenty, his none. Her custom is 14, the middle of the band from 12 to 15. No diamond custom.
6. The AI player's depth for iron: 15, three tenths of the way from 16 to 14, rounded.
7. The file is written at the next tick with her counts. A second keeper opened on it reads the same: she consents,
   twenty iron, custom 14.
8. She runs `/aiplayer watch off`: she no longer consents, nothing of hers is counted, and the depth is 16 again.
9. The file, once written again, holds neither her UUID nor his.

The ground the ore was set in is kept and put back after.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Not built, so nothing is gathered for them: shelter time, the palette, the hours people play, the heat map and where
to settle. A custom is the server's as a whole, one per ore, for the overworld. It moves only the goal's ore steps: an
expedition's or a build's digging does not read it.

Design changes:
- **A person's counts are kept apart, keyed by their UUID.** The design kept no individual record beyond "this person
  opted in". But opting out must delete what was learned from them, and from a single total nothing can be taken
  out. So each person's share is kept as counts by height band: no time, no x or z, no order, no event. The players
  use only the sum.
- **Only the mining depth is built**, and so only ore breaks are heard. The design's other customs (shelter time,
  palette, hours, heat map, settling) would each need more watched (beds, placements, positions every minute) and
  are not in the acceptance row. Data is gathered only for a use that exists.
- **The prior blended is the campaign's own depth**, not the atlas's (above).
- **A custom stands on 16 ores mined in all**, the median by four-block bands, so that a few breaks move nothing.
- **Who runs the command is checked**, not only whose name it runs under, so `/execute as` cannot opt anyone in.
