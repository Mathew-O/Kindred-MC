## Why

From [the rung c read](eval/rungc-read-1123.md) of e40c4-b23acc0, consoles only:
- Five Nether players stood at one spot until the run ended, 37 to 130 game minutes each: 303 Echo, 31102026 Echo,
  404 Echo, 606 Kindred and 20261030 Kindred. Together that is about 436 of the batch's ~1,300 Nether minutes.
- Each freeze starts the same way:
  - every task in turn is cancelled with "Hazard/threat detected: urgent retreat required";
  - then "waiting for a feasible step";
  - then one report, `candidates=0 ... runner=CANCELLED ... blocker=Cancelled: Hazard/threat detected`;
  - no retreat ever runs.
- e40c3 shows the same freezes.

The hazard retreat offers only:
- a cell one block N, S, E or W at the same level, on a full solid floor that is not magma or cactus, with no fluid and
  room to stand;
- the lava escape, and only when in lava.

With none of those there is no candidate at all. The consoles do not say which block or which trigger it was. The
interrupt fires on any of three triggers: the hazard test, a lit creeper, or a fall to break with water.

### What the consoles already rule out

The hazard lasted: on 404, Echo's walk was tried again every 15 seconds or so and cancelled at once, for 100 minutes,
at one cell. Yet it took no harm: no death, no lower health shown. At b23acc0 the body did not sneak (E-71a's sneak came
later), so lava, fire or magma underfoot would have killed it within minutes.

That leaves the interrupt's other trigger: a lit creeper (ignited, or with a fuse of 25 ticks or under) in the **last
tactical observation**. Its abort text is the same, "urgent retreat required". It offers no hazard retreat, since the
hazard test is false. And `refreshTacticalObservation` swallows any failure of `observe` (`catch (Exception ignored)`),
so a failing refresh would leave the last observation, perhaps from the Overworld before the portal, in use for good.

This is a hypothesis from the consoles, not shown. The diagnostic below is widened so e40c5 can tell it apart.

## Part 1: the diagnostic (log only)

On the hazard interrupt, once a spell, a spell ending on the first tick none of its triggers holds:

```
<name> hazard spell at <x, y, z> in <dimension>: trigger <hazard | creeper | fall to break, joined by "and">;
saw <each clause of the hazard test that holds, with its block and offset from the feet; for being in lava without lava

retreat cells: north <free | why refused>, south ..., east ..., west ...; lava escape: <not in lava | to x, y, z |
no dry ground in sight>; cancelling <the skill cut short>
```

How the line is kept honest:
- **The refusals** come from `retreatRefusal`, the same function the retreat candidate now calls. Its checks were moved
  there unchanged and in the same order, so the line cannot differ from the decision. The reasons it can give: not
  loaded, out of the build limit, outside the border, the floor not a full top, magma or cactus floor, fluid at its
  feet, fire, a berry bush, powder snow or lava at its feet, or no room (with both blocks named).
- **What the test saw** comes from `hazardSeen`, which mirrors `isHazardousSituation`'s clauses one for one. That
  function is unchanged.
- **No behaviour changes.** The interrupt's condition, its cancel and every candidate are as before; only the line is
  added.

Widened for the creeper hypothesis, still log only:
- **The line's `creepers:` field.** For a creeper trigger, each lit creeper in the last observation (its distance, its
  fuse, whether ignited), with the observation's age in ticks and the dimension it was made in. Otherwise `none`.
- **A closing line at the spell's end:** `<name> hazard spell over at <pos> after <N> ticks, <M> task(s) cancelled`.
  It shows a lasting hazard as such.
- **`refreshTacticalObservation`'s failure,** logged at its first failure, and again only after a refresh has
  succeeded: `<name>: the tactical observation could not be refreshed at tick <t>; the last, from tick <t0>, stays in
  use: <exception>`. Before, it was swallowed.

## Part 2: the fix (next)

Fixture-first:
- staged on Nether ground where none of the four cells qualifies, using one of the triggers the consoles cannot yet
  tell apart;
- with this line in the fixture's log;
- it must freeze on main.

The rule: while hazardous, there is always a way off. Either a retreat beyond the four cells (a step up or down, or a
block to break or place, all through the ordinary break and place rules), or a move away from the hazard that the
interrupt does not cancel.

After that:
- the Nether and hazard classes ×3 and the corpus gate;
- once e40c5's lines show the real trigger, check that the fixture's staging matches it.
