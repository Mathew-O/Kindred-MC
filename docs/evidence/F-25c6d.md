The hazard retreat offers only:
- a cell one block N, S, E or W at the same level, on a full solid floor that is not magma or cactus, with no fluid and
  room to stand;
- the lava escape, and only when in lava.

With none of those there is no candidate at all. The consoles do not say which block or which trigger it was. The
interrupt fires on any of three triggers: the hazard test, a lit creeper, or a fall to break with water.

## Part 1: the diagnostic (log only)

On the hazard interrupt, once a spell, a spell ending on the first tick none of its triggers holds:

```
<name> hazard spell at <x, y, z> in <dimension>: trigger <hazard | creeper | fall to break, joined by "and">;
saw <each clause of the hazard test that holds, with its block and offset from the feet; for being in lava without lava
at the feet, the nearest lava cell it touches and its distance>; feet <block>, floor <block>;
retreat cells: north <free | why refused>, south ..., east ..., west ...; lava escape: <not in lava | to x, y, z |
no dry ground in sight>; cancelling <the skill cut short>
```

How the line is kept honest:
- **The refusals** come from `retreatRefusal`, the same function the retreat candidate now calls. Its checks were moved
  there unchanged and in the same order, so the line cannot differ from the decision. The reasons it can give: not
  loaded, out of the build limit, outside the border, the floor not a full top, magma or cactus floor, fluid at its
  feet, fire, a berry bush, powder snow or lava at its feet, or no room (with both blocks named).
- **What the test saw** comes from `hazardSeen`, which mirrors `isHazardousSituation`'s clauses one for one. That
  function is unchanged.
- **No behaviour changes.** The interrupt's condition, its cancel and every candidate are as before; only the line is
  added.

## Part 2: the fix (next)

Fixture-first:
- staged on Nether ground where none of the four cells qualifies, using one of the triggers the consoles cannot yet
  tell apart;
- with this line in the fixture's log;
- it must freeze on main.

The rule: while hazardous, there is always a way off. Either a retreat beyond the four cells (a step up or down, or a
block to break or place, all through the ordinary break and place rules), or a move away from the hazard that the
interrupt does not cancel.
