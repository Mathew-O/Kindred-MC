## Scope

Test only. No product code, and no assertion, tick limit or timeout is touched.

1. **An audit.** `FixtureBounds`, with mixins in the gametest source set only, notes every block set during a test's
   start, its steps and its end-of-test work against that test's structure box. When a test ends it logs once:
   `Fixture ground outside its plot: <test>: it set N blocks, K in ground it keeps; left: M not air above its floor;
   kinds; from … to …; its plot …`, or "all in ground it keeps". From the second batch on, a test's players' own
   placing and breaking are counted too, as "its players".
   - The test server's own placing of a test (its structure, the area cleared round it, its barriers) and its marks
     (beacon, glass, lectern) are left out.
   - It reports only. The failure corpus runs without it.
2. **The restore.** `FixtureGround.restore` no longer writes into, or discards an entity within, the plot (and
   barrier shell) of another test that is placed and not yet ended. It logs how many blocks and entities it left.
3. **A second kept box.** `FixtureGround` kept one box per test. A test that kept two, such as the nights helpers' pad
   and floor, had the first replaced by the second, and the first was never put back. Every box is now kept and put
   back, the last first.
4. **The audit splits what is left from what is kept.** Blocks a test sets outside its plot are counted apart when they
   fall in ground it keeps. A test whose writes all fall in kept ground logs that instead of a warning.
5. **The atlas fixtures keep their raised ground.** Five atlas fixtures lay a floor, causeway or pad 20 blocks up past
   their plot: `walkPastAMarkedCell` (night and day), the diamonds, restart and restart-plan tests, and the loose
   diamond. They now keep and put back that ground, ore included.

## Why the restore overwrote a neighbour

The test server places the next batch within the tick of the test that ends the one before. A stack trace on the
placing shows `GameTestState.init` called from `TestRunContext.prepareStructure`, inside that tick. This happens
before the ending test's own listeners run, `FixtureGround`'s restore among them. Tests in one batch also run side by
side.

So a box kept past its test's plot held cells that, when it was put back, were already a neighbour's. In
`NativeBringGameTests`, each test's restore found 390 such cells in the next bring's plot, including its barriers and
structure block. On main it wrote all 390 back. The restore also discarded every non-player entity in its box,
whosever it was.

## Second batch: the players' own blocks, the build and house fields

- **What the audit missed.** It counted only blocks set during a test's own steps, so an AI player's placing and
  breaking on the server's tick went uncounted. LOOPS found one such leftover on #274: house2's player set a crafting
  table down in the plot next door, 41 s before the table-loop batch began there.
- **What it now counts.** Each native player belongs to the placed test whose plot it comes into, or the nearest within
  16 blocks. Its `interactBlock` and `tryBreakBlock` writes are counted for that test as "its players".
- **What that found** in `NativeHouseGameTests` and `NativeBuildGameTests` (1 run). The house players left their whole
  buildings outside their plot, none of it kept:
  - the cabin: 248 not air;
  - the two-floor house: 517, chests and a furnace among them;
  - the manor: 955, bookshelves and chests among them.
  Eight build fixtures' players left a crafting table, cobblestone or dirt.
- **The fix.** Every house field (3) and build field (8) keeps its ground.
- **Result.** Both classes passed 14 of 14 in each of 3 runs. No player write was left outside ground its test keeps.
