# F-35m1 — A base, hall, pen or plot sited only on ground the player has seen

Status: in progress. The code, the staged fixture, the classes and the corpus gate are below; a live run remains.

## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: an AI player choosing where to build sites its first base, its trading hall, its animals' pen and
its wheat plot on ground it has looked at. Before, the siting read the server's heightmap of every column within reach,
seen or not, and chose ground it could not know: flat ground behind a wall, the far side of a hill. F-35m made the far
walks follow the same rule; this is its siting half.

1. **A memory of the ground it has seen.** Each player keeps the tops of the columns it has seen
   (`Sight.SeenColumns`, at most 8,192, the farthest from its last look dropped first), filled by its own sight
   lines: 16 a tick, spiralling out to 32 blocks from where it stands (`SeenGround.sweep`). A column is kept only when
   a sight line from its eyes reaches the column's top, and is read then by the base's own column rules (firm ground,
   open cells over it, water). It is not saved: after a restart it looks again.
2. **Siting reads only that memory.** The base stage, the trading hall, the pen and the plot, and the prospective
   site the base waits on, are chosen from seen columns alone. A column it has not seen is no ground to it.
3. **A wait while it has seen too little.** With no site yet, the base looks again in 200 ticks while what it has seen
   is still growing, and in 1,200 once it has stopped.
4. **The site checked at the stand.** When it comes within 6 blocks of a site newly chosen (base, hall, pen, plot), it
   looks over the site's own columns again from there (`SeenGround.lookOver`) and reads the floor (`BaseSite.floorAt`).
   If the site no longer fits, the unbegun record is dropped and it sites again. A site it has begun is never dropped.

Still reading unseen ground, and not changed here: the rail route, the water crossing and the bring's ground
(`RailRoute.plan`, `WaterCrossing.plan`). They are their own slice.
