## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: where walking will not do, the End goal makes a way as a player would, choosing by cost:
- across a gap or a lava inlet, by bridging with its own blocks;
- up a cliff, by pillaring;
- through a wall or a hill, by digging.

Walking is cheapest. Digging costs by the block's hardness (half a point plus one and a half times the hardness). A placed block costs six, and fourteen more over lava within six blocks below. A route therefore builds only where going round is much longer. The player asked for this in so many words: "determining whether it's optimal to climb in situations or just to build up to it".

Changed modules/files:
- `NativeRoutePlanner` (new): an A* search over the navigator's own walking moves plus three edits, reading only loaded blocks:
  - DIG: the one or two blocks in the body's way at the next cell, when the server's rules and the player's tools allow them;
  - BRIDGE: a block of its own under an open cell with nothing to stand on;
  - PILLAR: a block under its feet, with head room to rise into.
  It edits nothing itself.
- `PortalRunCampaign.buildRoute`: the next step of that route as one ordinary recorded skill:
  - a walk to where the first change is made;
  - `native_break` for a dig, `native_place` for a bridge, `native_pillar` for a pillar.
  The route is planned again from wherever the player then stands. Blocks come from what it carries (cobblestone, dirt, stone kinds, netherrack), and none at all when the server's edit policy forbids edits. `diggableLater` applies the break rules (edit policy, spawn protection, no liquid beside, a tool that can harvest it) without the reach and sight that hold only once the player is beside the block. A change that fails (a jump capped by a ceiling) sets its cell aside for a minute, so the next plan goes another way. The search leans toward the target (weighted A*, twice the distance estimate) and stops at 2,500 positions; a plan that takes 20 ms or more is logged.
- `NativeNavigator`: each search now reads each block, chunk, standing test and terrain cost once. Move generation was about 50 µs a position without the caches and about 25 µs with them (in a cold test server), which every walk's search shares.
- `PortalRunCampaign.tunnelStraight`: an explorer's tunnel that keeps to its heading and never steps down below a floor.
- `StrongholdLeg` (the End goal), which uses all of this:
  - exploring the Nether: where a walk on fails, or gets nowhere, or a ledge leaves no stand ahead, it makes a way along its heading for a while, and only then cuts straight on through the netherrack;
  - headings are chosen by the longest open view, never straight back, and seldom over ground already explored;
  - it keeps above the lava sea (Y 40), and climbs back above it by the cheapest way when below;
  - to a fortress it has seen, it makes a way once a walk there has failed;
  - in the Overworld, toward the stronghold, it makes a way when every straight line is refused;
  - on a shore where water refuses every step down, it goes inland first.
- `PlayerRuntime.nativeRouteTo`: one route step for fixtures, as the goal takes it.
- `NativeNavigator.world()`.
- `NativeNavigationGameTests.aRouteBridgesAGapAndDigsThroughAWall` (new, batch `buildRoute`).

Dependencies satisfied: F-10c (the navigator), F-10d (walking), E-03a (roaming edits).
Schema/configuration changes: none.

What the staged runs found, in order:
- Run 6: the first made way bridged three blocks east and then gave up for the explorer's tunnel, which turned round and walked 13 blocks back. A walk-only route is now taken unless a walk along a made way has just failed, and the explorer's tunnel keeps to its heading.
- Run 7: with no way on along the heading, the explorer returned nothing. The campaign's last resort, a jump down, took it down toward the lava sea, and a hoglin knocked it in. The explorer now tries its other headings, then looks round. Jump-downs never land beside lava, and in the Nether never below Y 40.
- Run 8: on a ledge it stood looking round, because a made way was tried only after a failed walk. The made way is now the fallback wherever no stand lies ahead. It then turned round and walked about 200 blocks back along its own tunnel, which led to headings by open view and the memory of explored ground. A pillar that failed was proposed again and again, so failed changes are now set aside.
- Run 9: it flipped between walking to a stand and making a way in one spot, because the spells were timed and a sprinted server's ticks ran them out within two passes. Spells are now counted in passes too, a walk that gets nowhere counts as failed, and one pass never turns straight back. Then, by the fortress, it circled: the fortress stood below the explorer's Y 40 floor, and a walk there kept ending where it began. The floor now drops to a known fortress's level, a walk there that gets no closer turns to a made way, and with no brick in walking reach it makes a way onto the nearest one.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Not tested / known failures:
- Not yet used by the Nether goal's own walks, only by the End goal's.
- Each pass plans again from scratch (about 25 ms when it makes a way). Following the last plan while it still holds would cut that.
- No ladders, scaffolding, towers or water bucket drops; no sneaking at a bridge's edge.
- Leaves are dug through like any soft block. Shears, to keep them as building blocks, are a note for later.
- Water routes (boats, swimming down) are a later slice.
