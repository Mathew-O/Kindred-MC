## Scope and implementation

The fixed portal route (logs, stone, iron, diamonds, obsidian mined with a diamond pickaxe) becomes the cheapest plan in an ordinary world rather than the only one. Requested by the owner so that a player can still progress in a world with limited resources, such as Skyblock, and so that the plan follows the world as the player learns it.

Slice (a), the choice machinery:
- `WorldFacts` (core, pure): the kinds of sources the player has actually seen, each with the distance to the nearest (trees, stone, coal, iron and diamond ore, lava, water, gravel, caves), and the methods the adapter can carry out. The planner never chooses a method outside that set.
- `PortalRunPlanner.plan(snapshot, facts)` and `stage(snapshot, facts)`: for the frame's obsidian, `frameMethod` picks the cheaper of mining it (a diamond pickaxe: free once owned, cheap with diamonds in hand or diamond ore in sight, a long search otherwise) and casting it in place (a second bucket: three more iron). With casting chosen, the route asks for two buckets, skips the diamond stage, fills a water bucket and then casts the frame (`CAST_PORTAL`). The single-argument `plan` and `stage` keep the ordinary world (mining only), so existing behaviour is unchanged.
- `PortalRunCampaign.facts`: the nearest observed source of each kind from `WorldKnowledge`, rebuilt once a second, so the choice changes as soon as the player sees something new.

Slice (b), casting carried out in the world:
- (b1) `native_cast`, one recorded action per frame block: water from the player's own bucket poured above the target, lava from its own bucket poured into the target, where vanilla hardens it at once because water touches it, and the water taken back. Both cells are claimed; the ledger verifies obsidian at the target, one lava bucket emptied and the water bucket back. Each pour is aimed at a point on a solid neighbour's face that the player can actually see (the centre first, then points toward the edges, because the block below can hide a face's centre from a low eye), and the water at a visible point inside the poured source. If the lava cannot be poured, the water is taken back and the action ends with no effect instead of uncertain.
- (b2) `PortalRunCampaign.castPortal`, offered now that it exists (`CAST_OBSIDIAN` is in the campaign's methods): a lava bucket filled at the nearest lava it has seen (walking up to the pool when the fill is refused from where it stands), a frame site chosen and cleared as for a mined frame, cobblestone corners, then the ten frame blocks bottom-up, each after a mould block from its own stock behind the cell and the one above it so every pour has a face to aim at; it waits for draining water and steps to another spot on the front row when a cast is refused. Then flint and steel and ignition as before.
- The frame site is found from where the player stands or, failing that, from the nearest spot within six blocks that has room, as a player steps aside to a clear patch; the same search now serves the mined frame. (The fixture spawns the player in different places each run; from some of them, with the pool on one side and the arena wall on the other, no site existed at its feet.)
- Clearing a frame site walks to its stand when a cell is only out of reach, and gives the site up only for a cell that cannot be broken (a fixture run picked a site from a spot beside the arena wall and abandoned it every tick). Mould blocks count reach to the face clicked, as for a player (the top of the block below), rather than to the cell's centre, which put the top-row mould just out of reach from a stand a few tenths off centre. When the lava cannot be poured after the water is (the bucket still full), the cast takes its water back and ends with no effect instead of uncertain. A refused cast is retried from another spot each time, including the row behind the front row, from which the upper side cells' faces show past the frame below; a site given up because a cell cannot be cleared is never chosen again, and the cell is logged. Sites with lava within three blocks of the frame, the row in front or the stand are not chosen: in one run the stand was on the pool's edge and water from the casts hardened the rest of the pool. A cached lava source that has been set aside gives way to the next, and a refused fill is tried again from closer up before its source is set aside. The knowledge sweep now also checks sight to a fluid's surface, not only its centre: a pool set into the floor was otherwise seen only from within about two blocks, and in one run the player never noticed it three blocks away.
- The navigator no longer stands the player beside lava at body height or under it. In one fixture run the player filled its bucket from the edge of the pool, walked into the one-deep hole that left, and died when lava from the neighbouring sources flowed in; the hole is now refused as a standing spot.
- Planner: the cast route keeps about two blocks per frame block still to cast (`castBlocks`) instead of the classic route's stockpile of eighteen, which would otherwise send it back to mine stone halfway through the frame; a lava bucket in hand counts as one of the two buckets; and only the iron pickaxe's two sticks (plus the spare pair every route now keeps) are kept in reserve, not two more for a diamond pickaxe it will not make (live run 37 climbed out of the caves for a log just to have those spare).
- `native_bucket` fill now also aims at points on the visible surface of a source, not only its centre, as a player clicks a lava pool set into the floor from a few blocks away.

Slice (c), renewable sources for scarce worlds (cobblestone generator and replanting done; charcoal not yet):
- World type from what a player can tell: the adapter offers `DIG_FOR_STONE` where there is a floor at the bottom of the world under the player (bedrock), and `COBBLE_GENERATOR` and `REPLANT` where there is none, as under a sky island. The planner takes stone from stone in sight or ground to dig; with neither it builds a generator from a bucket of lava and one of water (water first), filling either bucket at a known source if it has an empty one (`FILL_LAVA` is new). Where there is no ground to dig, stone and leaves in sight count only within eight blocks (`ISLAND_REACH`); farther off they are likely on another island. The cobblestone stockpile waits while no more stone can be had, so a limited island with a few stone blocks and no starter buckets gets its iron, and with it the buckets for a generator, first.
- The generator (`PortalRunCampaign.buildGenerator`, `mineGenerator`): a one-wide trench dug into the ground, lava at one end and water at the other, the water falling into a hole dug beside the cell where cobblestone forms, so lava flowing down the trench meets falling water and hardens there each time that cell is mined; the player mines from a pocket dug beside that cell, low enough to pick up the drop. The site is chosen near the player where every cell to dig is plain ground open above, the floors and walls that hold the fluids are solid, and nothing that burns but clearable grass is within the lava's fire range (three blocks around and above); the grass there is cleared before the lava goes in. It digs and pours from the ground beside each end. Once built, its cells and walls are never dug into, its lava and water are never taken for other work, and no wooden workstation is placed within the lava's fire range (the player first walks out of it). Mining the generator's cobblestone beside its flowing lava is allowed because water beside the cell hardens the lava that runs into the gap (`RoamingEdits`); `native_bucket` gained a lava pour.
- Replanting: carried saplings are planted on dirt or grass open to the sky with room for a trunk, two blocks from other saplings and trunks and out of a generator's fire range; with no tree left, leaves left behind are broken by hand for saplings; when wood is needed and only planted saplings remain, the player waits by them. Wood is the fuel where there is no coal. The rest of a trunk whose lower logs were cut now counts as reachable from the ground beneath it (one tree is all a sky island has).
- No iron chestplate where there are no caves to go down into (no ground below), which saves eight iron on a sky island.
