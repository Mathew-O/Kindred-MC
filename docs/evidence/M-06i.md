## Scope and implementation

`companion-core` `factions/FactionPlay`: one pure decision over an immutable `View`. The view holds the ledgers' public
facts (factions, power, claims, safe zones, captures), the player's coins, its own sightings of enemy land, the
market's quotes for its spare stock and its wants, the auction house's listings, and its personality's bounded
weights. The decision returns the choices in order, the first to do now.
- Membership:
  - A sociable player (sociability 35 or more) joins the invitation with the most members.
  - Otherwise it founds its own, at once with initiative 50 or more, or after a game day without an invitation.
- Claims (officers and the leader):
  - With nothing held, it claims the chosen base site. After that, the free chunk touching its land nearest its
    first claim.
  - Each claim is tried against `Territory.claim` with the view's facts, so capacity, separation, the border, the
    spawn exclusion and the cost all apply.
  - Beyond the first claim it keeps a reserve back: one claim's cost, plus up to two more for a cautious player.
- Earning and spending:
  - It sells all spare stock.
  - For each want, it bids the least the house takes (`Auctions.least`, newly public) when that is under the market's
    ask, or buys at the ask. Either only within what it would pay and what is left above the reserve.
  - It never bids on its own listing.
- Defence:
  - A loss within ten minutes (a capture under way) puts going home first and alone.
  - A cautious player (caution 50 or more) also goes home while its faction is exposed, after earning and before any
    raid.
  - Neither applies when it already stands on its own land.
- Raids (officers, healthy, combativeness 40 or more), only from chunks it saw:
  - Standing in a seen enemy chunk that `Capture.capture` accepts now, it captures it.
  - Otherwise it scouts the nearest seen chunk of an enemy that is now exposed, unshielded and on that enemy's border.
  - A chunk it never saw is never chosen.

Order (revised while wiring): a capture needs claim capacity to spare, and a player with capacity to spare claims free
land first, so as first written it would almost never raid. A combative player (`RAID_FIRST`, 60 or more) with an
exposed enemy border in sight now raids before it expands; any other player expands first. No raid is chosen while a
defence is.

Runtime (companion-server-adapter):
- `FactionsPlayer`, per AI player:
  - its sightings: claimed chunks of another faction that it stood in or beside, kept to the last 512 and not across
    a restart;
  - its spare stock: common building blocks beyond 64 of each;
  - its faction name;
  - where a walk ends: three blocks inside the chunk.
- `PlayerRuntime.factionCandidates`, run among the routine candidates after a match's own (duel, hunt, raid):
  - once every 30 seconds it builds the `View` and takes `FactionPlay`'s first choice;
  - weighing only chooses: it never acts.
- The choice becomes an ordinary task:
  - a walk (`native_goto`) to a claim elsewhere, a border it saw, or its faction's home;
  - or a `native_faction` request (`NativeFactionAction`), made only when that task is dispatched under the player's
    lease and written to the action ledger like every recorded skill;
  - nothing is asked again after a stop or restart, and a stale copy of the task is answered "Already asked".
- The request goes through the same `FactionsService.request` / `MarketDesk` / `AuctionHouse` calls a person's
  commands use, with every rule checked again where the player stands.
- A first version acted while candidates were only being weighed. On a body whose operator had not set it playing,
  it founded and claimed. That was replaced with the dispatched task above before anything was committed.

The evaluation scenario (`mode=factions`):
- `EvalScenario.Mode`: the run's faction world.
- `EvalBootstrap`:
  - once the starters are in, it sets the mode (`PlayerRuntime.factionsWorldForEval`) and the hub safe zone;
  - at the end it audits the Record before writing the result.
- Core `FactionsAudit`: the locked rules checked from the Record alone.
  - Each claim and capture is decided again by `Territory.claim` / `Capture.capture` from the state just before it,
    under the run's settings.
  - No player kill inside a safe zone.
  - Coin balances chain and are never negative; coins come only from starting coins and market sales; the auction
    house only moves or destroys coins.
  - Each sale has its seller's uncancelled intent; deliveries are begun once and handed over at most once.
- `EvalRecord.factions`: the audit in `eval-result.json`. `score.py` adds the audit's table to the summary.
- `tools/eval/README.md` documents the key and the result.
- `FactionPlay.beyondSpawn`: a faction whose site lies inside the spawn exclusion (a run's starters spawn at spawn)
  claims its first land just beyond the exclusion on the same bearing, walked to in legs of 48 blocks
  (`FactionsPlayer.LEG`).
- `GameModes` FACTIONS stays unplayable for operators until M-06i is complete; the run sets the mode for itself.
- Founders and joiners:
  - `players=<names>` adds AI players by name beside the canonical starters (`createNative`).
  - `founders=N` sets bounded traits as an administrator may (`PlayerRuntime.personalityForEval`): the first N players
    found and invite (initiative 80, sociability 60); the rest wait to be asked and join (20, 70).
  - `FactionPlay`'s new INVITE choice: an officer with sociability 40 or more asks a player it met (within 24 blocks,
    seen in the last five minutes) who is in no faction. It is asked before land is bought.
  - An invited player joins the faction with the fewest members that asked it.
  - A founder's site refused (another faction settled beside it) moves to the nearest chunk within three that the
    rules allow.
- A walk ends on standable ground nearest the player's own height. The column's top was once a block above the
  corridor, and the capture fixture failed one run in three until this.
- The native-player limit (the lead's decision, 2026-11-08):
  - `maxNativePlayers` in `edits.properties`, the server's own policy, read at startup (`PlayerRuntime.readNativeLimit`).
  - Default 3, so nothing changes anywhere else; clamped to 1..12.
  - Logged at startup; the refusal names the setting; recorded in the manifest.
  - `policyFile` keeps it when an operator's command rewrites the file. Before this, any edits, dragon or bases change
    would have dropped it.
  - The attention scheduler takes up to 12 players in a tick's batch (`AttentionSchedulePolicy.withMaxPlayers`), with
    the same per-tick budget shared. At three it guarded a batch of three, and a fourth body crashed the server
    ("Batch size 4 exceeds maxPlayers 3").
  - `run.py --policy maxNativePlayers=8` sets it for an engine run.
  - Every run's result now carries the server's load (`server`: tick mean and worst, heap at most), which `score.py`
    tabulates.
  - Qualification at 8 stays separate: the owner's month run uses 8 only after an engine run at eight players on one
    server measures tick time (mean and worst per seed) and heap. That measurement is not run yet; it is queued
    through the lead.

A walk it can find no way to (the engine run `factions1-99a710a`, 2026-11-08, showed the same unreachable target
refused 14,604 times on one seed, re-issued at every evaluation):
- Each refusal of a faction walk backs that target off: 200 ticks, doubling, to a game day at most.
- After three refusals in a row the target is set aside for its wait. `FactionPlay`'s view excludes set-aside
  places from claims, raids and defence, so it chooses another.
- A walk that gets there forgets the target's refusals.
- A refused walk also clears the weighed candidates, so their stale copy is not dispatched again. That copy was a
  fourth refusal in the same second.

The faction channel on the Wire (F-34, on main as #66):
- The runtime sets the Wire's faction membership from the Factions ledger (`factionKey`: an AI player by its body).
- An AI player's accepted request is sent on its faction's channel as `faction.founded`, `faction.joined`,
  `faction.claim` or `faction.capture`. Setting out to defend or scout is sent as `faction.defend` or
  `faction.scout`. Each carries its place and line, every fact visible to the faction only, and nothing goes outside it.

## The standing goal (after factions2b)

factions2b (the same ten seeds with `bases=true`, at ffd02cb) made no progression either. The seeds' consoles show why:
not one `native_break`, `native_craft`, `native_place` or `native_eat` in four game hours on seeds 104 and 110, the
only base lines three within twelve seconds of the start ("Stone tools first: the cabin's walls are stone"). A player
with no goal only reacts to needs and requests (`SurvivalGoal.NONE`), and nothing in a faction world set one.

What changed:
- `StandingGoal.of` (core, pure): the goal a player works toward is its own when one was set for it ("none" included,
  `goalChosen`, not saved); else, in a faction world with world edits on, the survival progression: the dragon where
  the server allows the fight (`dragonFight`, the check `/aiplayer goal dragon` makes), the End portal otherwise.
  Computed each time (`PlayerRuntime.goalOf`, every read of the goal), never stored; leaving Factions mode or setting a
  goal wins at once. No saved key changed (the goal is not saved at all).
- Life first holds in any faction world, goal or not (`lifeFirst`), so the order there is life (its bed at night,
  food when hunger is low), then the faction world, then goal steps. Outside faction worlds it is as before: life
  first only for a player with no goal and work of its own.

The lead's three checks:
1. **One controller.** Life first's tasks are candidates in the same list as the goal's
   (`evaluateScheduledRoutineCandidates`: `lifeCandidates` at the life-first step, `factionCandidates` after it,
   `campaignCandidates` for the goal), dispatched through the same `SkillRunner` and `ActionArbiter`; its hunt is the
   goal campaign's own (`PortalRunCampaign.huntFood`), registered in the goal's task record (`entry.campaignTasks`).
   No second path drives the body.
2. **No double care.** Food: life first takes over only when hunger is low with too little to last, through the
   goal's own hunt, and gives the turn back once it has enough; the goal's steps are not weighed meanwhile. Night: a
   player its goal has dug in for the night (`PortalRunCampaign.dugInForTheNight`) is not walked to a bed; the shelter
   is its night. The one-seed engine run is to confirm it from the logs (hunts and bed walks against shelters).
3. **Outside faction worlds, unchanged.** `goalOf` is the own goal outside a faction world, and life first there
   requires no goal, so the shelter check never applies; `NativePortalCampaignGameTests` and
   `NativeLifeFirstGameTests` narrowed with `NativeFactionPlayGameTests` and `NativeFactionsEvalGameTests`, 3 of 3
   (109 of 109 each run, main merged in).

The pack's README (`packs/factions/README.md`, "What the AI players do") says what AI players pursue by default.
