## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): villager trading, understanding what is valuable and how to profit from villagers. This slice trades as a player does, at the villager's own screen, by E-45a's value model.

**A look.** A villager at work (not a nitwit, a child, one asleep or one trading with someone) within 16 blocks and in sight, whose offers it has not seen, has its trading screen opened with a use click, as a player opens it, and shut: its offers as the screen shows them, at the villager's own prices (demand and the player's standing included), are what it knows of it. Nothing else is read of the villager.

**A trade.** A villager whose offers it knows and that has something worth trading for (E-45a's plan: purchases worth making that it can pay for, and the sales of what it can spare that pay for them), within 48 blocks, is walked to and traded with: its screen opened, each trade selected as a client's click on the offer selects it (which fills its slots from the inventory), what it yields taken from the result slot into the inventory one trade at a time, as many times as planned; stopping when the offer yields nothing more (out of stock until it restocks, or not paid for) or there is no room; and shut, which gives back what is left in its slots. The plan is made again from the offers it sees when it opens the screen, not from what it remembers.

**Recorded and conserved** (`NativeTradeAction`, `native_trade`). One record under the durable ledger for each look or trade, with a claim on the villager. What the inventory lost and gained is exactly what the trades it made ask and yield, item by item, and what the trades left it with is in the inventory as the villager offered it, enchantments and all; otherwise the outcome is uncertain and the player is held for reconciliation. The villager's own counts of each offer move with it. The first trade locks a villager to its offers (vanilla's own rule).

**What it says.** What it traded, to those who hear it: "I sold 48 paper for 2 emeralds and bought a Mending book for 2 emeralds and a book." A purchase worth making that it cannot yet pay for, or that the villager is out of, once for each villager: "The librarian here sells a Mending book for 2 emeralds and a book: worth it, once it has restocked."

**Let be.** A villager with nothing worth trading is let be until it has restocked (half a day); one it could not reach, a minute; one it has traded with, two minutes, then looked at again, its offers having moved on. Never in a match.

**Found alongside and fixed** (E-43). A walk to look at a store of its own where it saw someone, once blocked, cleared what it had seen, so it never looked: a look, or the rest moved out of a store it no longer trusts, now waits ten seconds and tries again, what it saw kept for five minutes.
