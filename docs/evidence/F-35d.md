## Scope and implementation

The walking navigator and the route planner that digs, bridges, pillars and builds stairs lived in the server adapter
and read `BlockState`s directly, so the headless simulator (`companion-sim`, core only) could not plan with them. This
change moves both into core, unchanged in what they decide, behind an interface of per-cell facts.

- `companion-core`, new package `dev.kindred.core.route`:
  - `Terrain`: per-cell facts over integer x, y, z, with no Minecraft types: `loaded`, `fluid` (`NONE`, `WATER`,
    `LAVA`), `harms`, `passThrough` (no collision or a top at most 0.2 high, or a hand-opened door when doors count),
    `supports` (a top from 0.8 to 1.0), `bears` (a top of 0.8 or more, taller ones included: the route planner's
    footing), `scorches` (magma or fire), `bottomHalf` (a bottom slab or stair), `portal`, `replaceable`, `hardness`,
    `placeAgainst` (what a placed block can go against, as `NativeStairAction.solid` has it).
  - `Cell` (an int position record, its key packed as the game packs a block position), `Side` (the six faces in the
    game's order, the four round the compass in its horizontal order).
  - `Navigator`: the port of `NativeNavigator`'s logic: the walk, step-up, drop, swim and gap-jump moves, `find`,
    the low stands, terrain and ground costs, the safe stand and the clutch and water-drop limits, with the same
    constants. Mob dangers are plain doubles and `MobSenses.Profile`; the shun cost and the ending test take cells.
  - `RoutePlanner`: the port of `NativeRoutePlanner` (walk, dig, bridge, pillar, stair), its allowance the caller's
    `diggable`, `setAside` and `blocksHeld`.
  - What a search works out is remembered per search as before (which cells pass and stand, each cell's cost, the
    allowance's dig answers), in a small open-addressed table of its own, as core has no fastutil.
- `companion-server-adapter`: `NativeNavigator` and `NativeRoutePlanner` keep their names and every method and type
  their callers use. They are thin wrappers now: `NativeNavigator` reads the live world as a `Terrain` (the block and
  chunk caches stay there, and the doors flag), converts block positions to cells and back, and hands every decision
  to the core. `NativeNavigator.hazard` is kept as it was behind `harms`; a later change asks the capabilities instead.
- The search order is the original's throughout: the eight horizontal offsets in the same order, the compass and
  six-face orders where the original used `Direction`, the same `PriorityQueue` comparator and insertion order.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Step 3 is not done: `SimWorld` implementing `Terrain` so the simulator plans with `Navigator` and `RoutePlanner`, and
the corpus gate on its routes. `Terrain.fluid` answers only none, water or lava. The old code asked "no fluid at all" in a few places (dry ground, a
cell to dig or to place in), which a third kind of fluid would have failed; neither the pinned game nor its pinned
mods have one, so nothing differs, but a mod that added one would see it counted as dry there. No climbables yet. Open on the branch, not from this change: the pit fixture above fails before it as after it.
