## Scope and implementation

The walking navigator and the route planner that digs, bridges, pillars and builds stairs lived in the server adapter
and read `BlockState`s directly, so the headless simulator (`companion-sim`, core only) could not plan with them. This
change moves both into core, unchanged in what they decide, behind an interface of per-cell facts.

- `companion-core`, new package `dev.kindred.core.route`:
  - `Terrain`: per-cell facts over integer x, y, z, with no Minecraft types: `loaded`, `fluid` (`NONE`, `WATER`,
    `LAVA`), `harms`, `passThrough` (no collision or a top at most 0.2 high, or a hand-opened door when doors count),
    `supports` (a top from 0.8 to 1.0), `bears` (a top of 0.8 or more, taller ones included: the route planner's
    footing), `scorches` (magma or fire), `bottomHalf` (a bottom slab or stair), `portal`, `replaceable`, `hardness`,
    `placeAgainst` (what a placed block can go against, as `NativeStairAction.solid` has it).
  - `Cell` (an int position record, its key packed as the game packs a block position), `Side` (the six faces in the
    game's order, the four round the compass in its horizontal order).
  - `Navigator`: the port of `NativeNavigator`'s logic: the walk, step-up, drop, swim and gap-jump moves, `find`,
    the low stands, terrain and ground costs, the safe stand and the clutch and water-drop limits, with the same
    constants. Mob dangers are plain doubles and `MobSenses.Profile`; the shun cost and the ending test take cells.
  - `RoutePlanner`: the port of `NativeRoutePlanner` (walk, dig, bridge, pillar, stair), its allowance the caller's
    `diggable`, `setAside` and `blocksHeld`.
  - What a search works out is remembered per search as before (which cells pass and stand, each cell's cost, the
    allowance's dig answers), in a small open-addressed table of its own, as core has no fastutil.
- `companion-server-adapter`: `NativeNavigator` and `NativeRoutePlanner` keep their names and every method and type
  their callers use. They are thin wrappers now: `NativeNavigator` reads the live world as a `Terrain` (the block and
  chunk caches stay there, and the doors flag), converts block positions to cells and back, and hands every decision
  to the core. `NativeNavigator.hazard` is kept as it was behind `harms`; a later change asks the capabilities instead.
- The search order is the original's throughout: the eight horizontal offsets in the same order, the compass and
  six-face orders where the original used `Direction`, the same `PriorityQueue` comparator and insertion order.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Step 3 is done (below); the corpus gate on the simulator's routes remains. `Terrain.fluid` answers only none, water or lava. The old code asked "no fluid at all" in a few places (dry ground, a
cell to dig or to place in), which a third kind of fluid would have failed; neither the pinned game nor its pinned
mods have one, so nothing differs, but a mod that added one would see it counted as dry there. No climbables yet. Open on the branch, not from this change: the pit fixture above fails before it as after it.

## Step 3: the simulator plans with the core's planners

Date: 2026-11-10. Branch `claude/movement-1110-5`. Pure tests and a headless simulator scene only: no native fixture or
live run is claimed for this step, and nothing in `companion-server-adapter` changed.

### Scope and implementation

- `companion-sim` `SimWorld` implements `Terrain`. Each fact is answered as the adapter's `WorldTerrain` reads it from
  the matching vanilla 1.21.1 block's state:

  | Simulator block | passThrough | supports, bears, placeAgainst | replaceable | fluid | harms | hardness |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | AIR | yes | no | yes | none | no | 0 |
  | TORCH | yes (no collision) | no | no (vanilla's torch is not replaceable) | none | no | 0 |
  | WATER | yes (no collision box) | no | yes | water | no | 100 |
  | LAVA | yes (refused by the navigator as lava and as a hazard) | no | yes | lava | yes | 100 |
  | STONE, DIRT, GRASS, LOG, LEAVES, the three ores, GRAVEL, PLANKS, COBBLE, BEDROCK, OBSIDIAN, TABLE, FURNACE, SAND | no | yes (full cubes) | no | none | no | vanilla's: 1.5, 0.5, 0.6, 2, 0.2, 3, 0.6, 2, 2, -1, 50, 2.5, 3.5, 0.5 |

  `scorches`, `bottomHalf` and `portal` are false for every block: the simulator has no magma, fire, slab, stair or
  portal block. `loaded` is the 128 square and the heights -64 to 127; outside it `fluid` is none and `placeAgainst`
  false. Leaves support a route as the adapter's do: vanilla's leaves have a full collision box, and the adapter reads
  the box. (The simulator's own `Block.solid()`, which its skills and mobs use, still leaves leaves out, as before.)
  `SimWorld.staged(seed)` is an empty world for a staged scene.
- `SimRun.walkTo` plans with the core:
  - Every walk is `Navigator.find` from the feet's cell with the goto's own budget, radius and range:
    `DEFAULT_MAX_NODES` (6,000), radius 96, within 1.5 of the target (`NativeGoToSkill`'s default).
  - Where the campaign makes a way, a walk that finds none then tries `RoutePlanner.find` with `buildRoute`'s budget and
    radius: `ROUTE_NODES` (2,500), radius 48. Its allowance is the campaign's: dig what the pickaxe held harvests, with
    no liquid near it (the campaign's `liquidNear`, ported); place up to the dirt and cobblestone held. These walks are
    the approach to a block to mine or fill (`reach`) and the walk back to the cast frame.
  - The campaign's partial walks (`go(..., 3, true, ...)`: an explore over the ground, the watchdog's move elsewhere)
    go as far as the navigator's unfinished route does, as the goto's `partial` does.
  - No route, or one that does not reach on a walk that is not partial, is BLOCKED with the goto's words, "No walkable
    route toward x, y, z". "Route to x, y, z is blocked" is said only if a made way runs out of blocks to place: no
    replan happens mid-walk in the simulator, which is where the goto says it.
  - The body then moves along the route's cells a leg at a time, with the old pacing: 4.63 ticks a block walking,
    3.56 sprinting, two a block up or down, and each block dug for its break time by the pickaxe held. Only the route's
    own cells are dug (each block's drop in hand); its bridge, pillar and stair cells are placed from the dirt, then
    cobblestone, held, 5 ticks each. A hit cuts the walk short where it has got to, as before.
  - The straight two-high tunnel is kept, as `tunnelTo`, only where tunnelling is the action: the explore for an ore not
    in view below ground, which stands in for the campaign's dig down and branch mine.

After, the last task before the spell ended "BLOCKED: No walkable route toward" in 1,838 of the 1,869 spells (portal
1,456, caving 334, night 48). By where the target was against the feet, the refused walks after (blocks in all three

- To mine (`native_break`): 6,700 targets below, 4,069 above, 81 at the same level.
- To gather wood (`native_gather`): 959, all above.
- To fill a bucket: 406, all above.
- To walk (`native_goto`), mostly the walk to a cave in view for ore: 1,267 at the same level, 73 above, 65 below.

The cause is the planners' own reach, now that the simulator uses them:
- `RoutePlanner` digs only at the body's own level. Its DIG clears the cell ahead and the one over it.
- It has no step that digs a staircase up or down through rock. Its STAIR places a block in open air, and its PILLAR
  needs open headroom.
- So from a two-high tunnel no route goes up or down, unless there is a cave to walk or a drop of three or less.
- Ore, logs and water seen above or below the tunnel are out of reach, and the simulator asks for the same nearest one
  again each time its 600-tick aside ends.
- In the adapter, the campaign climbs and digs down with its own skills (`climb`, `digDown`). It also gives a target
  up after failed approaches (`avoid`, `unreachable`), which the simulator does not.
- In the portal pack, 18 starved, stuck below ground with no way up to hunt.

None of this is a change to what the planners decide. It is the first view the simulator gives of them.

### Remaining (step 3's handoff)

- The corpus gate on the simulator's routes.
- What the scene and the packs show the planners lack: a dig-up and a dig-down staircase step in `RoutePlanner`, or
  the campaign's climb and dig down in core. The simulator also lacks the campaign's giving up of an unreachable
  target, its liquid-refusing tunnel, and water that flows.
- Other gaps in the simulator's movement fidelity:
  - No gravel or sand falls.
  - Mobs do not plan routes (they walk straight and step up one).
  - No drowning or breath. A route may swim under water for as long as it likes.
  - Swimming is paced as walking.
  - No knockback off a route.
  - No doors, slabs, stairs, fences, magma or fire, so those facts are never exercised.
  - The world does not change under a walk, so the goto's mid-walk replans are not exercised.
