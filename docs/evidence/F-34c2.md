## Scope and implementation

A raid on a faction's claim can be told to the faction only from a recorded fact. `TerritoryGuard` refused but wrote
nothing. Session A agreed to `territory.refused` on five conditions (2026-11-15):
1. only real attempts;
2. off the tick;
3. only land a faction owns;
4. the bound as its own child (#330);
5. a native fixture with controls.

The lead added two evidence points:
- the refusal path costs the server thread nothing but a lookup and a queued write;
- a griefer at the cap writes no more than the limits allow.

**The change:**
- `TerritoryGuard.refused(actor, world, pos, what)` (new) is called only from the branches that refuse an act. It is
  never called from `mayChange`, `denial` or any planning query; none of those changed. The call sites:
  - `KindredMod`'s break (`BEFORE`, "break");
  - the dig start (`AttackBlockCallback`, "break");
  - placing or using a block (`UseBlockCallback`): "place" with a block in hand, at the place beside, or else "use", at
    the block itself. Only the block acted on is written: opening one's own door whose face looks into another
    faction's chunk is refused as before, and writes nothing (Session A's review);
  - frames, stands and storage carts (`UseEntityCallback` and `AttackEntityCallback`, "use" and "break");
  - the explosion mixin, only when its causing entity is a player, once a chunk a blast ("explode");
  - the projectile mixin, only when its owner is a player and the shot would have changed the block ("hit"): a
    burning arrow or trident, a fireball or fire charge, a wind charge, a splash or lingering potion, or a trident with
    channeling (`TerritoryGuard.wouldChange`). A plain arrow, trident or snowball is cancelled as before but writes
    nothing, since a hunter's miss over a border is no attempt on the land (Session A's review).
- `FactionsService.refused` writes `territory.refused`:
  - actor: who tried. subject: the owning faction. place: the chunk. `data.what`: what was tried.
  - Only on claimed land, never a safe zone or the wilderness.
  - Through the service's `record` consumer, the runtime's queue onto the ordered writer.
- `RefusalLimit` (core, new) holds the limits in memory:
  - once a minute for each actor and chunk;
  - 100 a game day for each owning faction;
  - a repeat within the minute dropped before any ledger is read. An attempt over a faction's cap is kept for the
    minute too, so its repeats take the same one-lookup path (Session A's review).
- The cost line adds `territory refusals N written, M the same minute's and K over a faction's daily cap, only counted`.
- `TerritoryRefusals.WHAT` gains "use".
- The operator guide describes what is written.

Not body behaviour: what a refusal the guard already makes writes down.

The fixture's first AI attempt was a bare `tryBreakBlock` on the AI body. `NativePlayerBody.allowBreak` refuses that
before any territory handler, since it lets only the body's own mining target go. So it wrote nothing. The fixture
now starts the dig as the body's mining does (`START_DESTROY_BLOCK`). The first measure, about 6 to 11 µs a call, read
the ledgers before the limit; the repeat check now comes first.
