# E-41c — Opponent tendencies and short prediction (Milestone 2)

Date: 2026-10-29
Agent / branch: Claude / claude/modest-faraday-r3spro
Build: 6db4b3b, 856cfe8, a1f044f and the commit after it (each run below names its build; "final" is the commit this evidence came with)
Status: in progress. Both parts are done and benchmarked against a control with each off. On the final build neither shows a measurable gain: the turtle and the spammer are beaten every time either way, and a runner is caught as often and as soon. On earlier builds each did (against the turtle, 52 of 60 against 33; a runner caught in 1207 ticks against 1932), and each gain went once the melee and the flee themselves were fixed. Remaining: an opponent whose habits change as it plays, and arenas where a lead matters.

## Scope and implementation

- **Tendencies** (`Tendencies`, core, new). What an opponent did when it had the chance, counted only over its chances:
  - whether its shield was up when the player was near it while its own swing recovered, past a shield's five ticks (or it was never seen to swing), once a swing: one that shields only while its swing recovers is a blocker as much as one that never lowers it;
  - whether it swung before its own swing had recovered.
  Each count decays with a half-life of twelve chances, so it follows an opponent who changes. A confidence grows with the chances seen, so a guess from two chances weighs little. What the melee uses is the rate drawn toward a half by how unsure it is. They are kept per opponent across the player's fights with it (`PlayerRuntime`), and forgotten with the control's switch.
- **What they change.**
  - Against one known to raise its shield as the swing comes full, the strike scores higher, so it is taken before the shield is up.
  - Against one known to swing uncharged, the shield is not raised for every weak swing; it strafes instead.
  - Against one it has learned keeps its shield up, it takes up an axe, which disables a raised shield. The change costs a swing's recovery, so it is made once.
- **Short prediction** (`Tendencies.predict`). Where the opponent will be is worked out from how far it was seen to move each tick, not from a velocity the server may not know for a client's player. It is held to that pace, at most ten ticks ahead, and closing in leads to it.
- **Controls** (`PlayerRuntime.meleeTendencies`, `meleePrediction`): each part off on its own, for the same benchmark.

Environment: Linux, OpenJDK 21, Fabric 1.21.1 game tests on a game-test server, each benchmark alone; pure tests with the JUnit console launcher.

What the turtle runs found, each fixed in turn:
- **A worn-out shield stopped the benchmark** (t1, t2). The turtle's long fights wore the player's shield through; the fight counted it a loss and blocked for reconciliation. A shield, armour or weapon the game counts broken is now wear.
- **The habit was never seen** (t3 to t9). The chance was first taken as the player's swing came full in reach, when the turtle's shield is down to strike; then on a window that, for an iron sword, held no tick. It is now taken once a swing, over the whole time that swing recovers.
- **A hit knocked the turtle's shield down before it guarded**, so a shield that only counted once it guarded was seen up a third of the time (t10). What is counted is the shield raised, as a player sees it come up.
- **The shield came up late** (t9). A move in hand was kept up to five ticks, so the turtle's swing landed while the player still walked in or strafed. The shield is now taken at once, as the strike is; having given up on a swing that did not come, it waits its turn again for a while. A jump for a critical gives way to the shield against an opponent that does swing.
- **The turtle ran from its own duel** (t3, t4): hit hard by a critical, it judged itself outclassed by the hardest hit it had taken and ran for a way out past the arena's wall. In a duel the kit alone is judged now, and a player with no way it can walk away fights on (E-41b).

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- **An opponent whose habits change as it plays.** The tendencies' decay is built for that; no declared opponent does it yet.
- **Benchmarks where prediction can matter.** Open ground; a runner that turns; ranged attacks.
- **The remaining habits.** Getting away when hurt, and strafing, are counted in core but not yet from the world.
