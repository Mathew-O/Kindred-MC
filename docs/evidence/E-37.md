# E-37 — Native taming by an AI player (2026-10-29)

Agent / branch: Claude / claude/modest-faraday-r3spro
Build: 56c022f
Status: in progress. A wolf is tamed with the player's own bones, under the policy's caps and with pets on. Remaining: cats and parrots, refusal fixtures (a player-owned, penned or farm animal), a stop during the approach, restart and death, and pets kept on over a restart.

## Scope and implementation

- **`/aiplayer pets on|off`.** An operator's switch, off by default; it is not yet kept over a restart.
- **The choice** (`PlayerRuntime.petCandidates`). Between its other work, an AI player looks for a wild wolf within 16 blocks: in sight, grown, not angry. It must hold bones of its own. Then the pet policy (`PetPolicy`, above) decides from the facts gathered just before:
  - pets on;
  - under two pets, and one wolf;
  - bones to hand;
  - the wolf wild and in sight.
  If eligible, it brings bones to its hotbar if they are in its pack, walks to the wolf, and gives it a bone.
- **The click** (`NativePetAction`, new; skill `native_pet`, mode `tame`). It works as a player's use click: vanilla takes the bone and tames the wolf to the player one time in three. Exactly one bone is spent under the durable ledger, and nothing else of the player's is touched. Whether the wolf took to it is the game's: the action reports it, and the next bone comes from the next choice. A wolf that is not wild, or that is another player's, is never clicked.
- **Said once** (`PlayerRuntime.petCandidates`): "The wolf has taken to me."
