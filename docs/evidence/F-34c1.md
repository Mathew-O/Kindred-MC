## Scope and implementation

F-34c's faction news on the Wire, one kind at a time (the lead's order, 2026-11-14): first a member's death, the
clearest verified fact.

**The change** (`PlayerRuntime.recordDeath`, the one place every death is written, AI players' and people's):
- In a faction world, when the one who died is in a faction, its `death` event is written and then told
  (`recordAndTell`). The message is the server's, `faction.member_died`, scoped to that faction alone, and it cites the
  death's event id.
- Its facts, both faction-only:
  - `who`: an AI player's name. A person is "A member": nothing records a person's consent to be named, so none is.
  - `cause`: the damage type as the game gives it (`generic`, `fall`, `player`, …).
- Never the killer or where it fell.
- Anyone else's death, or any death outside a faction world, is only written, as before.
- The line for a person: `[faction] Ada died: generic`, through `Wording`, within each person's listener limits like
  every Wire line.

Not body behaviour: what is told of a death the Record already writes.

Changed modules/files:
- companion-server-adapter: `PlayerRuntime` (`recordDeath`, `memberDiedMessage`, `MEMBER_DIED`, its wording).
- gametest: `NativeFactionDeathGameTests` (new, three fixtures), registered in `fabric.mod.json`.
- companion-server-adapter also: `PlayerRuntime.factionsWorldOffForTest` (test-only, the reverse of `factionsWorldForTest`).
- docs: this file, the tracker.

On the first runs the person's death fixture failed in its own wiring, twice:
- The mod's death hook (`KindredMod`, `AFTER_DEATH`) reaches the server's runtime, not a fixture's. The fixture now
  hands the person's death to its runtime as that hook does.
- The fixture read the inbox oldest first, but `inbox(ai, kind)` is newest first. Its indexing was corrected.

Nothing in the change itself moved.

**Which path each fixture covers** (the lead's condition, 2026-11-14):
- An AI player's death goes through its runtime's own body hook, `body.onDeath` to `onNativeDeath` to `recordDeath`, as in play. Nothing is handed in.
- The first fixture's person is a staged handoff. The mod's hook reaches the server's runtime, not the fixture's, so the
  fixture calls `playerDied` itself.
- The third fixture closes that gap: a person's real death on the server's own runtime, through `KindredMod`'s
  `AFTER_DEATH`, to the faction channel.
