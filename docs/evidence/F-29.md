## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: with the strategist on, a player that is stuck, its own move elsewhere tried and failed, asks
the strategist for a way out, at most once for each stuck spell and five minutes apart. It sends what it can see round
it, what it carries, what it was doing and what it tried, about 800 tokens. The reply is a few steps from a fixed list:
walk, dig, pillar, place a block, wait, eat, back off. Each step has small bounded numbers. The game checks the
steps, then the player carries them out a step at a time as ordinary moves, each checked again as it is made. The
first step that does not go ends the plan. The log says `rescue: pillar 3, walk north 2 -> unstuck`, or `-> failed
<why>`, or `-> refused: <why>` when the reply was turned down with nothing moved. `KINDRED_RESCUE=off` turns it off.

| `dig <north\|south\|east\|west\|up\|down> <n>` | 1 to 4 | `native_break`, each block in the way: two high sideways, one up or down | as designed |

## Plans refused as stale (1 November)

With the compatible route fixed, the arm's first two seeds asked for 4 ways out: 3 came back refused, "the proposal's
revisions are stale; it is no longer where it asked from", and the fourth ran and failed at its walk. A plan's fence
compares the arbiter's intent revision, and that moves on with every command the arbiter takes. The goal's own tasks
are given out as the console's commands (`Actor.console()`, so `ActionArbiter.acquire`, not `acquireAutonomous`), and
the goal worked on while the plan was asked for: any task it gave out in those seconds made the plan stale, and its
walks took the player from where it asked. The pit fixture never showed it, as its player had nothing left to try. The
claim in this evidence that the intent revision stood still while the goal worked was wrong.

The change: while a way out is asked for, the goal gives out nothing, unless something about the player is a danger
(a hostile mob within 16 blocks, water, lava, fire, its breath short, its health or food at half or less). A walk of
its own already under way stops where it is, and a task the goal chose in the same tick, just before the bridge
asked, is not given out. The bridge tells the campaign as it asks and again when the reply has come or failed; the
reply takes seconds. The fence is as strict as before: a task given out meanwhile, by the goal in danger or by a
person, still refuses the plan. Holding still from the ask alone was not enough: in the new fixture the watchdog's walk
elsewhere, under way when the ask went out, carried the player off, "refused: it is no longer where it asked from", in
2 of 2 runs of the class. The arm's
seeds before the change are kept apart (`e49-rescue-stale`, not filed), and the arm was run again with it.

## Plans still refused as stale, and the steps as they are carried out (1 November)

The arm run again with the hold, `e49-rescue` (the E-49 build, `39af678`, with F-26's spawn fix and the two changes of
this day above), showed two faults in its first six seeds: of 29 ways out asked for, 14 came back refused before
anything moved, 12 of them as stale; of the 15 plans carried out, 4 got the player out and 11 stopped at a step.

Why stale. Every task the player starts begins on a direct lease: the runtime dispatches its own choices with
`Actor.console()`, so `ActionArbiter.acquire`, which moves the intent revision the fence compared. The hold stops only
the goal's own tasks, and only while the player is calm. Read from the consoles, between each stale ask and its reply
the player had started something of its own: its needs' eat, or a discard of surplus cobblestone, while held; a task
the goal chose before the ask, begun after it; or, not calm (most often a hostile within 16 blocks, underground), the
goal's own mining or walking. The replies took one to two seconds.

The change: the runtime counts the tasks the player starts of its own choosing (`ownStarts`, at the one place its own
choices are dispatched), and the rescue's fence compares the intent revision less that count (`commanded`). A stop, a
resume, a mode change or a person's command still makes a plan stale; a plan is still refused when the player has moved
more than two blocks or the stuck spell is over. The hold stays: a walk would take the player from where it asked.
Every other place the runner is started is a person's command (a movement skill, a checkpoint continued).

Why the steps stopped. Of the 11, 7 stopped at a walk the route planner found no way for ("No walkable route"), 4 of
them just after a `dig up` the plan seemed to take for a climb (`dig up 2, walk north 2`); 3 at a dig with no pickaxe
("No owned tool can harvest minecraft:cobbled_deepslate"); 1 at a block out of reach. The prompt said "dig breaks a passage two high
sideways, or a shaft up or down", and nothing of what moves the player or what a dig needs. It now says what each step
does as it is carried out: a walk goes at about the same height, climbing one block at a time; a dig does not move the
player; `dig up` does not lift it (a `pillar` climbs the shaft); `dig down` drops it into the hole; it breaks only what
a tool it carries can harvest, and stone, deepslate and ores need a pickaxe; a pillar needs blocks in its hotbar and air
over its head. No test reads the prompt's words: the final build's arm is where the change is measured.

The whole arm, ten seeds, every run complete and earned ([summary](eval/e49-rescue/summary.md), [against
e49-base](eval/compare-rescue.md)): 46 ways out asked for; 27 refused before anything moved, 25 of them as stale and 2
as moved off; 19 plans carried out, 5 getting the player out, 14 stopping at a step (9 at a walk, 4 at a dig with no
pickaxe, 1 out of reach). Against `e49-base`, the same build with the strategist off, no measure differs clearly; stuck
game minutes 37.5 [15 to 84] with rescues against 28.5 [9 to 54] without. The rescues used 85,001 tokens over the ten
seeds, about 1,850 an ask, almost all of them input: about a cent at the price listed for the model that ran them
($0.10 a million in, $0.50 out, for prompts under 100,000 tokens).

## The compatible route's thinking (1 November)

The release jar does not carry the Anthropic SDK, so the rescue arm asked Anthropic's server through the compatible
route (`KINDRED_STRATEGIST_PROVIDER=openai-compatible`, its `/v1` base URL). Every rescue of its first two seeds came
back "no plan (stopped: length)". The model it named thinks unless told not to, and the thinking counts against the
reply's bound: a rescue's 300 tokens were spent before a word of the plan. The SDK route asks for no thinking on every
rescue, ask and chat line, and on advice unless `KINDRED_STRATEGIST_THINKING=on`; the compatible route asked for
nothing. Now every request it sends to Anthropic's server asks for no thinking. Other servers are not sent the field,
as some refuse one they do not know. The arm was stopped and run again on a jar with the change (below).

## ModelGateway's accounting retired

`ModelGateway`'s request admission, per-lane concurrency, rates, daily budgets and returned-plan checks are removed. They
duplicated `StrategistBridge`'s, which is what every request goes through, and nothing called them. The tests for them
went with them: `ModelGatewayTest` (8), `SupportBatch02ModelGatewayTest` (6) and `SupportBatch03GatewayRegressionTest`
(2), 16 in all. Their gate checks are covered in core by `SupportBatch04SkillProposalTest` and `RescuePlanTest`. Its
`Lane` (the chat and planning lanes' deadlines and reservations) stays, for `ReasoningPolicy`. F-19's row says so.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- Most plans that run stop at a step whose geometry the model got wrong (a dig of one block, then a walk of two; a
  `dig up`, then a walk; a pillar under a ceiling). A check of each walk's route and each pillar's head room when the plan
  comes, before anything moves, would turn those into refusals with nothing moved; the report could show the cells each
  step needs.
- A step is checked as every task is when it is given out, and the plan stops at the first that does not go. With
  edits switched off on the server the goal pauses whole, and a plan waits with it; a step given out that never ends
  ends the plan after a game minute.
- The design's `bridge` and `climb_out` are not in the vocabulary, and `wait` is 1 to 4 seconds (above).
- The fence compares the session and the intent revision less the tasks the player started of its own choosing, not the
  task revision: a stop, a resume or a command refuses a plan, its own eating or digging meanwhile does not. The goal
  gives out nothing while a way out is asked for, unless the player is in danger, and the player must still be where it
  asked from, in the same stuck spell.
- Updated docs: [operator guide](../operator-guide.md) ("Rescue plans (F-29)"), `tools/eval/README.md`.
