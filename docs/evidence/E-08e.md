## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: a player building never steps off a drop it could not climb back up, to break or place a block from below. Before this, any spot it could walk to counted. A drop of up to three blocks is walkable one way, so a player could go down off the edge of its ground to take a scaffold down and be stranded there.

Found in this session's ten runs of `NativeLifeFirstGameTests` (run e21b-life5): the hungry fixture failed with its cabin unfinished.
1. Its base stood on its field's last row. Beyond it the fixture's ground is three blocks lower.
2. Taking the cabin's scaffold down, the planner sent it down off that edge to break a scaffold block from below.
3. From there the last scaffold block was out of reach, and the way back up was a three-block wall.
4. It waited ("Scaffolding … is out of reach to take down") until night. Then the walk to its bed found no route, and the fixture ended with the cabin unfinished.

Changed modules/files:
- `ConstructionPlanner` (core):
  - The spots it may stand at are what it can walk to, as before, intersected with `returnable`: the spots from which it can walk back to the work.
  - `returnable` floods back from where it stands, the build's footprint and the block of ground round it, over the same steps taken the other way: a step down, a level step, a climb of one, or up a scaffold column it descended.
  - Spots on the build or that ring always count, so stepping off a scaffold onto the ground beside is unchanged.
  - `walkable`'s single step is now `stepTo`, used by both directions, with the same rules as before.
  - `ConstructionPlanner` is not a file `modest-faraday` changes.
- `ConstructionPlannerTest`: one case, `neverABreakFromWhereItCouldNotWalkBackToTheBuild`.
