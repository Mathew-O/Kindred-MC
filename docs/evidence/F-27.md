## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: with `KINDRED_REPLAYS=on` (on by default in an evaluation run, off in play), each player's death
is kept as a copy of the ground round it, 33 by 17 by 33 blocks with the mobs in it, and of its state ten seconds
before. A copy put into the gametest resources becomes a test the suite finds on its own and plays three times by the
real runtime. Nothing in the world or the player changes when a copy is taken.

Changed modules/files:
- `companion-server-adapter`:
  - `DeathReplays`: the capture. On the server thread, each block's state is read in the box, with the data of the
    few blocks that have any, and the mobs' saved data. The state is copied as plain values and item copies. The
    structure file is built from those copies off the thread, in the layout vanilla's `StructureTemplate.writeNbt`
    writes (blocks, palette, entities, size, data version). The `.json` is written off the thread too. It holds:
    - the inventory with its components, the selected slot and the effects;
    - health, food, saturation, air and absorption ten seconds before death (a note of each player's vitals once a
      second; at the moment of death its health is none);
    - position, yaw and pitch, dimension, time of day, weather and difficulty;
    - goal, stage, task, stance, habits and the cause;
    - its own last twenty decision lines from the log.
    One capture a minute at most for each player.
  - `KindredMod`: `KINDRED_REPLAYS` read, the replays put under `kindred/replays/<world>/`.
  - `PlayerRuntime`: the vitals noted once a second; the capture called as a native player dies, before its work stops
    and vanilla drops what it holds.
  - `PortalRunCampaign.stopFor`: the fix the first replay's log showed (below).
  - `PortalRunCampaign.surface`, `airThroughTheWater`: the drowning replay's fix (below).
- gametest:
  - `ReplayGameTests`: the generator, and a fixture for the capture's time and read-back.
  - The replay `echo-50149`: `data/kindred_gametest/gametest/replays/echo-50149.json` and
    `structure/replays/echo-50149.snbt`.
  - `NativePortalCampaignGameTests`: one fixture for the fix.
  - `NativeEvalGameTests`: a failure is raised as a `GameTestException` (a plain `AssertionError` inside a timed step
    stops the test server).
- `tools/eval/run.py`: keeps a seed's replays beside its result.
- No new dependency; the manifest is unchanged.

Schema/configuration changes: the `KINDRED_REPLAYS` environment setting; the replay files. Nothing saved by the game
or the runtime changes.

## What the design said existed, checked against the code

| Claim | Found |
| --- | --- |
| A death is a log line and a `DeathRecord` (tick, x y z, cause, stage) | As said: `ModelContextStore.DeathRecord`, kept only while the strategist's bridge runs |
| GameTests load a `.snbt` template from the gametest datapack and build the scene by hand | As said (`data/kindred_gametest/gametest/structure/`) |
| The generator is yarn's `@CustomTestProvider`, vanilla's `GameTestGenerator` (to be checked against 1.21.1's mappings) | Verified in yarn 1.21.1: `net.minecraft.test.CustomTestProvider`. `TestFunctions.register(Method)` passes an annotated method to `getCustomTestFunctions`, which makes an instance of its class and calls it. Fabric's gametest API registers each `fabric-gametest` entrypoint class through `TestFunctions.register(Class)`, so the method in `ReplayGameTests` is found with nothing else changed. Fabric's own mixin wraps only the `@GameTest` path, so a generated test runs exactly the function it was given |
| `StructureTemplate.saveFromWorld` builds the template on the server thread within a tick | Changed. Vanilla's took 58 to 71 ms on the server thread here with two evaluation servers on the same cores, so it is not used. It looks up a block entity for every block and tests the shape of every block. The copy reads only the block states, the data of blocks that have a block entity, and the mobs' data on the thread; the template is made from those copies off it |
| A `.json` with the player's state | As said, with one choice the design left open: health, food, saturation, air and absorption are those ten seconds before death |
| "The test creates a native player from the `.json`" | As said, with one detail. The runtime makes a native player only in one of the eight cells round the command's source: on a solid block, with room and no mob there, out of any liquid. So the replay looks for such a cell near the death, anywhere in the scene or on the test world's ground beside it, and makes the player from the cell beside that. Then it puts the player where it died, with the yaw, pitch and state. The drowning's scene has no dry cell at all. The personality is the fixtures' own, not the run's |
| Each replay three times in its batch | As said: batch `replay_<id>`, tests `replaygametests.<id>_1` to `_3` |
| "The template's palette keeps most captures under 200 KB" | Not so. The seven deaths captured on seed 8675309 wrote 575 to 853 KB each; a cave is mostly stone, not air. The drowning's copy, under a lake, was 1,041,894 bytes, at the 1 MB bound. The capture now writes vanilla's text form with each line's indent left off (whitespace only), and the committed copy is the same file so stripped: 893,698 bytes. The fixture's capture on open ground writes 274 to 338 KB |

The fix: `stopFor` broke off any fight below six health that it could not finish in two swings. `defend` fights
anyway when a mob on foot is in reach and there is no way off from it; its comment says "with no way off from one that
is, it is fought". So the goal chose a fight and its own stop rule ended it the next tick, again and again, while the
mob struck. Now the stop rule keeps that fight (`cornered`: a mob on foot in reach, and no back-off to take).

The drowning's fix: `surface`, the plan's way out of deep water, looked for air only straight up, sixteen blocks. Echo's
shaft bends two blocks up into the lake, so it found rock overhead, no dry ground in reach, and fell back to digging a
step out of the shaft's wall. Under water a pickaxe works at a fifth of its pace, afloat at a twenty-fifth. The stop rule
then cut each step for breath, so nothing was left to do but start again. Now, head under with rock straight above,
`airThroughTheWater` searches outward through the water alone for the nearest cell with open air above it, and the
player swims there ("Get out of the water: up for air"), round the bend. It searches at most 4,000 cells, 24 blocks
aside, 48 up and 4 down. In the replay the scene's water ends eight blocks above the shaft, under the test world's open
air, where the lake in the run went on; the swim up through the bend is what the fix changes, and in the run the lake's
surface was farther.

Environment: Linux x64 cloud container (4 cores, 15 GB), OpenJDK 21, Minecraft 1.21.1, Fabric Loader 0.19.5, Fabric API
0.116.17+1.21.1, yarn 1.21.1+build.3. Two evaluation servers (F-26's comparison) ran on the same cores throughout.
Commands executed: the gametest entrypoints narrowed to `ReplayGameTests`, or to a scratch class for the fixture, and
restored after each run; the before runs with the committed `PortalRunCampaign` swapped in and put back.
Fixtures: a replay is staged by definition. The ground, its mobs and the player's state are copied. The player is made
by the fixtures' runtime with its own personality, and no teammates are there. The fixture for the fix stages the stone
round the tunnel and a zombie whose AI is off, its target kept on the player.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- `sol-60203` stays out of the suite: it fails about half the time, fix or no fix. A brawl's replay needs a fix that
  wins the brawl, not only the one its log showed.
- A replay starts ten seconds before the death. Some deaths are already settled then: `sol-21811` (one heart, at night,
  no weapon, a spider on it) died three of three, and nothing at its start could save it.
- A brawl replay is random by nature: the same scene was won and lost in turn. A replay of a fall, lava or drowning
  would not be.
- Stuck replays (F-29) are not captured yet.
