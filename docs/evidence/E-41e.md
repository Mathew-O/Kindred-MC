## Scope and implementation

- **A policy behind one interface** (`MeleePolicy`, core). The fight asks it for the move each tick. Two policies implement it:
  - `Melee`, the hand-set one;
  - `LearnedMelee`, which runs a model with the same commitment.
  Both choose only among the moves the player can make at all: a strike only in reach, the shield only with one, eating only with food.
- **Demonstrations** (`LearnedMelee.Sample`, `MoveLabels`, core). A demonstration is what a player saw, as a `Melee.Sight`, and the move it made. The move is read from what the server sees it do each tick:
  - eating, a swing, or a raised shield;
  - otherwise how it moved against its opponent: closing; opening fast (getting away) or slowly (stepping back); or going round (strafing).
  A tick it stood and did nothing is no label. Samples are kept a line each in a demonstrations file.
- **Recording** (`PlayerRuntime.setDemonstrating`, `recordDemonstrations`). A human player turns it on for themselves (`/aiplayer combat record on`). While they play a duel, each tick's sample is taken from their side:
  - the distance and their swing's charge;
  - their health, food and shield;
  - their opponent's readiness, from when it last swung;
  - the opponent's health as they could believe it, from the blows they were seen to land;
  - the opponent's tendencies as counted for E-41c.
  Each finished duel is written off the server thread to `kindred/demonstrations/<player>/<match>.csv`. Nothing is recorded of anyone who has not turned it on.
- **Training offline** (`LearnedMelee.train`, `TrainMelee`, testkit `trainMelee`).
  - A softmax over the moves on twenty fixed features of the sight, by gradient descent with weight decay.
  - Deterministic: the same samples in the same order give the same model.
  - The last fifth of the samples is held out, and the model is scored on it.
- **Versioned, refused when damaged** (`Model.version`, `write`, `read`).
  - A model is named by its weights and the ruleset it was trained for, and kept as text under that name.
  - Read back, it is refused if its weights do not give its name (changed or damaged), or if its schema or features differ.
  - A model for another ruleset is not used.
- **A way back** (`/aiplayer combat model on|off`). An operator turns a model on from `kindred/combat/melee.model`, read off the server thread, and off again. With none on, every fight is the hand-set melee. Training never puts a model in place.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- Demonstrations from skilled human players, recorded with an ordinary client.
- A policy chosen by a weakness the benchmarks show: without shields the melee only matches the baseline (E-41b).
- A self-play curriculum, and the benchmark of a learned policy against the hand-set one.
- The model is not yet consulted for a strategist's lean (E-41f): a learned policy takes none.
