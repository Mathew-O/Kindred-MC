## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): "it should build villager stations near its own area for ones with good trades and some how get them there, via mine-cart or other methods".

**User-visible behavior.** With its trading hall standing (E-45d) and a villager it keeps (E-45c's whom to keep) within 48 blocks of it and not yet in a cell, a player brings the villager home by minecart, as players do, if it holds what that takes: the rails the line needs, a minecart, the villager's job block and a plain block it can take back. A villager that has never traded is left where it is ("moved, it would let its job go"). In order:

0. Short of rails or a minecart, it makes them from its own iron where it has enough to spare (what its run does not keep for itself): sixteen rails from six iron and a stick, sticks from planks if it has none, a minecart from five iron, at a crafting table within sixteen blocks. With too little iron for them it first goes and gets more, as its run gets its iron (`PortalRunPlanner.ironFor`, pure; carried out by the run's own steps): iron ore in sight mined, else the caves it knows walked, else down to where iron is; fuel found (coal, or wood where trees are grown again and no coal is known); a furnace made from stone, or one it holds put down; the raw iron smelted, and taken out once the furnace is done.
1. Where the line crosses water, a bridge first: a plain block put down at the water's top in each column it crosses, against the face of the bank or of the bridge so far, from the bank it is on, so that the line runs level over it. A rail line's search counts a bridge's block as one more than a rail, so a line goes round a puddle only where that costs no more.
2. The hall's door taken off for the line.
3. The line laid (`RailRoute`, core, pure): from two rails behind the villager, over firm ground with room over it or water bridged, a block up or down at most from one column to the next and never on a turn, straight in at the hall's door, along its corridor's south row, and north through a free cell's front onto its floor; the cheapest line, with fewest turns among the cheapest.
4. The minecart put on the rail two behind the villager (`native_cart`, recorded: one minecart spent, the cart then on that rail).
5. Pushed by walking into it at a walk (`native_push`): a cart is not solid to a player, and one inside its bounds shoves it away. An empty cart rolling faster than a tenth of a block a tick takes up a mob it touches, and a loaded cart rolls a long way on level rails: it takes the villager up and runs on into the cell, against its bed. Where it stops short it is pushed again from behind; stopped half in the cell's front, it is nudged on from the corridor.
6. The cell's front shut with the plain block; the cart broken through the opening above (`native_cart`, recorded: nothing lost), which sets the villager down inside, on its bed, the only room left it.
7. The plain block taken back; the player steps into the front for the cart (it drops there) and the floor's rail; steps out, and puts the job block in the front, which shuts the villager in.
8. Every rail it laid taken up again; the bridge taken back toward the hall, the block furthest from its door first, broken from the block beside it on the hall's side, so that it ends on the hall's bank; what it took up and did not pick up as it went (a rail left behind, a bridge's block that floated off) walked to and picked up; and the door put back by the build runner, as the hall's plan has it.

Off the line the cart will run along when it is about to be pushed (a villager wandered, or pushed aside), it lays the line again from where the villager now stands, four times at most: its rails off the new line taken up first (a rail takes its shape from those beside it), the cart broken, picked up where it lies and put down again behind the villager.

It says so: "I've brought the librarian home: it's in its cell in my trading hall." Given up part way (the villager gone, the cart that would not take it up, a step refused three times over), it takes back the cart and every rail and puts the door back before it says why. Under the server's AI world edits only; never in a match.

**Found alongside and fixed.** The GameTest server sets no simulation distance, so a player keeps entities moving only in the chunk it stands in: a minecart rolling out of the player's chunk stopped dead, and E-45c's villager by a chunk's edge stopped ticking when its player stepped over into the next. A rail is a sliver on the ground, and a look at its cell's middle passed over it: the body's aim now tries the middle of a block's own shape too (rails, carpets, pressure plates).
