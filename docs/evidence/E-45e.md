## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): "it should build villager stations near its own area for ones with good trades and some how get them there, via mine-cart or other methods".

**User-visible behavior.** With its trading hall standing (E-45d) and a villager it keeps (E-45c's whom to keep) within 48 blocks of it and not yet in a cell, a player brings the villager home by minecart, as players do, if it holds what that takes: the rails the line needs, a minecart, the villager's job block and a plain block it can take back. A villager that has never traded is left where it is ("moved, it would let its job go"). In order:

0. Short of rails or a minecart, it makes them from its own iron where it has enough to spare (what its run does not keep for itself): sixteen rails from six iron and a stick, sticks from planks if it has none, a minecart from five iron, at a crafting table within sixteen blocks. With too little iron for them it first goes and gets more, as its run gets its iron (`PortalRunPlanner.ironFor`, pure; carried out by the run's own steps): iron ore in sight mined, else the caves it knows walked, else down to where iron is; fuel found (coal, or wood where trees are grown again and no coal is known); a furnace made from stone, or one it holds put down; the raw iron smelted, and taken out once the furnace is done.
1. Where the line crosses water, a bridge first: a plain block put down at the water's top in each column it crosses, against the face of the bank or of the bridge so far, from the bank it is on, so that the line runs level over it. A rail line's search counts a bridge's block as one more than a rail, so a line goes round a puddle only where that costs no more.
2. The hall's door taken off for the line.
3. The line laid (`RailRoute`, core, pure): from two rails behind the villager (three for one in a boat), set out only a way that leaves that run-up and the push room behind it on level ground, over firm ground with room over it (two open over each rail, three for a villager in a boat, one more over a step's slope) or water bridged, a block up or down at most from one column to the next and never on a turn, straight in at the hall's door, along its corridor's south row, and north through a free cell's front onto its floor; the cheapest line, with fewest turns among the cheapest. It never comes back onto or beside a column it came through, nor beside its run-up, nor onto the push's spot: a rail takes its shape from the rails beside it, and in suite run 49 the cart's rail, joined to the line's return beside it, curved and turned the pushed cart off the way it was laid.
4. The minecart put on the rail two behind the villager (`native_cart`, recorded: one minecart spent, the cart then on that rail).
5. Pushed by walking into it at a walk (`native_push`): a cart is not solid to a player, and one inside its bounds shoves it away. An empty cart rolling faster than a tenth of a block a tick takes up a mob it touches, and a loaded cart rolls a long way on level rails: it takes the villager up and runs on into the cell, against its bed. Where it stops short it is pushed again from behind; stopped half in the cell's front, it is nudged on from the corridor. A cart that stops dead against the villager without taking it up (rolling faster than a fifth of a block a tick, it can pass over the gap in which it takes a mob up straight into touching it) is taken back and put down again behind it for another push, four pushes in all at most.
6. The cell's front shut with the plain block; the cart broken through the opening above (`native_cart`, recorded: nothing lost), which sets the villager down inside, on its bed, the only room left it.
7. The plain block taken back; the player steps into the front for the cart, which drops anywhere from the cell's floor to its bed: it waits there a moment, edges in to the front's far edge for one by the bed, and leaves one still out of reach where it lies, and logs it. The floor's rail is left to the cell, as players leave one (broken from the front, it once popped onto the bed, out of reach). It steps out, and puts the job block in the front, which shuts the villager in.
8. Every rail it laid but the floor's taken up again; the bridge taken back toward the hall, the block furthest from its door first, broken from the block beside it on the hall's side, so that it ends on the hall's bank; what it took up and did not pick up as it went (a rail left behind, a bridge's block that floated off) walked to and picked up; and the door put back by the build runner, as the hall's plan has it.

A villager that strolls (its AI on) walks off a line in the minutes it takes to lay, so with a boat and a tool that takes the hall's stone it is held first, as players hold one: a boat put down just beside it (on the middle of a block beside it where the boat's box, wider than a block, overlaps no wall, the villager or the player; else just clear of the villager along a side) is taken up by the boat, which takes up a mob within a fifth of a block of it; a boat it did not take up is broken, picked up and put down again, six times at most, and a boat not put down at all counts as a try. The line is then laid from where it sits. A mob let out of a vehicle cannot ride another for three seconds (vanilla's riding cooldown), so the boat is not broken for the cart: the empty cart, rolling, takes the boat up with the villager in it. Riding in a boat in the cart the villager's eyes are 2.06 blocks over the rail, not 1.87, so the line needs three open over each rail, and before it is laid the player takes out the hall's two blocks at that height over the line, the wall over the doorway (from outside) and the cell's lintel (from the corridor), each falling where it stands to be picked up. Rolled into the cell, the lintel is put back first (a villager on the shutter would otherwise walk out over it). A villager that strolls is never let loose with its cell's front open: at work it makes for its old job site, and in suite run 48 the librarian walked out through the front in the moment the player took the shutter out to step in for the cart. So the cart stays in the cell, spent as the floor's rail is: the front's rail is taken up and the job block put in across the front where it misses the boat (a lectern's foot is under the boat's overhang and its post clear of it), else the opening shut a moment against the lintel, the height left under it too low for a villager; then the boat is broken over the cart, which sets the villager down where it sat, inside the cart's box on the floor. The boat's item falls in the cell, out of reach under the cart, and is left there and logged. A cart the villager then shoves off its rail into the front is broken there and picked up from the corridor before the job block goes in. The wall over the doorway goes back with the door.

Across water it cannot bridge with what it holds (too few plain blocks, or more than 32 columns of water), one that strolls is rowed over in its boat, as players bring one (`WaterCrossing`, core, pure). Looking out from the villager along 72 straight lines, it finds the banks it can row to: over at most four columns of level ground to water with room for a boat and its riders, across water level all the way, within 64 blocks, to firm ground no higher than a block over it. Of those, the eight nearest the hall's door are tried for a line home with three open over every rail and no bridge, and the one least rowed and laid is taken. Holding the villager in its boat as above, it gets in with it (`native_boat`, `board`: a player takes a boat's front seat), rows over the ground into the water and across to the water by that bank (`native_row`, rowed out from land, a few blocks a go and four goes at most), and gets out onto the bank (`land`), the villager left in the boat on the water: let go of, it would wade or swim back to its work. A line is laid along the bank from a column beside the boat, close enough for the cart's box to meet the boat's as it passes, and the cart, rolling by, takes the boat up from the water, villager and all, and carries it home as before. Nothing is built on the water, and the lake is left as it was.

Off the line the cart will run along when it is about to be pushed (a villager wandered, or pushed aside), it lays the line again from where the villager now stands, four times at most: its rails off the new line taken up first (a rail takes its shape from those beside it), the cart broken, picked up where it lies and put down again behind the villager.

It says so: "I've brought the librarian home: it's in its cell in my trading hall." Given up part way (the villager gone, the cart that would not take it up, a step refused three times over), it takes back the cart and every rail and puts the door back before it says why. Under the server's AI world edits only; never in a match.
