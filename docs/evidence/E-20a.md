## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: with the optional strategist switched on, a player spoken to by name in chat answers in its own words. The model phrases one short, clean line in the player's personality from what the player knows right now, and is told only what this listener may know: whoever may command the player hears everything, its position included; another player hears what anyone beside it could see or ask, never where it is or what exactly it carries; an opponent in a match hears only what it could see for itself. The line goes out through the same social lane as the fixed replies, with the same cooldowns, listener limits, visibility and public-chat checks, after the typing delay the line would take (the model's own time counted in it). Whatever goes wrong (no key, the voice switched off, the budgets or rates spent, a line already being phrased, a timeout, an error, a refused line), the player says its fixed reply as before. Nothing said makes the player act: words only.

### Where the code and the design differ (code wins)

- The lane writes a line as `[Kindred] Echo: ...`, not `<Echo> ...` as the design's first GameTest has it; the log line is `<Echo> ...` as designed.
- A native player's own record is its identity (`Companion.create(id, id, ...)`), so the lane's owner check (`canTalk`) never admits a person; its controller, like anyone, is heard only while the player talks in public. That is F-18's lane, unchanged here. The tiers do not use the lane's owner: a controller (`nativeState.access()`'s controllers, else the legacy owner) is "your owner", an operator "a server administrator", and both are told everything.
- The talk range is the lane's eight blocks.
- `docs/personality-preferences.md` has no one-line gloss per trait, so `ConversationPrompt` writes its own and a sentence for the voice the traits add up to.
- The strategist's single thread would queue a reply behind a planning request of up to fifteen seconds, so each provider has a second thread for the chat lane (AI-05's separate lanes); time waiting for it counts against the eight seconds.
- A line with formatting codes (`§`) is refused, as the brief says, not stripped as the design said.
- Clean speech is checked in code too, not only asked for: a short word list refuses the line for every listener.
- Every line is checked twice: by the provider, and again by the bridge, so a provider that let a place or a command through is still refused.
- An answer that has not come within the deadline and a second's grace is taken as no line (`completeOnTimeout`), so a hung request can never leave the player silent.
- The run's recent events needed an accessor on `PortalRunCampaign` (`lastEvents`), outside the claimed files; the claim was updated with it.
- The design's GameTest 5 reads "two messages during one flight": the fixture sends a third, the first message's, before them, so that the second replaces the first in waiting. A player answers one direct message per three seconds and each speaker one per three seconds, so the messages are spaced by real time.

- The first request of a fresh client took 6.8 s, close to the eight-second deadline; a request past it is given up, and the fixed reply is said.

## Cost

A conversation is reserved at a third of its characters plus 220 tokens (about 1,000 for a typical prompt) and settled to what it used. The worst case is six a minute per player. At about 1,000 to 1,500 tokens each, nonstop chat to one player spends the steady pace's 50,000-token daily player budget in about six to eight minutes. After that the fixed replies carry on, and one log line says the budget is spent.
