## Scope and implementation

M-01b gave a base three stages and the rule for when a base moves on. This slice has a player act on that rule by itself. It chooses its next stage, picks the ground for it, and builds it as any build, fetching what it lacks through its needs (E-08c).

**The operator's leave** (`/aiplayer bases on|off`, operators only, off by default). Saved in `edits.properties` as `bases`, as the dragon fight's leave is. A base is still world edits: AI world edits must be on as well. The leave follows AGENTS.md: autonomy is subject to server policy, not to a mode toggle, and unqualified autonomy stays off until enabled.

**When a player looks to its base.** It does so with the leave, while it is playing (not just looking on, as a new player does until it is set to play), with no goal, build or ranch of its own, not stopped, in the Overworld. It looks every ten seconds.
- **A stage it began and did not finish** is taken up again where it stands. One that ended short is tried again after five minutes, three times at most; after that it is the player's base as far as it goes.
- **Otherwise the next stage** `BaseProgression` calls for (M-01b): a cabin with stone tools, a house with iron tools once the cabin's chests fill or three days pass, a manor with an enchanting table's makings. Its reason is logged and said ("A cabin to start the world from and store what it gathers: a cabin at ...").

**Where** (`BaseSite`, core, pure).
- **The nearest good ground.** The site is the nearest ground to an anchor, within 24 blocks of it, where the stage's footprint and a ring of one block round it are firm, natural ground. Water, a tree, a container and another's build all rule a site out.
- **Level enough.** The ground under it may step down one block from the highest column, no more; the floor is laid on top of the highest.
- **Room over it.** The stage's height must be open over the floor, and two cells over the ring.
- **The anchor.** The first base is anchored by the player's home in its setup, if any, else by where it stood when it first looked. Each later stage goes beside the last, three blocks clear of every base before.
- **What the player sees.** The ground is read as the player could see it: the top block that blocks movement, leaves aside. Ground it does not know is never chosen.

**Its bases are remembered** across restarts in their own small file (`kindred/memory/<player>.bases.json`): each stage, where it stands, and whether it is finished. The world says the rest: a stage taken up again is read from the blocks that stand. A record it cannot read as one of its stages (an edited file) is left out rather than stopping the player.

**What building its own base needed of E-08c's needs:**
- **Stone as the goal gets it.**
  - **Where.** It takes stone in sight within six blocks, else digs a staircase down for it, as the goal does.
  - **How deep.** The staircase goes to six below the build's floor, then a tunnel runs along at that depth. It does not go deeper each time it runs out of stone in sight.
  - **Off the site.** It first walks eight blocks off the site. It never digs the site, a block round it, or three below it.
  - **Its own stairs.** It never mines a block it has stood on going down: those are its way back up.
  - **Why.** Mining stone from the surface had pocked the ground round a tree until the player had nowhere to stand.
- **Back up to the build.** With all it went for, or going on for trees, a player below its build's floor gets back up first. It takes a route to the ground round the site that digs, bridges or pillars where walking will not do (F-10e), since its own staircase may have been mined away for stone, else it climbs out as the goal does. A way up refused for the moment (a step that did not land is set aside a while) is waited for, up to thirty seconds, rather than ending the build for want of trees it cannot see from below.
- **Over to it, on the ground.** Every build gains this.
  - **On the ground beside it.** A block it cannot reach from where it stands, and that is more than three blocks off across the ground, is walked over to two blocks outside the walls, within a block of that spot. A walk to a roof's cell had taken the player up onto the roof, where it had no stance left. A walk that could end anywhere within reach of the spot had ended inside the walls.
  - **Getting down.** Stuck up on its own build, it gets down to the ground first.
- **Its scaffold is its own.** Only the pillars it puts up for the build's own scaffold are scaffold to take down at the end, not one a route out of its digging put up. That one had been left far below, out of reach.
- **The trip ends when all is in hand**, however the last of it came in. A trip that never ended kept the climb-out from running.
- **A table set down to craft at** is recognised as the player's own, even when the build planned again while it was being set down. Otherwise it crafted a second table from the build's planks.

**The fixture.**
- **The ground:** grass on two of dirt over four of stone, so no stone is in sight; two trees of six logs 18 blocks east.
- **Mason holds:**
  - a stone pickaxe and axe;
  - the cabin's planks, stairs, slabs, door, bed, chests and torch, as though gathered;
  - six of its twelve logs and none of its stone;
  - a spare crafting table, dirt to scaffold with, and bread.
- **Set to play** in survival, with the leave and AI world edits on.

**Hand controls.** Each reverts one rule. Two of the fixes are not shown by a control: the trip ending once all is in hand (its unit test above), and the needs' table recognised by its task. Each matters only in an order of events the fixture does not always take, so neither control fails it every time.

Found on the way:
- A new player only looks on until it is set to play, so nothing happened at first. A base belongs to a player that is playing.
- Mining stone from the surface pocked the ground round the first tree until the player could stand nowhere to cut it, nor walk to the second.
- Down its staircase for stone, the player mined the stairs themselves, then could not walk back up. A climb-out was added, and the trip now ends when everything is in hand, which had kept the climb-out from running.
- A table set down to craft the furnace went unrecognised when the build planned again meanwhile. The player crafted another from four of the cabin's planks, and the cabin ended "built but for 4 oak planks".
- Between a failed first try and the next, its idle routine spent four of the cabin's planks. Building at the first go removes the gap; keeping a base's stock from routine work is for E-40a's reservations.
- Repeated runs showed more:
  - The walk off the site before digging ended within 2.5 blocks of a spot six out, still near enough to count as on the site, so it walked again and again. The spot is now eight out.
  - Out of the pit by a climb that failed one step, it found no trees from below and ended short. It now waits for a way up.
  - Sent "over to" a roof stair it could not reach, it walked onto the roof and waited there.
- The first suite runs showed more still:
  - The staircase for stone went six deeper each time stone in sight ran out, down to just above bedrock, then found no way on.
  - A pillar from the route back up was taken down as the build's own scaffold, from far below.
  - Mining the stone in sight at the bottom took its own stair steps.
  - Measured in three dimensions, a roof block over its head was always "far", so the walk over to it never ended.
- With the last of these fixes in, the fixture passed six runs in a row alone, and the house and build-needs fixtures passed again with the build runner's changes.
