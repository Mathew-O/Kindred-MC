## Scope and implementation

F-35e left three causes in the simulator for its portal pack's gap: it saw treetops through rock, its cast walked to
lava the campaign digs toward, and its world held a fraction of vanilla's iron. The lead's decision: fix the three on
the simulator's side, reusing the game's own rules, ported to core where they lived only in the adapter.

1. The game's perception, in core.
   - `companion-core` `dev.kindred.core.route.Sight` (new): the adapter's `WorldKnowledge`, ported line for line:
     - its exposure rule (a face beside air or a block that is not an opaque full cube, not under a fluid; water and lava
       only beside air; obsidian under water);
     - which kinds need a sight line (ores, fluids, cave floors, a fortress's and a stronghold's blocks, cobwebs, chests);
     - the sight line, with its second look at a fluid's surface, within the sweep's 32 and 8 more;
     - the cave floor;
     - the incremental sweep of the 5 by 3 by 5 chunk sections round the player, nearest first, 6,000 block reads and 24
       sight lines a scan, cave floors whose sight lines were spent kept for the next scans;
     - the close look, the trees on the skyline and the memory (per kind, keyed as the game keys a block, 2,000 a kind,
       stale entries dropped when asked).
     - The campaign's `trunkFoot` and its `builtIn`: a log is a tree's to chop from the ground at most five below it.
   - `SightWorld` (new): the per-cell facts it reads, over integers (and doubles for a sight line): no Minecraft types.
   - `companion-server-adapter` `WorldKnowledge` keeps its API; its rules call core, its facts answered from the live
     world as it read them before (the sight line by the game's own ray cast from the player, the blocks of the section a
     scan reads from that section). `PortalRunCampaign.trunkFoot` calls core.
   - `companion-sim` `SimWorld` implements `SightWorld` as the adapter answers for the matching vanilla block, and
     `SimRun.look` sweeps with it and remembers what it saw, as the game's knowledge does. The nearest it goes for is
     taken from that memory by the campaign's own `gatherable` (not set aside, not the block underfoot, the gather's
     radius, and for a log the trunk's foot within five below).
2. The cast's dig toward lava, in core.
   - `Digger.toward` (new), the campaign's `digToward` ported line for line; `PortalRunCampaign.digToward` calls it.
   - `SimRun.lavaForCast` (new) fetches the cast's lava as the campaign's `lavaForCast` does: walked to; a walk that
     finds no way sets the source aside and the pool is dug toward from then on, until it is in reach; then filled.
3. Ore from vanilla's generation.
   - `SimWorld.ORES`: coal, iron and diamond as vanilla 1.21.1 places them, chunk by chunk, read from the mapped game
     jar's `OrePlacedFeatures` and `OreConfiguredFeatures` (`javap -c` on `minecraft-merged-1.21.1-...yarn.1_21_1.1.21.1+build.3-v2.jar`):

     | Feature | Tries a chunk | Height | Vein size | Dropped where air touches |
     | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
     | `ore_coal_upper` | 30 | uniform 136 to the top | 17 | no |
     | `ore_coal_lower` | 20 | triangle 0 to 192 | 17 | half |
     | `ore_iron_upper` | 90 | triangle 80 to 384 (the high-mountain band) | 9 | no |
     | `ore_iron_middle` | 10 | triangle -24 to 56 (peak 16) | 9 | no |
     | `ore_iron_small` | 10 | uniform -64 to 72 | 4 | no |
     | `ore_diamond` | 7 | triangle -144 to 16 | 4 | half |
     | `ore_diamond_medium` | 2 | uniform -64 to -4 | 8 | half |
     | `ore_diamond_large` | one chunk in 9 | triangle -144 to 16 | 12 | 70% |
     | `ore_diamond_buried` | 4 | triangle -144 to 16 | 8 | all |

   - The vein's shape is the simulator's own (blobs along a short line, as vanilla's ore feature lays one). The atlas's
     priors no longer place ore: they are the player's knowledge.

Also: `SimCorpus` and `tools/sim/corpus_scenes.py` (new) play the corpus in the simulator (below).

The packs then showed where the simulator still did what the campaign does not; each was fixed to the campaign's own
rule, in the simulator only:
- Its frame's site is chosen once it holds the lava, where it stands, as the campaign's `castPortal` chooses it by the
  lava. It was chosen where it stood before it went for the lava (on the surface), and every walk back was refused.
- Water is gone for within 64, as `fillWater` asks; gravel within 48, as `flint` gathers it, and gravel it carries is
  set down and dug again until it drops flint.
- For logs and for animals, below ground the campaign's only step is its way up (`GATHER_LOGS`, `SEEK_FOOD`); with no
  step up it does nothing. The simulator walked about instead.
- Vanilla's breath, drowning, lava and burning (300 ticks of air, two points a second out of it; four points each half
  second in lava, fifteen seconds alight). Its regeneration, starvation and spawning now keep time from whatever tick a
  staged scene starts on (the same ticks as before for a run that starts on a multiple of five, as every pack does).
- The straight tunnel stand-in, kept for gravel, stops a cell short of lava, as no tunnel of the campaign's goes into
  a liquid.

### (a) The corpus in the simulator

- Loadable: a replay (not the corpus's one staged fixture), its ground captured (146 of the 225 scenes), and every block
  of the ground one the simulator knows, by the table in `tools/sim/corpus_scenes.py`. 110 load. Of the rest, 78 have no
  ground captured, 1 is the fixture, and 36 hold a block the simulator has no form of: magma 15, a fence 7, dripstone 4,
  cobwebs 2, big dripleaf 3, a sculk sensor, a portal, ice, cocoa and a chest one each.
- Each is staged as the replay is: the ground at its own heights, alone in the square over bedrock; the hostile mobs
  captured with it where they stood, with their health (a husk or a drowned as a zombie, a stray as a skeleton, a cave
  spider as a spider); the player's place, health, food, saturation, air, the items the simulator has a use for, the
  time of day and the corpus's ticks (1,200). It passes, as the replay does, alive at the end.
- `tools/corpus/baseline.json` gives the game's outcome: of the 110, 31 pass, 41 fail and 38 are intermittent. The
  intermittent ones are played and not counted.
- Agreement: 55 of 72, 76.4%. Game pass, simulator pass 26; game fail, simulator fail 29; game fail, simulator pass 12;
  game pass, simulator fail 5. The intermittent: 12 pass and 26 fail in the simulator.
- The 12 it survives and the game does not: zombies 6 and skeletons 2 (the player was fighting or backing off), a
  creeper, a phantom (none in the simulator), a suffocation (nothing falls) and a fall hit too hard (no momentum). The 5
  it dies in and the game does not: a drowning, a fall, a husk's knock, a spider and a zombie.
- Before mobs, breath and lava were staged, the agreement was 45.8% (the simulator passed 107 of 110); with mobs, 65.3%.

## Merged with main after F-35j and F-30d (2026-11-11)

Main brought F-35j's water columns and open-water test, and F-30d's recall of an atlas's sightings, into the adapter's
`WorldKnowledge`, and F-27a's replay memory and logs-kept-out line, which read the campaign's set-aside ledger and its
dug and tunnelled cells. Here those live in core: the column memory, `seenOpenWaterToward` and `recall` (capped as
`remember` is, no water columns, no listener) are in `Sight`, with `WorldKnowledge` delegating and still keeping the
dimension; the replay memory reads and restores through read-only views and restore methods on `SetAside` and `Digger`;
`builtIn` is `Sight`'s. The same rules, line for line; `WorldKnowledgeOpenWaterTest` checks the first.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

- (a) is below the bar: 76.4% of 72 against 80%. The differences are in combat (the simulator survives six zombie
  fights the game lost), in what it lacks (phantoms, falling blocks, momentum) and in a few deaths the game came through.
- (b) holds for iteration 17's ranges, not iteration 16's stuck range, and not the pooled rates.
- Not ported, and so not in the simulator: the campaign's `wayUp` beyond `climb`, its `lead`, `cave` and `lookAround`,
  the cast's legs toward lava beyond 96 blocks, the danger zones.
- The sight sweeps all at once (the game's is spread over ticks), and light is not spread.
- Docs: `companion-sim/README.md` (its gaps, and the corpus in the simulator).
- Manifest: no change (no release gate, dependency or version moved).
- Next, by what the corpus shows: the simulator's combat against the corpus's mob deaths, then the water walks above
  the feet in the portal pack.
