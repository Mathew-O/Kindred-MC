## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: a stuck spell is now a conversation of up to three rounds. When a plan stops at a step, the
next round is asked at once, and its report lists each step of the rounds before and how it went ("walk north 2: went
0, then a two-high wall of obsidian"). A plan carried out to its end gets twenty seconds of game time for the goal to
make progress. Progress ends the spell as out; three rounds without it end the spell, and the player's own ways go on.

Walking, tunnelling, digging a staircase and bridging are worked out a block at a time from the blocks round the player
as it sees them, never by the route planner. Each says how far it went and what stopped it. `look <way>` turns the
player that way, and the next report shows the blocks four that way, only as far as the player sees them from where it
stands.

## What the design said, checked against the code

| Claim | Found |
| --- | --- |
| "each verb maps to an installed skill id (`native_rescue_walk`, `native_rescue_tunnel`, ...)" | Not done this way. An executor is the campaign's own block-by-block logic. Each of its breaks, placements and moves is one of the installed recorded skills (`native_break`, `native_place`, `native_walk`, `native_traverse`), dispatched through the one runner, arbiter, ledger and claims as every task is. The gate checks each verb's skills as installed (`RescuePlan.uses`). New composite skill ids would have needed their own lease, ledger and cancellation lifecycle for a break followed by a move, beside the ones every skill shares; AGENTS.md asks for one `SkillRunner` and one arbiter |
| `walk` "steps block by block" | F-29's walk was `native_goto`, the route planner. 7 of its 11 stopped plans were that planner's "No walkable route". It is now the executor |
| `tunnel` stops "at liquid it would release or a drop of more than two" | It stops at water or lava it can see touching a cell it would break, at a block it cannot break, and at a drop of more than one, since `native_traverse` steps down one block at most. Liquid it cannot see is not guessed. The break's own guard (`RoamingEdits.breakDenial`, on every break in the game) still refuses a break that would let in lava or water the player cannot see; its words ("Breaking it would release lava") then reach the next round's report |
| `tunnel` places "a torch every eight blocks" | A tunnel is at most six blocks, so never; `torch` is a step of its own |
| `swim_up` "as F-25c4ax does" | F-25c4ax's rise is `native_goto` to the surface: the route planner. The body has no local rise skill, so `swim_up` is not in the vocabulary yet rather than break "no executor calls the route planner" |
| `water <way>` "as the clutch does" | `native_clutch` places water under a fall. A pour-and-take-back descent or crossing is not built |
| `shelter` "as F-29 and the survival rules" | F-29 had no shelter step; the goal's night shelter (dig in, seal) is many tasks, not one bounded step. Not built |
| `look` "costs a round only if it is the plan's only step" | Every plan is a round. A plan that is only a look is a round with nothing moved, and the next is asked at once |
| A spell ends in success on "progress on its goal for 20 seconds" | Taken as: progress (a new stage, a new best in a counter the route needs, or new ground, the watchdog's own measure) while a plan of the spell runs or within twenty seconds of one carried out to its end |

Fixtures:
- The scenes are staged twenty blocks up: floors, walls, a cliff, a gap, water in a basin, lava under the floor, the pit.
- For a verb's fixture, the plan is handed to the goal before its first look, as the bridge hands a checked one. The run
  is stopped the moment the plan's line comes in, so the checks see what the plan left.
- The rounds' stub answers each round with its own plan.
- The player's tools and blocks are given by the fixture. Every break, placement and move is a recorded task.

## How the fixtures were got to pass

- **Before any fix, 7 of 9 failed.** The goal's own tasks were under way when the plan was handed. A break found its cell
  already broken by the goal's own mining, and a move found the body walking.
  - The fixture now hands the plan before the goal's first look.
  - The executor works out an action refused once afresh.
- **Breaks with pickup sent the player after the drops**, past the passage's end. Breaks now leave their drops, as the
  goal's own staircases do.
- **A bridge block over air had nothing under it to place on.** It is now placed against the side of the floor the
  player stands on.
- **A torch in the player's own cell was "out of reach".** It now goes on the open floor beside its feet.
- **`native_traverse` stalled on the level in a two-high tunnel.** Level moves are `native_walk`.
- **A move right after breaks was refused once while the body settled.** It now waits a second and tries again.
- **The three-round success failed in 1 of 3 runs with a lone log on the ledge**: the goal went elsewhere for wood within
  its twenty seconds. The ledge now has a small oak; the twenty seconds are unchanged.
