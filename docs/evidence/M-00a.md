## Scope and implementation

The first match of the game-mode framework (M-00), built for the owner's combat design (E-41b needs a duel to fight in) and for the modes after it: Manhunt (M-02) and Factions (M-06) are matches between players who joined. D7 holds throughout: an AI player strikes another player only while a match both joined runs.

**Who may strike whom.**
- **A match rule.** `MatchRegistry` (core) holds the matches and answers one question: whether one player may attack another. The answer is yes only when both are joined participants of the same running match. A strike at a player is refused before the match, through its countdown and after its end. Mobs are fought as before.
- **The server's own rule first.** With vanilla PvP off on the server (`pvp=false`), no player can hurt another, so no duel is proposed or started, and the rule above answers no as well.
- **Every strike.** The body checks the rule on every strike at a player (`NativePlayerBody.mayStrike`), whatever skill asked for it. A fight checks it every tick and ends at once when it no longer holds, mid-fight included.

**Who joins.**
- **An AI player is entered by its administrator.** Proposing a duel needs administrative access to each AI player in it.
- **A human joins only for itself.** A human is invited and told how to join; it is in the match only after running `/aiplayer match join <match>` itself. No command enters a human into a fight.
- **One match at a time a player.** A player already in a match that has not ended cannot be invited to another.

**A match's life.** Proposed (the lobby), then started by an operator or a player in it once everyone has joined. Three seconds of countdown follow, then the fight. It ends:
- on a fall (the other player wins);
- on a forfeit;
- after five minutes, a draw;
- on a player leaving the game, a forfeit; an AI player's body taken out counts too;
- or when an operator stops it.

A player leaving before the start calls the match off, with no winner.

A server stop ends them all. Matches are held in memory, not saved, so a restart never brings back a leave to strike. Each change of state goes to every player invited, in chat.

**The fight.** An AI player in a running match makes the duel its first choice: the opponent as its fight's target, with the weapon that deals the most over the fight (E-39e), or a walk toward it when it is over 20 blocks away.
- **Like any fight.** It is the same `native_fight` as against mobs: one controller, the same strike timing and jumps for critical hits, the same refusal on a mob's terms. Nothing is restored afterwards. The fallen player dies as in any death, drops what it carried and respawns.
- **A command does not hold it back.** A command to an AI player holds its own choices back briefly so the requester sees the outcome. A match starting releases that hold for the players in it: the fight is what their administrator entered them for.

Changed modules/files:
- Core: `MatchRegistry` (new), `MatchRegistryTest` (new).
- Body and fight: `NativePlayerBody` (`pvp`, `mayStrike`; `attack` refuses a player target outside the rule), `NativeFightAction` (ends when the rule no longer holds).
- Runtime: `PlayerRuntime`:
  - the registry, and the rule set on each body with the server's PvP;
  - `duelCandidates`, ahead of the routine candidates; autonomy admitted for a duel in an idle-observing playstyle;
  - `proposeDuel`, `joinMatch`, `startMatch`, `stopMatch`, `playerFell` and `announce`;
  - the clock on the server tick; all matches ended on stop.
- Hooks and commands: `KindredMod` (deaths, disconnects), `PlayerCommands` (`/aiplayer match duel|join|start|stop|list`, by short id); a human's invitation carries a **[Join]** that runs the join as that human.
- Fixtures: `NativeMatchGameTests` (new).

Dependencies satisfied: E-41a (the ruleset, reach, beliefs, the encounter record); E-39e (weapon choice, timing, critical hits).
Schema/configuration changes and migration coverage: none. Nothing is saved.

**The fixture.** Two AI players, Ada and Bram, in a walled ring three blocks apart:
- Ada holds an iron sword. Bram holds a wooden sword and wears a leather chestplate. The server's PvP is on from the start, so only the match rule stands between them.
- **Before any match** Ada is told to fight Bram. It is refused with the rule's reason, and Bram is unhurt.
- **With the server's PvP off,** a duel is refused, and no match exists. With it on, the duel is proposed. Both are entered by their administrator, and it starts.
- **Through the countdown** Ada is told to fight again. It is refused, and neither is hurt. The check is kept, since a failing game-test step is only tried again.
- **Then the fight.** Both fight, and Bram falls to Ada's three critical hits, as the ruleset predicts: an iron sword's critical hit takes 8.8 through leather. Ada wins by the fall.
- **After the match,** with Bram back on its feet, Ada is told to fight once more. It is refused, and Bram is unhurt.

**Hand controls.** Each reverts one rule, and the fixture must then fail:

| Control | Expected | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The body's match rule removed (any player may be struck) | Fails: the strike before the match is not refused | Failed: "Refused before a match" |
| The server's PvP not consulted when proposing | Fails: the duel is proposed with PvP off | Failed: "Refused with the server's PvP off" |
| A command's hold not released when the match starts | Fails: Ada, held by the countdown's command, does not fight back and loses | Failed: "Ada won by Bram's fall" (Bram won) |
| A body taken out does not leave its match | Fails: the duel runs on after Bram is gone | Failed: the duel still RUNNING |

The first run of the no-gate control passed when it should have failed: the test server starts with vanilla PvP off, so the leaked strike did no harm. The fixture now turns PvP on before its first check, and the controls were run again on it.

Found on the way: the first fixture run's duel ran, but neither player was hurt. Hits landed, but the game-test server has vanilla PvP off. A server with PvP off is now its own refusal, as above, rather than a fight that cannot hurt.
