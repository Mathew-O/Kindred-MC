## Scope and implementation

**What it enchants and when** (`Enchanting`, core, pure). The pieces a run wants enchanted, each for what it is worth on that piece: a sword's Sharpness, a bow's Power, Protection on chest, legs and helmet, Feather Falling on boots, a pickaxe's Efficiency. `Enchanting.next` gives the piece to enchant next from what it holds: the best of each kind, iron or better, not already enchanted, its sword first, then its bow and armour. `ready` says it goes to its table with 30 levels and 3 lapis, as players wait for the top offer. `choose` takes, of the table's three offers, the best it can pay for: by what the hinted enchantment is worth on that piece times its level, the higher button where two are alike.

**The enchanting** (`NativeEnchantAction`, new, `native_enchant`). As a player does it (`NativePlayerBody.enchant`): the table opened with a use click, the piece clicked into its first slot and the lapis into its second, the three offers read from its screen (the levels each asks and the enchantment it hints at), the chosen button pressed, which enchants the piece for that button's lapis and levels, and the piece and the lapis left put back where they were. A recorded action, verified against the durable ledger: the piece the same in every way but what it gained, the lapis and the levels spent both the button's number and one, and nothing else held changed; else held for reconciliation. Using a table is a workstation's use under the server's edit policy.

**In play** (`PlayerRuntime.enchantCandidates`). Out of any match, between its work: ready and with a piece to enchant, to its own table (one standing in a base it built, such as the manor's enchanting room, or one it put down), and there the best offer it can pay for. With no table standing but one in hand, it puts that down beside it first, where the server's edit policy allows. It does not make a table: a table's diamonds, obsidian and book are its run's to get. It says "I enchanted my diamond sword with Looting II, Unbreaking III."
