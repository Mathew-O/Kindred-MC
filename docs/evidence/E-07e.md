## Scope and implementation

The owner asked that hoglins not be attacked unless it is really needed, believing gold keeps them calm. In vanilla 1.21.1 gold armour keeps **piglins** neutral, not hoglins. Hoglins attack any player they see. They keep away from warped fungus, nether portals and respawn anchors. And they cannot reach a player standing two blocks up: a mob's attack box (`MobEntity.getAttackBox`) grows sideways by the attack range but not upward, and a hoglin stands 1.4 blocks high. So with a goal running, a hoglin after the player is dealt with in this order:

1. **Out of reach.** With time to get there (the hoglin more than four blocks off, and not just hit), the player pillars up with its own blocks until its feet are two above the hoglin's, then waits.
2. **Waiting it out.** It stays up while a hoglin near enough to see it is about: hoglins wander close by rather than leave, and one came straight back for the player that climbed down beside it in an early run. After 90 seconds with a hoglin near but not after it, it goes on with its work.
3. **From above.** Kept up there 30 seconds with the hoglin in reach below, it strikes the hoglin from its perch with single swings, never stepping off. The hoglin cannot strike back. This is the one time a hoglin is fought by choice.
4. **Fighting back.** A hoglin already on the player, or one that reaches it while it climbs, is fought with the goal's own fight (the edge and toss rules of E-39e apply). A pillar half built is where a hoglin's toss throws a player off, and an early run died exactly that way, climbing while being hit and never fighting back.

Running work stops for a hoglin as for any attacker. A climb gives way to a fight once the hoglin is on the player.

Without a goal, the runtime's tactical strikes leave a hoglin alone unless it is after the player within three blocks.

Pillar steps planned mid-jump (between two steps of a climb) are now planned for the cell the player is about to land in, and the step waits up to twelve ticks for the landing. Before this, each step cost a one-second pause.

**Gold for piglins.** One piece of gold armour worn keeps piglins from turning on a player.
- **A need with its sources.** `GOLD_ARMOR` (one piece) and `GOLD` (ingots) are needs in the guide, with their recipe and sources, and the strategist can queue them. The piece made is a **golden helmet**: five ingots, and as good as an iron helmet (two armour points each). Boots would cost four ingots, but they guard less than iron boots, so wearing them would cost protection.
- **Wanted for the Nether.** Before the Nether trip (it is among the tasks ahead), the piece is wanted for staying alive. Like any need, it is taken from what is at hand: nether gold ore within twelve blocks and a pickaxe of any kind, or nuggets enough for an ingot. In the Nether with no gold on, the gold is wanted at once, so known ore up to 48 blocks away is gone for.
- **Made by hand.** Nether gold ore gives two to six nuggets a block. Nine nuggets make an ingot at its own crafting table, and five ingots a helmet. Nothing is given.
- **Worn in the Nether, iron again outside it.** In the Nether with no gold on, a golden piece it carries is put on in its slot, swapped with the piece worn there by vanilla's own inventory swap, so the iron helmet is carried, not lost. Out of the Nether, a carried piece that guards better (or as well, with more wear left) goes back on, and the gold is carried for the next trip.
- **No gold mined with a piglin about.** Vanilla angers every piglin within sixteen blocks of a player who breaks a block piglins guard (vanilla's `guarded_by_piglins` tag: gold ores of all kinds, gold blocks, gilded blackstone, chests and the like), seen or not, and gold worn does not calm a piglin angered that way. So no such block is broken with a piglin within sixteen blocks (the break rules, for every way of breaking), and gold ore with a piglin near it is set aside rather than walked to again and again.
- **A calm piglin is no threat.** A piglin that is not after the player is no longer offered to the tactical strikes (with no goal, the runtime's strikes could hit one standing near). One struck turns every piglin about on the player. The goal's own fights already took only mobs after the player. Piglin brutes, which are always hostile, are unchanged.

Found on the way:
- **The test server simulates almost nothing.** The game-test server runs with no simulation distance to speak of: a world ticks entities only in the chunk the player is in, and the framework force-loads only the Overworld's test areas. In a Nether room far from them, a piglin one chunk from the player was frozen after a few ticks, and the first gold fixture passed with it frozen. The Nether fixtures now force-load their room as the framework does for its areas, and count the window in the piglin's own ticks. A real server simulates five chunks or more around every player, so this does not affect play.
- **Fixture hygiene.** A fixture that failed before taking its piglin away left it beside the next fixture's room, and the next player rightly kept off the ore. Each room now clears leftover piglins first.
