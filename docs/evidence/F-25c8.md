## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: none yet in play. `Playbook` (core, pure) lists the ways of doing each part of the run that has more than one:
- into the Nether: a frame cast in place, obsidian mined, a ruined portal completed;
- pearls: endermen hunted, gold bartered with piglins;
- the way to the stronghold: across the Overworld, or an eighth of it through the Nether;
- a caged crystal: shot through the bars, or a pillar;
- the dragon: perch strikes, or beds set off under its head;
- the respawn point: a bed by the End portal.

It chooses the cheapest the player can do now. In normal play a speedrunner's way is weighed only when the chance is at hand, never sought out; at speedrun pace the quickest possible way is taken. A way that fails twice is set aside for the next. The pace comes from the player's playstyle: `/aiplayer mode speedrun [player]` sets speedrun, and `optimal` or `idle` set normal play. It reaches the planner's preferences (`PlanPreferences.mode`) and the strategist's report ("Pace: ..."). The optional strategist may advise one way from the same fixed set (`approach` in both providers' reply schemas, validated in core: an unknown way or `NONE` counts as none). The advice is taken only where the way can be done and costs at most half as much again as the best. The Minecraft guide sent to the strategist lists the ways.

A pearl onto a caged crystal's roof was a way here for a while and is gone: see [E-39f](E-39f.md).
