## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): "When the players reach enchanting stage they should opt to name their weapons and tools cool names."

**The enchanting stage.** An enchanting table of its own: one it holds, or one standing in a base it built (the manor's enchanting room, M-01b). Enchanting itself is E-16a's, still open; so in play the stage comes with the manor, or with a table it made.

**What it names** (`KitNames`, core, pure). Of each kind it holds (sword, axe, bow, crossbow, trident, mace, pickaxe, shovel), the best piece, by material and then by its enchantments, when that is worth a name (diamond or netherite, or enchanted) and has no name yet; weapons first; as far as its levels go, a name costing what vanilla's anvil asks for a rename alone, one level and the work the piece has had. A best piece already named, by it or by anyone, stays as it is, and so does every lesser one of its kind. A plain iron sword is not named.

**The name.** The model's, when one is set up: asked once for the piece, within the strategist lane's rates and daily budgets, with the player's temper (from its personality's strongest traits), what the piece is ("Name your diamond sword, with Fire Aspect II and Sharpness III") and what it has named already. The model's name is used only if it passes the naming rules (`NameForge`): 2 to 32 characters of letters, spaces, apostrophes and hyphens, a capital first, and not one it has used. Otherwise, or with no model, its own: drawn from what the piece is, its most telling enchantment first (Fire Aspect: Ember, Cinder, Blaze, Pyre; Smite: Grave, Wraith; Fortune or Looting: Gold, Fortune; Silk Touch: Velvet, Whisper; Efficiency: Swift, Wind), then its material (netherite: Ash, Dread; diamond: Gleam, Frost, Shard), with an ending for its kind (a sword's fang, bane, edge or brand; a pickaxe's delver, breaker or heart), in the manner of its temper: one word ("Scorchbane", "Windbore", "Gleamheart") for most; for a curious player a title ("Fang of the Embers", "Delver of the Deep"); for a playful one, about half the time, a joke ("Sir Pokes-a-Lot", "Pebble Popper"). The same player and piece always give the same name; two players name alike only by chance. Its word lists leave out parts that make well-known names from games and films (Shadowfang, Stormbreaker), and the model is told to give none.

**The anvil.** Its own: one it holds, put down beside it; or, with none, one made of iron it can spare beyond what its run still wants (three blocks of iron and four ingots, 31 in all), at a crafting table within reach, or one it walks to within twelve blocks. It never fetches iron for a name, and never names anything in a match. A name is done at the anvil when it is by it, up to 48 blocks off.

**The rename** (`NativePlayerBody.rename`, `NativeRenameAction`, new, `native_rename`). As a player does it: the anvil opened with a use click, the piece clicked into its first slot, the name typed, the named piece taken from the result and put back in its own slot, for the levels the anvil asks; shut with nothing taken, the anvil gives the piece back. A recorded action: verified that the piece is the same in every way but its name, nothing else held changed, and exactly the anvil's cost in levels was spent; else held for reconciliation. Using an anvil is a workstation's use under the server's edit policy.

**What it says.** "I named my diamond sword Scorchbane.", to whoever controls it and, if it talks in public, those within earshot; and it remembers it as a fact ("Named its diamond sword Scorchbane at an anvil").

Changed modules/files: `KitNames`, `KitNamesTest` (new, `companion-core`); `NativePlayerBody` (`rename`, the `RENAME` intent), `NativeRenameAction` (new), `NativePlayerSkills` (`native_rename`), `PlayerRuntime` (the stage, the pieces, the name chosen, the anvil, `renameEnded`), `NativeKitNameGameTests` (new).
