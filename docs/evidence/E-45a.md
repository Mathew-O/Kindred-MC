## Scope and implementation

The owner's direction (2026-10-25): "villager trading, not only should it understand what's valuable and how to profit from villagers, it should build villager stations near its own area for ones with good trades and some how get them there". This slice is the first part: what a trade is worth.

**Emeralds are the currency** (`Trades`, core, pure). An emerald costs the player what the cheapest income trade it can still make costs to gather: twenty wheat from its field, thirty-two sticks from its trees, twenty-four sheets of paper from its sugar cane. With no such trade known, an emerald costs what mining one does (`Worth`'s minutes). Selling a diamond for an emerald is never cheaper than mining one.

**What a purchase is worth.** What it gets, in minutes of play, as it stands, against what it pays: its emeralds at their price and any goods at their gathering. A book is worth its enchantments, each at its level's share of its highest level squared (Mending, Unbreaking III, Efficiency V, Fortune III and Protection IV high; a curse less than nothing). Anything else is worth what the value model says it is worth to the player (E-42): diamond kit better than its own an upgrade, against its own enchanted diamond only a spare; ender pearls for a run short of them. What it can grow or make of what it grows (bread from its wheat) is worth no more than that costs it. In words it can say: "A Mending book for 10 emeralds: worth it."; "A Protection I book for 30 emeralds: not worth it to me."

**Income.** A villager buying what the player can spare (beyond what its run wants) costs the player only the gathering; what its run wants is not for sale. Income from what grows back lasts: emeralds a day from a villager's trades fed by crops, sugar cane, sticks, rotten flesh, string or wool, each made as often as a restock allows, twice a day ("I can spare 20 wheat for an emerald, and they grow back.").

**A plan at a villager.** Its purchases worth making, best for each emerald first; and before them the income trades that pay for what its emeralds do not, with goods it can spare, cheapest emerald first, and only while an emerald costs it less than what it buys is worth for one.

**Whom to keep** (for E-45c). A villager is worth the profit of each purchase worth making once, and a share of its renewable income: a librarian with Mending far more than one with Bane of Arthropods; a nitwit nothing.

**What each profession buys and sells.** Vanilla's table, by level, as a player learns it from the wiki: so that it knows what to look for and whom (an expert cleric for ender pearls, a librarian for books from novice up), and each profession's job block (a lectern for a librarian, a brewing stand for a cleric). What a particular villager offers it knows only from its trading screen, opened as a player opens it (E-45b); the prices there are the villager's own, demand and the player's standing included.
