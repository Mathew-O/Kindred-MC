## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: `/aiplayer build hut <x y z> [quarter turns] [player]` has a native player build a generated hut from the blocks it holds (see the [operator guide](../operator-guide.md)). It does so a step at a time, as a player does, with the runtime's own recorded skills.

- **`BuildRunner`** (new, adapter). Each pass it looks at the world round the build (the blueprint's box with six blocks round it) and turns it into a `BuildSite`. It asks the construction planner (E-08a) for the next step and gives the runtime one task:
  - walk to the spot to stand, stopping near its middle;
  - ready the block in the hotbar;
  - place it with the planned look;
  - break what is in the way;
  - pillar a scaffold, then dig down its own scaffold to get off it.
  
  Progress is read from the world, so nothing is remembered but the project and the scaffold it put up. A block placed in the wrong state is broken and placed again, then left after two tries. A spot where the server refused a step is avoided until the build gets on again. After eight failed steps in a row the build stops and says why. Scaffolding comes only from blocks the build needs no more of.
- **Placement with the planned look.** `NativePlayerBody.placeBlock` and `NativePlaceAction` take the planner's look (yaw and pitch) and the exact point on the clicked face. The look is worked out again from where the eyes really are, keeping the planned facing. The action still runs under the same edit policy, claim, ledger and conservation check. A placed block is verified by its own item, so a torch placed as a wall torch passes, and the completed record carries the exact state placed.
- **Arriving at the middle of a spot.** `native_goto` takes `centre`, so a build's walk ends near the middle of its spot, as a player lines up to build.
- **In the runtime.** A build is a project candidate, ahead of the standing goal; survival needs still outrank it. It has its own finish hook. Builds start through `PlayerRuntime.startBuild` and the `build` command.

Found on the way, by running the fixture again and again:
- **A navigator bug that affected every walk.** A body standing over the edge of a block searches from the block below its feet. When that block was the goal, the route of one step was rejected as "no walkable route". The goto then failed every time.
- **The runner planned from the wrong cell.** It took the block position under a body over an edge as where it stands; it now finds the cell it really stands on.
- **Scaffold was recorded too early.** It was recorded before the pillar step landed, so the runner once tried to dig out air. It is now recorded when the step completes, and forgotten if the world no longer holds it.
- **The break check and the planner disagree near corners.** The server measures reach to the block's nearest point and casts an exact ray. The planner works from the middle of the spot and the middle of the block. Near corners the two can disagree, so refused spots are now avoided.

Changed modules/files: `BuildRunner` (new), `NativePlayerBody` (`PlaceLook`, `placeBlock` with a look), `NativePlaceAction` (the look, the verification), `NativeGoToSkill` (`centred`, the one-step route), `NativePlayerSkills` (`native_place` `yaw`/`pitch`/`hit`, `native_goto` `centre`), `PlayerRuntime` (the build candidate, the finish hook, `startBuild`, `buildStatus`), `PlayerCommands` (`build`); `NativeBuildGameTests` (new), the gametest entrypoints.

**Scaffolding down, nearest first (2026-10-23, found validating M-00a).** In one full run the hut was built, and the player then stood beside one scaffold column waiting for ever on another across the hut. The planner took scaffolding down by height alone. Of equal height it took whichever the scaffold set listed first, an order of absolute positions, so of where the test happened to run. It gave up when that one was out of reach. Now each column comes down from its top, the highest first and of equal height the nearest, and one out of reach waits while another comes down. When none can be reached from where it stands, the planner names the column, and the runner walks over to it with the navigator to plan again from beside it. Pure tests: `ofScaffoldTopsAsHighTheNearestComesDownFirst` (both orders of the set) and `aColumnOutOfReachFromHereIsLeftForTheRestAndNamed`.

## Remaining limitations and handoff

Not tested / known failures:
- A restart loses the project.
- Builds near water, lava or mobs, on uneven ground, or larger than the hut.
- A human changing the build while it works (the planner would rebuild what was taken and break what is in the way).
- It scaffolds more than a player would: the roof's upper courses cost about 30 scaffold steps for 25 blocks.
- One to three dirt are not picked up after digging down.

Exact next action, if incomplete: keep the project across a restart, as the blueprint's source, origin and turns in the player's memory, then resume it from the world.
