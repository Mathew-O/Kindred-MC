## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: where a way climbs and there is no way up but its own blocks, a native player builds a staircase as it goes, step after step in one movement, rather than a pillar straight up and a bridge across. It jumps on the move, and once its feet have risen past the step it sets a block in the cell ahead, under where it will land. It lands on it and jumps again, never stopping against a block's side.

- **Where a step's block goes.** Vanilla places a block only against a face of a solid block, and each step of a staircase is diagonal to the last, so the step before is no face to set it against. Each block goes against something solid under, beside or ahead of it: the ground at the foot of a climb, or a wall, cliff or ravine side alongside, as players climb them. That is one block a step.
- **In the open.** With nothing beside a step, a filler block goes in first, from the step below, against the block the player stands on, and the step's block on top of it. That is two blocks a step. A step that fails in the air then drops the player no more than a block, onto the filler.
- **One flight, every block recorded.** A flight of up to twelve steps is one recorded skill, `native_stair`. Each block in it is its own durable record under the edit policy and its own claim, as for any placement, and exactly one block item is spent on it. The next step's records are prepared while the player is in the air, so it lands ready to go on. The flight ends with the step up onto ground already there at the top, taken in the same movement. A flight that cannot go on ends with the steps it climbed, and the way is planned again from there.
- **In the route planner.** A step of a staircase gains a block's height and a block's way for one block (6.5), against a pillar's step (7), which gains only the height, and a bridge's (6). A filler costs a block more (5), and a step over lava costs what a bridge over lava does. A route climbing ahead and up takes stairs where it used to pillar and then bridge. A route straight up still pillars.

Found on the way:
- **A jump against the step's side loses its way.** Placed first and jumped onto, a step's block stops the player against its side until its feet clear it, and it then lands short with little speed. The block is set in the air, once the feet are past the step, as players do.
- **The fixture's expectations, not the climb.** The route walks to the start pad's edge before the flight. And the top step needs no block where the ledge is ahead of it to set against. The fixtures now read what was built.
- **A failed flight set aside the wrong cell.** A made-way change that fails has its cell set aside for a minute, so the next plan goes another way. A flight's coordinates are where the player stands, so its first step's cell is the one set aside.
- **The step onto the ledge.** At first the flight ended a step short of the top, and the walk onto the ledge followed as another task a quarter of a second later. The step up onto ground already there is now the flight's last step.
