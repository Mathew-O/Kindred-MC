## Scope and implementation

User-visible behavior: whoever may command a native player (whoever made it, and operators) now talks with it in private, as a legacy companion's owner always has:
- they can speak to it with public chat off, and are answered;
- they hear the remarks it makes unasked;
- it greets them as its owner.

Before this, a native player's own record was its only "owner" (`Companion.create(id, id, ...)`), so with public chat off nobody could talk to it, not even its creator, and its remarks reached no one. Anyone who may not command it still needs public chat, as before.

Changed modules/files:
- `CompanionSocials`: one notion of who owns a player in the lane, `owns(participant)`: its saved owner, or anyone the runtime's `setControllers` predicate admits. Conversation (`canTalk`), who hears its remarks, and how it classes a person it sees all use it.
- `SocialPerception`: `listeners`, `noticeable` and `observe` take that predicate; the old UUID versions delegate to it.
- `PlayerRuntime`: wiring only. A native player's controllers are whoever its `nativeState.access().mayControl` admits; a legacy companion keeps its owner alone.
- `NativeVoiceGameTests`: two fixtures.

Requirements covered: SOC-02 (public speech stays opt-in; private speech reaches only those who may command the player), AUTH-01 (the lane uses the same `mayControl` test every command makes; talking grants nothing).

Where the handoff page differs: it asks for the fix in `canTalk` ("two lines"). That covers conversation only. A native player's remarks and its greeting decide who is its owner in two more places (`SocialPerception.listeners`, and the contact kind in `observe` and `noticeable`). With `canTalk` alone, the second fixture still failed: the remark went nowhere. All three now use the one predicate.
