## Scope and implementation

**What it does next** (`Netherite`, core, pure). Netherite as players get it: ancient debris smelted into scrap, four scrap and four gold ingots made into an ingot, and a diamond piece upgraded at a smithing table with an ingot and an upgrade template, which is spent. A template is copied from another with seven diamonds and a block of netherrack. The pieces in the order a run upgrades them: its sword and pickaxe first, then its armour, chest to boots, then its axe. `Netherite.next` gives the next step from what it holds: a template copied first, keeping the one it copies from, when it has more ingots than templates, more pieces than templates and seven diamonds to spare with a block of netherrack; else an upgrade of its first piece when it has an ingot and a template; else an ingot from four scrap and four gold; else its debris smelted, up to an ingot's worth of scrap.

**The upgrade** (`NativeSmithAction`, new, `native_smith`). As a player does it: the smithing table opened with a use click, the template, the piece and the ingot clicked into its three slots, and the upgraded piece taken from the result into the piece's own slot (`NativePlayerBody.smith`). A recorded action, verified against the durable ledger: the piece is its netherite self with its enchantments and name kept, one template and one ingot spent, and nothing else held changed; else held for reconciliation. Using a smithing table is a workstation's use under the server's edit policy.

**In play** (`PlayerRuntime.netheriteCandidates`). Out of any match: debris it holds smelted into scrap in its furnace (one it holds put down first), and what the furnace made taken out, the scrap only; the ingot made at a crafting table; a template copied when the core says so; and the piece upgraded at a smithing table of its own, one it holds put down beside it first. It says "I upgraded my diamond sword to netherite."
