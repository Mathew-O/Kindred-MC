# Minecraft survival guide (Java Edition 1.21.1)

Reference for advising a survival player. Counts are vanilla's own.

## The way to the Ender Dragon
1. Wood: logs, then planks, sticks and a crafting table; a wooden pickaxe.
2. Stone: cobblestone for stone tools and a furnace.
3. Iron: raw iron smelted with coal or charcoal. In order: an iron pickaxe, a bucket, a sword, a shield, armour.
4. Diamonds, deep down (most common far below Y 0): a diamond pickaxe mines obsidian. Obsidian can also be cast: lava poured into a frame and hardened with water.
5. A Nether portal: a 4 by 5 obsidian frame (corners optional), lit with flint and steel (flint from gravel, and an iron ingot).
6. The Nether: a fortress (dark nether bricks) for blaze rods from blazes; ender pearls from endermen (Nether warped forests, or the Overworld at night).
7. Eyes of ender: one ender pearl and one blaze powder each (a rod makes two powder). Twelve fill any portal frame.
8. The stronghold: throw an eye, note where it flies, walk well to the side and throw again; it lies where the two flights cross. Dig down there to its stone bricks and find the portal room.
9. The End: fill the frame, jump in, destroy the End crystals, then kill the dragon.

## What each larger task needs before it begins
- The Nether trip: 12 or more food (the Nether has little to eat), 48 or more blocks for bridging lava and walling off, a spare pickaxe, a stone sword or better (iron is better), a shield against ghast fireballs.
- The stronghold: 6 or more eyes (each throw has a one in five chance to break), food, blocks; a boat when the way crosses open water.
- The End fight:
  - a bow and 32 or more arrows: the ten End crystals on top of the obsidian pillars heal the dragon, and an arrow from the ground breaks one;
  - 64 blocks: two crystals are caged in iron bars; one is shot through the gaps in its cage from far off, or reached by pillaring up beside it and breaking the bars (never by a pearl onto the cage's roof: a pearl that falls through the bars onto the crystal blows it up beside the player);
  - a water bucket: to break a fall from a pillar; endermen will not follow into water;
  - 16 or more food, an iron sword or better, armour;
  - 1 bed: set down by the End portal in the stronghold and used, so a death in the End starts again close by (a bed explodes if used in the End or the Nether).
  - Never look at an enderman's face (a carved pumpkin on the head prevents it). Beds explode in the End and in the Nether. The void below the island kills.
  - Do not stand next to a crystal when it breaks: it explodes. When the dragon perches on the exit portal in the middle, hit its head with a sword from in front of it (its wings fling a player off and touching its head hurts), with full swings and falling critical hits; keep out of its purple breath. Arrows do a perched dragon no harm at all; in flight an arrow hurts its head fully and any other part only a quarter. A sword's critical hits deal more over a fight than an axe's.

## Recipes
- Planks: 1 log makes 4. Sticks: 2 planks make 4. Crafting table: 4 planks.
- Bow: 3 string and 3 sticks, at a crafting table.
- Arrows: 1 flint, 1 stick and 1 feather make 4, at a crafting table.
- Shield: 6 planks and 1 iron ingot. Bucket: 3 iron ingots. Boat: 5 planks of one wood.
- Flint and steel: 1 flint and 1 iron ingot. Eye of ender: 1 ender pearl and 1 blaze powder.
- Bed: 3 wool of one colour and 3 planks, at a crafting table.

## Where things come from
- String: spiders drop none to two (spiders are calm in daylight unless hit); cobwebs broken with a sword drop one (mineshafts, some stronghold rooms).
- Feathers: chickens drop none to two.
- Wool: sheep, one from each kill, one to three shorn with shears.
- Flint: one in ten gravel blocks dug; gravel can be set down and dug again until it drops.
- Arrows: skeletons drop none to two; rarely a bow.
- Ender pearls: endermen drop none or one. Blaze rods: blazes drop none or one, only near fortresses.
- Food: cows, pigs, sheep, chickens and rabbits; cooked meat restores more. Rotten flesh and spider eyes poison.

## Mobs: how far they notice a player that they can see
A sneaking player is noticed at four fifths of these.
- 16 blocks: skeleton, stray, wither skeleton, spider, creeper, witch, slime, magma cube, hoglin, piglin brute, guardian, shulker.
- 35: zombie, husk, drowned, zombie villager. 32: pillager. 48: blaze. 100: ghast (it shoots across the Nether).
- Only when provoked: endermen (looked at, 64 blocks), zombified piglins (hit), piglins (by a player wearing no gold armour), wolves, bees, iron golems.
- Faster than a walking player: spiders, wither skeletons, hoglins, piglins. Walking away does not shake them off.

## Ways of doing a part of the run
The player knows more than one way for these parts and picks the cheapest it can do now. In normal play a speedrunner's way (marked *) is taken only when the chance is at hand; in speedrun mode the quickest is taken. A way that fails twice is set aside for the next.
- Into the Nether: CAST_FRAME (lava poured into a frame and hardened with water), MINE_OBSIDIAN (a diamond pickaxe), RUINED_PORTAL* (a ruined portal found and completed).
- Pearls: HUNT_ENDERMEN, BARTER* (gold ingots thrown to piglins while wearing gold armour; they trade pearls, obsidian and more at random).
- To the stronghold: OVERWORLD_WALK, NETHER_SHORTCUT* (an eighth of the way in the Nether, out by a portal of its own).
- A caged crystal: SHOOT_THROUGH (from far off, through the gaps), PILLAR (blocks of its own beside the cage). Never a pearl onto the cage's roof: its bars catch a pearl only about half the time, and a pearl that falls through onto the crystal blows it up beside the player.
- The dragon: PERCH_STRIKES (its head struck while it perches, arrows while it flies), BED_BOMBS* (beds set off under its head while it perches; the blast hurts the player too).
- A respawn point: BED_BY_PORTAL (a bed set down and used by the End portal).

## Dangers
- Lava: the Nether's lava sea is at Y 31; fortresses stand over it. Never dig straight down or straight up.
- Falls: more than three blocks hurt; water at the bottom breaks any fall.
- Water: drowning after fifteen seconds under water; drowned throw tridents. A boat outruns them.
- Nights: hostile mobs spawn in darkness. Dig in or wall off for the night; phantoms come after three nights without sleep.
- Caves: mobs spawn in their dark; go in with a shield, food and full health, or dig one's own way.

## The player's queue of follow-ups
While the player works on what is in front of it, its queue holds what later tasks will need. Each entry is a need from a fixed list (BOW, ARROWS, SHIELD, WATER_BUCKET, BED, STRING, FEATHERS, WOOL, FLINT, STICKS, BLOCKS, FOOD, GOLD_ARMOR, GOLD, IRON_SWORD), a count, and the task it is for (NETHER_PORTAL, NETHER_TRIP, STRONGHOLD, END_FIGHT, SURVIVAL). The player breaks each into gathering and crafting by itself (a bow becomes string and sticks, then the bow), takes the chance when it comes along the way (a chicken in sight while feathers are wanted), and gathers what is still missing before the task begins. Each task's own needs are queued already (the Nether trip's food and blocks, the End fight's bow, arrows, blocks, water bucket, food and bed); the player gathers what a task still lacks before the task begins, and takes the chance for any of them on the way, whatever it is doing. While the Nether trip is ahead it also wants a piece of gold armour (GOLD_ARMOR, a helmet from five GOLD ingots; nether gold ore gives nuggets, nine to an ingot) so piglins leave it alone, taken as the chance comes rather than gathered before going through, and worn in the Nether. Gold does not calm hoglins; the player gets two blocks up out of a hoglin's reach instead.
